<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual, on the other enemy">You will be asked, afterwards, why you drew on a man. Have the answer ready before you draw.</FieldQuote>

A Skirmish is the one fight where the enemy has reasons. Every Foe still acts from a closed rule the table can check, and your judgment enters at three named places: **whether the Squad can come in unseen, why a Foe went for that soldier, and when a group has had enough.**

<SectionHead id="before-the-first-card" eyebrow="Section 1">Before the First Card</SectionHead>

A Skirmish starts where a rule names one — the Night table's Prowlers row, a Mission Brief — or where your framing sets the Squad against people.

<FinePrint title="what the start names, and the Ambush">

<Subsection title="What the start names" id="what-the-start-names">

One Foe kind from the records below, and, where it says so, how many, which side has the Ambush, that it is night, and who takes part. **One Foe group per Skirmish.** A Skirmish beginning at a Night Camp is at night — which is what puts firebrands in Bandits' hands.

Then set out: Foes labelled 1 upward at full Health, firearms loaded; every soldier Apart; the Ambush settled; round 1.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The Ambush is your one call here" id="the-ambush">

**If the start gives it to the Foes,** it is theirs and nobody rolls.

**Otherwise you rule whether the Squad can approach unseen at all** — on the ground and the fiction, out loud, before any roll. A picket in open country at noon cannot be walked up on; a camp in a wood at night can. If it can, one soldier rolls **Sneak** against the group's **Watch**: no Help, no Cover, Push allowed, never retried. Rule it once and let the dice do the rest.

</Subsection>

<RuleBox title="What the Ambush is worth" icon="ph:eye-closed">

A soldier or Foe **has not yet acted** until its first card of the Skirmish has come up.

- In round 1, every card of the ambushing side comes up first, keeping their order within each side.
- An attack on a target that has not yet acted gains **2 dice** — Bonus Dice for a soldier, Attack Dice for a Foe.
- **An ambushing side's attack on a target that has not yet acted cannot be answered:** no Reaction, no Guard, every success net.

That last line is where the harm comes from. **Three Bandits with the Ambush can put a Rookie on the ground before the Squad has thrown a die.**

</RuleBox>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="the-foes" eyebrow="Section 2">The Foes</SectionHead>

Three kinds, and these three are the whole list. **You add none** — a new kind waits for rules that are not written yet.

<RuleBox title="Bars, and what lifts them" icon="ph:eye-slash">

Every figure sits under a bar, because a Skirmish is where the Squad earns them. The group's size is not one, since the start already says how many.

A successful **Size Up** buys the group's Grit and how many more must be out before it breaks, in full, aloud. Everything else the players learn by fighting: Attack Dice and Guard they count as you roll them in the open, Health from how much a body takes.

</RuleBox>

<SectionHead id="the-foe-rule" eyebrow="Section 3">The Foe Rule</SectionHead>

On the group's card, every Foe still in the Skirmish takes one turn in label order. Take the first step whose test it meets.

- **Candidates** are every soldier taking part who is alive and not Down. **A Foe never attacks a Down soldier,** by rule or by your direction.
- **The last attacker** is whoever most recently rolled Fight or Shoot against this Foe, landed or not.

<StepFlow id="fig-foe-rule" fig="Fig. 1" title="Whose turn it is, and who it comes for" steps={foeRuleSteps()} />

<CommanderNote title="The rule is legible on purpose">

A Squad can read this rule off the table and steer a Foe onto whoever last struck it. **Let them.** A player who works out that striking the trooper pulls him off the medic has learned the fight, and the fight is better for it. Run it as written, and direct only where the fiction gives you a reason.

</CommanderNote>

<FinePrint title="Guard, firearms, and what a Foe never does">

<Subsection title="Guard, and their firearms" id="guard-and-firearms">

**Guard** is a Foe's cancelling roll, and its only roll besides its attack. Roll it in the open as base dice whenever a soldier's Fight or Shoot against it is final — every attack, however many come in a round, and a Held Foe still rolls. It takes none of these: {guardNever()}, and it spends nothing.

**Firearms** start loaded and are empty after one Shoot. A Foe's Reload spends its turn and no shot, and **a Foe never runs out**.

**Never.** No Foe takes {foeNever()}. A Foe has no Stress, no Fear Roll, and no Stress Response. At 0 Health it is out — dead from Cut, Pierce, or Burn, out cold from Crush — and never a target again.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="directing-a-foe" eyebrow="Section 4">Directing a Foe</SectionHead>

When the fiction gives a Foe a reason, you may direct its target or action in place of the rule's pick. **Say the reason aloud before it acts:** the sergeant goes for the medic who shot his man.

<FinePrint title="what a directed Foe still does, and a Size Up">

<RuleBox title="What a directed Foe still does" icon="ph:user-focus">

- **Only a turn the rule could give** — Fight, Shoot, reload, close in and Fight, or nothing, with its own weapons.
- **Never against a Down soldier.** The one direction that would read as an execution cannot be given.
- **A Held Foe still only Fights its holder,** bare-handed.
- **It never leaves alone.** A Foe leaves at 0 Health, or when its group breaks. Want one to run? Break its group sooner, with a reason.
- **Its numbers stand,** rolled in the open and never adjusted.

</RuleBox>

<Subsection title="After a Size Up" id="after-a-size-up">

A successful Size Up buys the truth, **and it holds you to it.** State the Grit, how many more must go, and what each Foe will do on the next card — including what a Foe you already mean to direct will do instead, and why.

Each Foe then does what you stated. Only something that happens **after** the Size Up changes it: a change in what the rule tests, a new reason said aloud, or an action a directed Foe can no longer take. **Never hold a reason back at a Size Up and produce it on that card.**

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="grit-and-breaking" eyebrow="Section 5">Grit and Breaking</SectionHead>

**Grit is the latest a group breaks, not the moment it must.** When its Foes out of the Skirmish reach its Grit, it breaks: every Foe still in it that is not Held becomes Apart at once, takes no further turn, and leaves at the end of the round.

**You may break it sooner, or have it surrender,** with a reason said aloud as it happens — the officer falls, the sergeant sees the flare, the third man hears what the first two sounded like. **Never later.**

**A failed threat hardens them,** raising the group's Grit and Parley value by 1 each for the rest of the fight.

<MarginNote>The three tests that end a Skirmish are the players' to read, on <a href="/guide/skirmishes/#ending-a-skirmish">Skirmishes</a>. You never end one yourself, and breaking a group is not ending the fight.</MarginNote>

<SectionHead id="parley" eyebrow="Section 6">Parley at the Table</SectionHead>

A group's **Parley value** is parted from its Grit on purpose: **the group hardest to put down is not the hardest to reason with.** A patrol under orders fights to the last and can still be argued round, for one success more than Bandits ask.

<GmTable table="parley-needs" />

- **Each Foe out takes 1 off the need,** never below 1, before the ask's own number. Talking works better once the shooting has gone badly for them.
- **A success ends the Skirmish.** Answer truthfully, from what its Foes actually know — **a group that answers with a lie you invented is a group the players will never talk to again.**
- **A failure costs nothing,** unless it was a threat.
- **With no Skirmish under way,** each soldier may make one ask: Parley value plus the ask's number, no Help, no Cover, never retried.

<FinePrint title="what to expect, and practical advice">

<Subsection title="What to expect" id="what-to-expect">

- **A Squad beats a Military Police patrol nearly every time,** and deaths are rare. The danger is the Critical Injury that rides home with them.
- **One soldier against one trooper is close to even.** Nothing about a lone Survey Corps soldier makes a trooper a walkover.
- **A night Ambush by Bandits costs blood and almost never a life** — the cheapest hard scene in these rules, which is why the Night table reaches for it.
- **A Skirmish sits a notch below a Titan fight,** and should run faster too: no Positions, no Attention, no clocks, one card for the whole group.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Practical advice" id="practical-advice">

- **Roll their dice in the open, all of them.** A Foe people can count is a Foe they can plan around, and that is the whole shape of this fight.
- **Say who each Foe is going for, and why, before you roll.** One short sentence: "he is still on Mila, she cut him last turn."
- **Let the Blocks happen.** People can be blocked and Titans cannot. Soldiers who have only ever fought Titans forget this; remind them once.
- **Watch the firearms.** A loaded musket is the one thing on the table that drops a healthy soldier in a single roll — and a sentry the Squad closes with has only his hands.
- **Give them the way out.** Every group but a patrol under orders breaks at the first body. Say what breaking looks like.
- **Bare hands never kill.** A Squad that wants a person alive has the Grapple and Release. Make sure they know it before the first blade comes out.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<RuleBox variant="stop" title="Never yours to move" icon="ph:prohibit">

- **A Foe's Attack Dice and Guard,** rolled in the open and never adjusted.
- **Damage, Net Successes, and what 0 Health means.**
- **The ban on attacking a Down soldier.**
- **That a Foe never Pushes, Helps, or Covers,** and that a Held Foe only Fights its holder.
- **A failed threat's rise,** and Grit as the latest a group breaks.
- **The three tests that end a Skirmish.**

Everything else you rule, you rule before the dice and say out loud.

</RuleBox>
