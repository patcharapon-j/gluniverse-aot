<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual, on the other enemy">You will be asked, afterwards, why you drew on a man. Have the answer ready before you draw.</FieldQuote>

A Skirmish is the one fight where the enemy has reasons. Every Foe still acts from a closed rule the whole table can check, and your judgment enters it at three named places: whether the Squad can come in unseen, why a Foe went for that soldier, and when a group has had enough.

<SectionHead id="before-the-first-card" eyebrow="Section 1">Before the First Card</SectionHead>

A Skirmish starts where a rule names one, such as the Night table's Prowlers row or a Mission Brief, or where your framing sets the Squad against people, exactly as framing starts a Titan fight. The stakes of a failed called roll can begin one too.

<Subsection title="What the start names" id="what-the-start-names">

One Foe kind from the records below, and, if it says so, how many, which side has the Ambush, that it is night, and which soldiers take part. One Foe group takes part in a Skirmish. Its number is the start's, or else the kind's own group size. A Skirmish that begins at a Night Camp is at night, which is what puts firebrands in Bandits' hands.

Who takes part: the soldiers the start names, or else every living soldier in the Squad on the same Expedition, or every living soldier in the Squad when no Expedition is under way.

Then set out: the Foes labelled 1 upward, each at full Health, with their weapons and their firearms loaded; every soldier Apart from every Foe unless the start says otherwise; the Ambush settled; and round 1 begins.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The Ambush is your one call here" id="the-ambush">

**If the start gives the Ambush to the Foes,** as the Prowlers row does, it is theirs and no roll is made.

**Otherwise you rule whether the Squad can approach unseen at all.** If it cannot, no side has the Ambush and nobody rolls. If it can, one soldier taking part who is not Down, chosen by the players, rolls Sneak against the group's Watch. It cannot be Helped or Covered, it can be Pushed, and it is never tried again. On a success the Squad has the Ambush; on a failure no side has it.

Rule it on the ground and the fiction, out loud, before the roll: a picket in open country at noon cannot be walked up on; a camp in a wood at night can. Rule it once and let the dice do the rest.

</Subsection>

<RuleBox title="What the Ambush is worth" icon="ph:eye-closed">

A soldier or a Foe **has not yet acted** until its first card of the Skirmish has come up.

- In round 1, every card of the side with the Ambush comes up before every card of the other side, and cards within a side keep their order.
- A soldier's Fight or Shoot roll against a Foe that has not yet acted gains 2 Bonus Dice. A Foe with the Ambush rolls 2 more Attack Dice against a soldier who has not yet acted.
- An attack by the ambushing side on a target that has not yet acted **cannot be answered**: no Reaction, no Guard, and every success of the attack is net.

That last line is where the harm comes from. Three Bandits with the Ambush can put a Rookie on the ground before the Squad has thrown a die.

</RuleBox>

<SectionHead id="the-foes" eyebrow="Section 2">The Foes</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Sighting report, inner district">Four of them, and not one of them looked at the ground.</FieldQuote>

Three kinds, and these three are the whole list. You add none: a new kind of Foe waits for rules that are not written yet.

<RuleBox title="Bars, and what lifts them" icon="ph:eye-slash">

Every number on these records sits under a bar, because a Skirmish is where the Squad earns them. A successful **Size Up** buys the group's Grit and how many more must be out before it breaks, in full, said aloud. Everything else the players learn by fighting: Attack Dice they count as you roll them in the open, Guard they count the same way, Health from how much a body takes.

Hover or focus a bar to look under it, and press it to keep it lifted.

</RuleBox>

<MarginNote stamp="Read twice">A Foe's weapon damage grows by 1 for each Net Success beyond the first, so a partial Block or Dodge is worth making. A musket ball that lands at one Net Success still deals 4, which is a Rookie on the ground with a Pierce Critical Injury.</MarginNote>

<SectionHead id="the-foe-rule" eyebrow="Section 3">The Foe Rule</SectionHead>

On the Foe group's card, every Foe still in the Skirmish that has not broken takes one turn, in label order. Take the first step whose test the Foe meets.

- **Candidates** are every soldier taking part who is alive and not Down. A Foe never attacks a Down soldier, by the rule or by your direction.
- **The last attacker** is the candidate who most recently made a Fight or Shoot roll against this Foe, whether it landed or not.
- **The lowest card** is the lowest initiative card this round, among the candidates the step names.

<StepFlow id="fig-foe-rule" fig="Fig. 1" title="Whose turn it is, and who it comes for" steps={foeRuleSteps()} />

<CommanderNote title="The rule is legible on purpose">

A Squad can read this rule off the table and steer a Foe onto whoever last struck it, and that is allowed. Let them. A player who works out that striking the trooper pulls him off the medic has learned the fight, and the fight is better for it. The rule is also the baseline everything else in this section is measured against, so run it as written and direct only where the fiction gives you a reason.

</CommanderNote>

<Subsection title="Guard, and their firearms" id="guard-and-firearms">

**Guard** is a Foe's cancelling roll, and it is the only roll it makes besides its attack. When a soldier's Fight or Shoot roll against the Foe is final, roll the Foe's Guard dice in the open as base dice. Each success cancels one of the attack's, and the attack lands on 1 or more Net Successes. Guard is rolled against every attack on the Foe, however many come in a round, and a Held Foe still rolls it. It takes none of these: {guardNever()}. It spends nothing.

**Firearms.** A Foe's firearm is loaded when the Skirmish begins and empty after it Shoots. A Foe's Reload spends its turn and no shot, and a Foe never runs out.

**Never.** No Foe takes {foeNever()}. A Foe has no Stress, makes no Fear Roll, and has no Stress Response. At 0 Health it is out of the Skirmish, dead if the damage was Cut, Pierce, or Burn and out cold if it was Crush, and it is never a target again.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="directing-a-foe" eyebrow="Section 4">Directing a Foe</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual, on command">A reason the table cannot hear is not a reason.</FieldQuote>

On a Foe's turn, when the fiction gives that Foe a reason, you may direct its target or its action in place of the rule's pick. Say the reason aloud before the Foe acts: the sergeant goes for the medic who shot his man.

<RuleBox title="What a directed Foe still does" icon="ph:user-focus">

- **Only a turn the rule could give.** It Fights, Shoots, reloads, closes in and Fights, or does nothing, with its own kind's weapons.
- **Never against a Down soldier.** The one direction that would read as an execution cannot be given.
- **A Held Foe still only Fights its holder,** bare-handed.
- **It never leaves alone.** A Foe leaves at 0 Health, or when its group breaks or surrenders. If you want one to run, break its group sooner, with a reason said aloud.
- **Its numbers stand.** Attack Dice and Guard come off its record, rolled in the open and never adjusted, and damage, Net Successes, and whether a Foe at 0 Health is dead or out cold are all as written.

</RuleBox>

<Subsection title="After a Size Up" id="after-a-size-up">

A successful Size Up buys the truth, and it holds you to it. You state the group's Grit, how many more of its Foes must be out before it breaks, and what each Foe will do on the group's next card under the rule as things stand. For a Foe you already mean to direct, you state what it will do instead, and the reason.

Each Foe then does what you stated on its turn on that card. Only something that happens **after** the Size Up changes it: for a Foe stated under the rule, a change in what the rule tests, after which the rule picks again; for either, a new reason the fiction gives, said aloud; for a directed Foe, an action it can no longer take, after which the rule picks unless you direct it again with a reason said aloud. Never hold a reason back at a Size Up and produce it on that card.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="grit-and-breaking" eyebrow="Section 5">Grit and Breaking</SectionHead>

**Grit is the latest a group breaks,** not the moment it must. When the number of the group's Foes out of the Skirmish reaches its Grit, it breaks: at once, every Foe of it still in the Skirmish that is not Held becomes Apart from every soldier and takes no further turn, and each leaves at the end of the round. A Held Foe stays Held, and leaves at the end of the round its hold ends.

**You may break it sooner, or have it surrender,** with a reason said aloud as it happens: the officer falls, the sergeant sees the flare, the third man hears what the first two sounded like. Never later. A group broken sooner breaks as above. A group that surrenders yields as the Surrender ask gives, and the Skirmish ends.

**A failed threat hardens them.** A Parley made as a threat that fails raises the group's Grit by 1 and its Parley value by 1 for the rest of the Skirmish.

<MarginNote>The three tests that end a Skirmish are the players' to read, on <a href="/guide/skirmishes/#ending-a-skirmish">Skirmishes</a>. You never end one yourself, and breaking a group is not ending the fight.</MarginNote>

<SectionHead id="parley" eyebrow="Section 6">Parley at the Table</SectionHead>

A group's **Parley value** is how hard it is to talk down, and it is parted from its Grit on purpose. The group that is hardest to put down is not the hardest to reason with: a patrol under orders fights to the last and can still be argued round, for one success more than Bandits ask.

<GmTable table="parley-needs" />

- **Each Foe out of the Skirmish takes 1 off the need,** never below 1, before the ask's own number is added. Talking works better once the shooting has gone badly for them.
- **A success ends the Skirmish,** and the group does what the ask says. Answer truthfully, from what its Foes actually know. A group that answers with a lie you invented is a group the players will never talk to again.
- **A failure costs nothing,** unless it was a threat, which hardens them.
- **With no Skirmish under way,** when a Brief or your framing sets the Squad before one of these groups, each soldier may make one Parley: the group's Parley value plus the ask's number, no Help, no Cover, Pushed if they like, and never tried again by that soldier with that group. Talking to anyone who is not one of these groups is a called roll.

<SectionHead id="running-the-fight" eyebrow="Section 7">Running the Fight</SectionHead>

<InkVignette caption="Two of the patrol still standing, and nobody has said the word surrender yet." label="Soldiers facing armed troopers across a narrow street at night, weapons lowered but not put away" art="gm-foes-and-skirmishes-standoff" />

<Subsection title="What to expect" id="what-to-expect">

- **A Squad beats a Military Police patrol nearly every time,** and a soldier dies in one of these fights only rarely. The danger is not the loss, it is the Critical Injury that rides home with them.
- **One soldier against one trooper is close to even,** a little better than even. Nothing about a lone Survey Corps soldier makes a trooper a walkover.
- **A night Ambush by Bandits costs blood and almost never a life.** It is the cheapest hard scene in these rules, which is why the Night table reaches for it.
- **A Skirmish sits a notch below a Titan fight,** and it should run faster at the table too: no Positions, no Attention, no clocks, and one card for the whole Foe group.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Practical advice" id="practical-advice">

- **Roll their dice in the open, all of them.** Attack Dice and Guard both. A Foe that people can count is a Foe they can plan around, and that is the whole shape of this fight.
- **Say who each Foe is going for, and why, before you roll.** With the rule, that is one short sentence: "he is still on Mila, she cut him last turn."
- **Let the Blocks happen.** People can be blocked and Titans cannot, and a Block that cancels one success takes a point off the damage. Soldiers who have only ever fought Titans forget this; remind them once.
- **Watch the firearms.** A loaded musket is the one thing on the table that drops a healthy soldier in a single roll, and a sentry the Squad closes with has only his hands. Cover that ground and the fight changes.
- **Give them the way out.** Every group but a patrol under orders breaks at the first body. Say what breaking looks like, so a Squad that wants prisoners or passage knows it can stop short of killing everybody.
- **Bare hands never kill.** A soldier who wants a person alive has that option in the rules, with the Grapple and with Release. Make sure they know it before the first blade comes out.

</Subsection>

<FieldReport id="example-prowlers" title="The Bandits at Far">

The Night table gave Prowlers: three Bandits with the Ambush, carrying firebrands, at a forest Waypoint at Far.

**Setting out.** Each Bandit rolls for its weapon. Bandits 1 and 2 roll 2 and 3, so they carry firebrands; Bandit 3 rolls 5 and carries a knife. Everyone is Apart. The Foes have the Ambush, so no Sneak is rolled.

**Round 1.** The Bandits' card comes up first. Among the soldiers, Private Mila Brandt holds the lowest card.

Bandit 1 has nobody Engaged with it and no firearm, so the rule sends it to step 5: it closes in on Mila and Fights with its firebrand. Four Attack Dice, plus 2 for the Ambush since Mila has not yet acted: two 6s. The Ambush leaves it unanswered, so both successes are net, and the firebrand deals 1 plus 1 for the second: 2 Burn. Mila's Health of 4 falls to 2.

Bandits 2 and 3 do the same, Mila's card still the lowest. Bandit 2 shows one 6, for 1 more, and Mila is at 1. Bandit 3's six dice show none.

**Oskar's card.** He closes in on Bandit 1 and Fights with the Blade Set in his handles: Strength 4, Hand-to-Hand 1, Gear Dice 1, Stress 3. Bandit 1 has acted, so no Ambush dice. Three 6s. Bandit 1's Guard of 3 dice shows one 6 and cancels one. Two Net Successes: 2 damage plus 1, so 3 Cut, and its Health of 3 is gone. It is dead.

Oskar has never killed a person, so the Fear Roll for a first human kill comes as the kill resolves.

**And that is the fight.** One Bandit out reaches a Bandit group's Grit of 1. The group breaks: Bandits 2 and 3 become Apart at once, take no further turn, and leave at the end of the round. No Foe is left, so the Skirmish ends and its end steps run.

</FieldReport>

<RuleBox variant="stop" title="Never yours to move" icon="ph:prohibit">

- **A Foe's Attack Dice and Guard,** rolled in the open from its record and never adjusted.
- **Damage, Net Successes, and what 0 Health means:** dead from Cut, Pierce, or Burn, out cold from Crush.
- **The ban on attacking a Down soldier.**
- **That a Foe never Pushes, Helps, or Covers,** and that a Held Foe only Fights its holder.
- **A failed threat's rise,** and a group's Grit as the latest it breaks.
- **The three tests that end a Skirmish.**

Everything else you rule, you rule before the dice and say out loud.

</RuleBox>
