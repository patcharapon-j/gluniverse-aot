<FieldQuote cite="Standing order to Squad Leaders">The country does not know you are coming. That is the only mercy in it.</FieldQuote>

The Squad always reaches the next Waypoint. What it costs to get there is the route you wrote, the rolls the players make, and two tables you read out exactly as they come up.

<SectionHead id="writing-the-brief" eyebrow="Section 1">Writing the Brief</SectionHead>

You write the Brief as Command, before the Expedition. Its route names the **Waypoints in order** with their kinds and the Depot; each Leg's **Distance Band** and **Formation Post**; the **days a Hard Ride is ordered**; and the **Abnormal** the two Abnormal rows mean.

**Set once, and nothing changes it.** The hazard roll reads Band, Post, and Pace as written — once the Squad rides out, no ruling of yours moves any of them.

<FinePrint title="the bounds of a route, and the interim route">

<RuleBox title="The bounds of a route" icon="ph:map-trifold">

- **4 to 6 Legs,** and so 4 to 6 Waypoints.
- **No Leg deeper than its place allows.** First and last are Near; second and second-to-last Near or Far; only the ones between can be Deep.
- **At most one Depot,** and never the gate.
- **A Hard Ride** at most once a day, and only as the day's third Leg.

A route outside these bounds is not a route in these rules. Write inside them, or roll the interim route below.

</RuleBox>

<GmTable table="waypoint-kinds" />

At a **Depot** the whole Squad takes a full Standard Issue and Squad Supply is restocked. Reaching the **gate** ends the Expedition at the end of that Leg — no further Leg, no camp, and Downtime begins.

<MarginNote stamp="Read twice">The Waypoint kinds are the strongest thing you write. A forest Waypoint and an open plain are the same Leg on the hazard table and two different Titan fights, because the kind sets the field rating.</MarginNote>

<CommanderNote title="What a route is worth">

An interim route climbs to Deep in the middle and comes back, and every Leg after the first night carries the camps already made. **That shape is what the hazard table was built for.** A Brief that runs Deep early, or sends a Squad six Legs out with no Depot, is a harder Expedition than the tables ship for. Write the hard route when you mean to, and know that you have. A Hard Ride comes up on a {hardRideOn()} — as often as Command should be pushing them.

</CommanderNote>

<SectionHead id="running-the-day" eyebrow="Section 2">Running the Day</SectionHead>

Two Legs and a Night Camp, with a Hard Ride third when Command orders one — on a Brief day, or on a {hardRideOn()} rolled after the second Leg on an interim route. **Legs are never ridden at night.** Frame a Waypoint scene after any Leg you like.

<FinePrint title="your side of a Leg, and what you may stake">

<Subsection title="Your side of a Leg" id="your-side-of-a-leg">

The Squad's side is on <a href="/guide/expeditions-and-downtime/#riding-a-leg">Expeditions &amp; Downtime</a>. Yours is short:

- **The Leg roll is theirs.** They choose the Lead and the Help. You name the step and let it fall — never retried.
- **They always arrive.** Success: 1 ration, and −1 hazard per extra success. Failure: 2 rations and +1. A Hard Ride doubles the rations.
- **Nobody able to roll?** It fails, with no Help and no Push, and nothing else changes. A row that names the Lead does nothing when there is none.
- **Then the hazard** — the whole of your work on a Leg.
- **A fight a row begins** takes its field rating from the Waypoint this Leg leads to; everything unnamed comes from the setup table. **A retreat** sends the Squad back to where this Leg started, to ride it again with a fresh roll and a fresh hazard.

</Subsection>

<RuleBox title="Circumstances, and what you may stake" icon="ph:cloud-rain">

Your step reaches the Leg roll, the camp roll, the Straggler's Endure, and every called roll in a scene. **It never reaches a table roll.**

- **Neither hazard table takes a step at all.** No pool, no Push, no Help, never retried.
- **A roll a row calls for** takes the step already in force when the hazard was rolled — never a new one for the danger the row describes.
- **Stakes belong to called rolls,** which live in Waypoint scenes. Leg rolls, camp rolls, and hazard rows carry their own costs, written in the table, and you add nothing.

</RuleBox>

</FinePrint>

<MarginNote stamp="Read twice">The hazard climbs with the distance and with every night already spent outside. A Squad four days out at Deep is rolling against a total no Formation Post can bring back down — and that is the country, not a ruling.</MarginNote>

<RuleBox variant="stop" title="Read the row as it came up" icon="ph:prohibit">

Both tables are rolled in the open, for the Squad, by nobody in particular. Describe what comes up and **never soften it, swap it, or reach past it** for something better. You choose nothing on either table: where a row lets the players choose, they do.

A danger you author belongs in a Waypoint scene, never in place of a hazard roll.

</RuleBox>

<FinePrint title="picking the Straggler, and hunger">

<Subsection title="The Straggler" id="the-straggler">

The row that takes a soldier picks them at random. **The pick is a roll, not a choice.**

1. **Which kind,** on the table below — only living soldiers not Down count. If neither kind has one, resolve the row as Titan on the formation.
2. **Which soldier.** Number them 1 upward in sheet order, roll D6, re-rolling above the count. **The sheet's order chooses nothing.**
3. **Endure,** needing 1 success. No Help, no Cover, Push allowed, never retried.
4. **On a failure,** a {straggler.type} Critical Injury at a rolled location, which {straggler.lethal ? 'can be lethal' : 'is never lethal'}. No Attack Dice, no Reaction, no Net Success rider — and it counts as gained inside the fight that follows.
5. **Then the fight,** victim In Reach and everyone else Distant.

</Subsection>

<GmTable table="straggler-victim" />

<Subsection title="Hunger" id="hunger">

When a Leg or camp must spend rations the Squad does not hold, it spends everything and **every soldier gains 1 Stress**, with no roll. Holding short counts as holding none, and a camp that meets hunger gives no Camp Relief however the roll went.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="waypoint-scenes" eyebrow="Section 4">Waypoint Scenes</SectionHead>

Between Legs you may frame a scene at a Waypoint: an abandoned town searched, survivors found, a flooded ford crossed. **This is where the danger you author goes.**

- **It runs on called rolls,** step and stakes named before the dice. A search adds Squad Supply only within its caps, and one scene yields to **at most two** searches.
- **A Skirmish or Titan Engagement may begin in it** by the fiction.
- **It rides no Leg:** no Leg roll, no camp roll, neither hazard table, and no ration unless a stake names one. **It never stands in place of a hazard roll.**

**A retreat in a scene** ends the scene and falls back exactly as a hazard's retreat does, to the Waypoint that Leg started from. The Leg ridden again takes the day's next slot — and if no slot is left, the Squad camps where it fell back to and rides that Leg as the next day's first, at Steady.

<SectionHead id="the-night-camp" eyebrow="Section 5">The Night Camp</SectionHead>

1. **The camp roll:** Survive, by one soldier not Down, for 1 success. Up to 3 may Help for nothing; Push and Cover allowed; never retried. Nobody able? It fails.
2. **Rations:** spend 1, or meet hunger.
3. **Camp Relief:** a successful roll with no hunger takes **1 Stress** off everyone.
4. **The night hazard.** Titans do not walk at night — only an Abnormal does, which is why the one Titan row names one.
5. **A day passes:** a care window with Field Repair in it, the day-limit Death Rolls, all damage healed, and a day of healing.

<FinePrint title="where the camp stands, and a retreat at night">

<Subsection title="Where the camp stands" id="where-the-camp-stands">

At the Waypoint the Squad is standing at — the one the day's last Leg reached, or the one it fell back to.

- **A fight the Night table begins takes that Waypoint's field rating.** A camp at the gate they rode out of has no kind, so roll the rating on the setup table; the Expedition goes on, because only the route's last Waypoint ends it.
- **The Distance Band the roll adds is still the day's last ridden Leg's,** whatever a retreat did to where they stand.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="A retreat at night" id="a-retreat-at-night">

The Squad falls back overnight to where the day's last Leg started, and the camp goes on there at its last step — no new camp roll, ration, or hazard, and it still counts as the night. **Falling back is not riding a Leg,** so no Leg is ridden in the dark; the morning rides that Leg again from the beginning.

A camp already standing where that Leg started falls back no further: it breaks off where it is and the queued Leg waits for morning.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<CommanderNote title="What the tables will do to a campaign">

Most Legs cost a ration and a little Stress. The fights come from the deep Legs late in a long ride, and they come without warning — which is the point of rolling in the open. Do not pre-empt a bad total by moving a Waypoint, and do not save a Squad from the Straggler by picking a different victim. **The Expedition is the one part of this game where nobody, you included, decides what is out there.**

</CommanderNote>
