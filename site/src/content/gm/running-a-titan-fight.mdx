A Titan fight runs itself once it is set up. Your work is the setup, one tracker, one hidden roll, and the honesty to let every open die stand.

<SectionHead id="making-the-call" eyebrow="Section 1">Making the Call</SectionHead>

Your judgment is part of the rules, and it works in one direction: **you rule before the dice, never after.** Once they fall, every die and table result stands. The players never have to wonder whether the fight is real.

<a href="/guide/rules-of-play/#when-the-gm-calls-a-roll">The Rules of Play</a> carries the procedure — the three tests, the ladder, the closed menu of stakes, and the long list a ruling never touches. This is how to use it well.

<FactTable caption="Three things you name, and one you do not" columns={['', '']} rows={[
  ['The roll', 'Only when no rule covers the act, the outcome is in doubt, <em>and</em> failure costs something. Otherwise say it happens, or that it cannot be done — now, before anyone reaches for a die. <b>A called roll always needs 1 success.</b>'],
  ['The step', 'One, weighing the whole moment. Rain, dark, and a snapped strap are one judgment, not three. <b>Standard is the assumption in a fight</b>; name another only for what no rule prices. It reaches the soldiers\' pools and never the Titan.'],
  ['The stakes', 'One cost, out loud, before the dice — and <b>state the soldier\'s current Health</b> with any stake naming damage or a fall. A failure should move the story on: "the window closes, and the patrol is on the stair" is a stake; "nothing happens" is a stop.'],
  ['Never yours', 'Behavior Tables, the Attention Ladder, Attack Dice, the Reaction procedure, the clocks, the retreat, a Titan\'s numbers, and every die once it is rolled.'],
]} />

Call rolls sparingly. A roll is a moment with something at stake, and calling one for everything turns a scene into a queue. **Inside a fight a called roll is the last resort:** an act is first a Catalog entry, then an improvised act, and a called roll only when it changes nothing the fight tracks.

<SectionHead id="setting-up-the-fight" eyebrow="Section 2">Setting Up the Fight</SectionHead>

**When a fight starts is scene framing. How it starts, runs, and ends is not.**

<Subsection title="The order of setup" id="the-order-of-setup">

1. **Take what is named.** The starting rule names the field (its size, its field rating, and any zone's rating), the Focus Titan, any Background Titans and their clocks, and the retreat clock's length. Your framing may name whatever it left out — **except the retreat clock**. Anything neither names comes from the setup table.
2. **Generate the field.** The Standard field of 13 zones by default, each zone rated from the field rating: {anchorCounts()}. Nothing is rolled that is already named. Whatever a zone's rating leaves is every soldier's Momentum cap there, and everyone starts at 0 Momentum.
3. **Label the Focus Titan A,** stood in the **centre zone**, Body Parts Intact, no Openings, clock empty, **Frenzy 0**, and roll its hidden Next Behavior.
4. **Place the soldiers and horses in the Squad's start zone,** one zone out, then let the Titan read its ladder with no cards dealt. A rung that picks out one soldier gives it Attention from setup; otherwise nothing holds it until its first card.
5. **Fear Rolls** for a first Titan fight and for an Abnormal. Squad Tactics become unused. Round 1 begins.

</Subsection>

<MarginNote>Focus Titan A stands in the field's centre zone; everyone else starts in the Squad's start zone, one zone out, mounted as they were, nobody airborne.</MarginNote>

<FinePrint title="what you may frame, and the setup table">

<Subsection title="What you may frame" id="what-you-may-frame">

Before any setup roll you may name the **Focus Titan** (any on the <a href="/gm/titans/">Titans</a> page), the **field size** (Skirmish, Standard, or Set piece), the **field rating**, **any single zone's rating**, and the **Background Titans** — none, one, or two, each on a clock of {backgroundClocks()} segments.

**What you name is never rolled,** so no Abnormal roll is made for a Focus Titan you named, and no terrain roll is made for a zone you rate. What stays closed to you: a soldier's zone or attachment, a Titan's zone, a Stride's length or route or tie, an entry zone, a Carry cost, which zone a wreck takes, the retreat clock's {RETREAT_CLOCK} segments, a setup row once rolled, and the opening Fear Rolls.

</Subsection>

<CommanderNote title="Framing and lethality">

The setup table's mix is the fight these rules are built around. A table that frames a Large Titan with two Background Titans every time is running a deadlier game than the numbers were set for, and it will bury a Squad. Frame for the story you want — and know which way you have leaned.

</CommanderNote>

<GmTable table="interim-setup" />

Roll each part in the table's order, skipping whatever is already named. **On a Medium Focus Titan, and only there,** roll again on the second table: that is what makes the Sprinting Abnormal the Focus Titan of {abnormalShare()} the table sets up. A Background Titan the table gives never rolls for an Abnormal.

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="what-you-track" eyebrow="Section 3">What You Track</SectionHead>

One tracker: an Engagement line carrying the field itself (size, rating, and every zone's own reading), a row per Focus Titan, a row per Background Titan, and a row left behind for each corpse. The players keep the rest — and their zones and attachments sit in one place, the zone and attachment columns of the Squad sheet. **Read a Position straight off those two columns: never write one down.**

<GmTable table="gm-tracker" />

<FinePrint title="keeping the tracker, and pacing the round">

<Subsection title="Running the round" id="when-to-update-it">

- **Update** after the deal, after each Titan card, after each strike or Break Attention, after each counted Grab turn, after each falling body and Heave, and at the end steps.
- **Anchors and Momentum are the players' arithmetic, not yours.** You mark a zone's rating down a step when an effect wrecks it, and say so aloud. You never grant, spend, or take a point of Momentum.
- **The loudest mark reaches far.** It matches every rung but *hooked into its body* — so read that rung first, then ask who is shouting.
- **Roll together** anything independent: the end-of-round Gas Rolls, and the witnesses' Fear Rolls for one event.
- **Frenzy rises at an end step,** 1 every third round to a cap of 3, at the end of rounds 3, 6, and 9, on every living Focus Titan. Say the new number aloud. It is the clock the players can do something about.
- **Expect the time.** One Focus Titan against four players and two Squadmates runs **12 to 20 minutes a round**; a round with a Grab runs to the top of that. Two Focus Titans run to half an hour — which is why the Background clocks are as long as they are.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="the-next-behavior" eyebrow="Section 4">Rolling the Next Behavior</SectionHead>

The one roll the players never see. Roll it where they cannot and record it face down — **once**: no re-roll, no second thought, no change of row.

<Subsection title="Frenzy" id="frenzy">

Each Focus Titan holds a **Frenzy**, starting at **0** and rising by **1 at the end of every third round, to a cap of 3**. It is public: say it aloud as it rises, and keep it on the tracker.

**The behavior roll is a D6 plus Frenzy,** and a result past the table's last entry reads as that entry. Every table is already ordered from terrorising to killing, so Frenzy walks the Titan down its own table: it postures for four rounds and is trying to kill somebody from round five.

That is the shape the fight is meant to have. **Every third round the Squad spends not killing it is a round it gets worse,** so a Squad that commits sees little Frenzy and a Squad that kites gets eaten. You do nothing to bring this about but add the number.

</Subsection>

Roll a new one when the Titan becomes a Focus Titan, after each card that resolved a behavior, and after a card spent inside a decoy's hold. A card that resolves nothing because the Titan holds a Grabbed soldier, or because nobody holds its Attention, keeps it as it stands.

<MarginNote>A telegraph shows the squad what is coming: reveal the new Next Behavior, and leave it revealed until a card resolves it or a decoy's card spends it.</MarginNote>

<FinePrint title="your side of a card">

<Subsection title="Your side of a card" id="running-the-card">

<a href="/guide/fighting-titans/">Fighting Titans</a> carries the card itself, and the players know it. Yours is the bookkeeping:

1. **Dead** — nothing happens. **Holding a Grabbed soldier** — nothing resolves, the ladder is not read, and the Next Behavior waits.
2. **A decoy holds it** — nothing resolves, the hold is one card shorter, and the Next Behavior is spent all the same. Only the hold's last card ends it and reads the ladder.
3. **Otherwise read the ladder.** Nobody holding Attention means nothing resolves and every mark stands.
4. **Stride.** If the holder is in another zone, it closes toward them one zone at a time, up to its Stride, and stops on entering their zone. A grounded Titan's Stride is 0. This is not a roll or a choice of yours: the destination is whoever the ladder just picked, and the route is fixed (lower-numbered zone on a tie).
5. **Choose:** the Next Behavior's entry, and Thrash without its Body Parts. **If the holder's Position does not fit, the Titan retargets:** read the ladder again among the soldiers who do fit, move its Attention to the one it picks, and resolve the entry against them. Only with nobody anywhere it can reach does the entry fall back — and the fallback is tested the same way, retargeting in its turn. Thrash comes when the fallback repeats the previous behavior, needs Body Parts it has lost, or reaches nobody either.
6. **Announce name, tier, and targets, then roll the Attack Dice in the open.** No step reaches them, and you never re-roll or adjust.
7. **Reactions and effects.** 0 successes whiffs against everyone and draws no dodge. A wreck takes one rating step off the Titan's own zone, landed or whiffed.
8. **Next.** Even a whiff resolved a behavior: decoys in a row to 0, marks clear, new Next Behavior face down.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="background-titans" eyebrow="Section 5">Background Titans and the Retreat</SectionHead>

A **Background Titan** is not a Titan in play. It is a closing clock — public, with nobody holding a Position relative to it.

<FinePrint title="the clocks, the retreat, and the stay limit">

<Subsection title="The clocks" id="the-clocks">

Each fills **1 segment** at the background-clocks end step, and **1 more per flare** spent on a Break Attention, resolving there and then, mid-round. **A flare never fills the retreat clock.** Several full at once resolve in tracker order.

**When one fills** with fewer than two Focus Titans alive, that Titan **enters**: next label, clean, standing in the **entry zone** (the edge zone holding no soldier and farthest from the nearest one, a tie going to the lowest-numbered; with no soldier on the field at all, the lowest-numbered edge zone, a provisional rule not yet settled), so everyone on the field holds Distant relative to it, and it reads its Attention. At an end step no cards break a tie, so its Attention is held by nothing until its first card. The soldiers make the second-Focus-Titan or Abnormal Fear Roll, one each, and it strides from its first card, taken from the next round.

**With two Focus Titans already alive it does not enter** — the fight becomes a retreat instead.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The retreat clock" id="the-retreat-clock">

Every fight carries one. The starting rule names its length, or the setup table gives it {RETREAT_CLOCK} segments. It fills **last** at the end step, after every Background clock has filled and resolved — so a Background Titan whose clock fills on the same step **enters before the retreat begins**.

</Subsection>

<MarginNote stamp="Why 8">A full canister lasts about eight rounds of ODM use. The retreat begins as the gas runs low, and a Squad that lingers past it is a Squad the Titans are gathering on.</MarginNote>

<Subsection title="Running the retreat" id="running-the-retreat">

From the moment it begins: no clock fills again and no Titan enters. Only Regeneration keeps running.

**Moves are forced** — the four options are on <a href="/guide/fighting-titans/">Fighting Titans</a>, and the move still narrows rather than vanishes, so an ODM move under one is still a rolled Flight. **Lift Comrade is not on that list:** a soldier who lifts a comrade carries them out by option 1 or 2.

**Order:** move before action, except a stay-put move (after the action taken for that comrade) and a move on a Lift Comrade turn (after the lift). **No Nape strike during a retreat,** and **no return** once a soldier has left.

</Subsection>

<RuleBox title="The stay limit" icon="ph:hourglass">

Stepping toward or staying with a fallen comrade lasts only as many rounds as the retreat clock has segments, counted from the round after the retreat began — which is what the Engagement line records the beginning round for.

It covers a comrade Pinned under a corpse in **every** retreat, and every fallen comrade once no Focus Titan is alive. After it, each move is a step out, leaving, letting go, or nothing.

From the first round past the limit, a soldier whom a lost-limb grade forbids every move **is not standing** — which is how a fight beside a corpse ends instead of running for ever.

</RuleBox>

</FinePrint>

<InkVignette caption="The last two out, one carried, the corpse steaming behind them." label="Two soldiers on a horse riding away from a steaming Titan corpse" art="gm-running-a-titan-fight-retreat" />

A retreat keeps the fight running until everyone is out or the Titans are dead. **It is not an ending.** Only the two tests end a fight, and you never end one yourself.
