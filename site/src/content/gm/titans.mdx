<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual, on identification">There is no such thing as an ordinary one. There is only one you have seen before.</FieldQuote>

Four Titans, each a record you can run a whole fight from. A standard Titan's numbers are public — read them aloud if a player asks. An Abnormal's sit under bars, and only a Read lifts one.

<SectionHead id="which-titan-appears" eyebrow="Section 1">Which Titan Appears</SectionHead>

**Name it as framing** and it is never rolled — any Titan here, standard or Abnormal. Otherwise the rule that begins the fight names it, or the setup table gives a Size Class and you take the standard Titan of that class.

**One exception:** a Focus Titan rolled Medium is re-rolled, and on a **6** it is the Sprinting Abnormal instead — {abnormalShare()} the table sets up. Background Titans never roll for an Abnormal, though a rule may name one.

An Abnormal that starts a fight as a Focus Titan, or becomes one, sets off the Abnormal Fear Roll.

<SectionHead id="reading-a-dossier" eyebrow="Section 2">Reading a Dossier</SectionHead>

Each record gives Size Class, **Tempo**, **Nape Depth**, the **Regeneration clock**, the **Heave rating**, the Body Parts in order with their Toughness, and the ladder it uses. **The order of the Body Parts is not decoration:** it breaks Regeneration ties and decides which arm Grabs.

**The heavy entry on every table wrecks an Anchor** — landed or whiffed, because it is the Titan going through the treeline rather than a blow at a person. Lower the fight's Anchors by 1, say so, and remember every Momentum cap just dropped with it.

<RuleBox title="Bars, and what lifts them" icon="ph:eye-slash">

A standard Titan hides only its Next Behavior.

An Abnormal hides four things until a Read buys each, one success at a time: **a Body Part's Toughness**, **its Nape Depth**, **its clock's length**, and **its Attention Ladder**. Hover or focus a bar to look under it; press to keep it lifted.

Until a Read buys the clock, show only its filled segments. **Its Behavior Table is never hidden.**

</RuleBox>

<MarginNote>The squad chooses which facts a Read buys; you reveal them to the whole table at once.</MarginNote>

<Subsection title="Thrash, and why you will resolve it so often" id="thrash">

Thrash is the entry these Titans resolve most, and almost all of it comes from an Attention holder standing where the rolled entry cannot reach — usually a Nape striker who fell short and hangs at Blind Spot. **A Titan with a blade at its Nape mostly thrashes.**

That is the fallback rule working, not a fault in the tables. Read the line as written: the Titan failing to get at the soldier who is hurting it.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="small-titan" eyebrow="Section 3">The Small Titan</SectionHead>

Three to five metres, **two cards a round**. It crouches, crawls, and ducks, so its bite and hands come low — it clutches at legs. Its parts are Toughness 1, so two successes break one.

<TitanDossier titan="small-titan" id="small-titan-dossier" />

<GmTable table="behavior-requirements" titan="small-titan" />

- **Lurch Closer and Bite need no Body Part,** so with both legs Broken it still lurches head first and bites.
- **Take its arms and you take its Grab:** Clutch at the Legs and Grab move up to Scrabble and Gape.
- **A quick fight that still costs.** Two cards a round is twice the chances to be hit, so it lands more Critical Injuries and more Grabs than the Medium Titan, out of smaller pools.

<SectionHead id="medium-titan" eyebrow="Section 4">The Medium Titan</SectionHead>

Six to ten metres, one card a round. **The Titan every other fight is measured against,** and the one to reach for when you want the fight these rules were built around.

<TitanDossier titan="medium-titan" id="medium-titan-dossier" />

<GmTable table="behavior-requirements" titan="medium-titan" />

- **Fixed Grin uses its eyes,** so blinding it moves that result up to Snap Short — and costs the table its telegraph. **Blinding it takes away the Squad's own warning.**
- **Snap Short and Shake Off need no Body Part.**
- **Its Bite is fixed at the torso,** which reads worse for each torso wound already carried — and a Grab's crush is one of them. A soldier who has survived a Grab or two can die outright to a Bite, with no Death Roll.
- **Grab sits at 6 and Bite at 5,** so breaking both arms moves the Grab's share up to Fixed Grin rather than doubling the Bite.

<SectionHead id="large-titan" eyebrow="Section 5">The Large Titan</SectionHead>

Eleven to fifteen metres: thicker arms, a longer clock, the heaviest pools in Phase 1. It kills in about the same round as the Medium Titan — but far more fights against it end with nobody dead and nothing dead either.

<TitanDossier titan="large-titan" id="large-titan-dossier" />

<GmTable table="behavior-requirements" titan="large-titan" />

- **Its mouth is far above the ground.** Bite needs a soldier On Body; against one In Reach it becomes Crush, pinning them under its bulk.
- **Heavy Tread needs both legs.** One Broken leg grounds it, so that result moves up to Crush — **cutting one leg changes what it can do to a whole Position at once.**
- **Crush and Shrug Off need no Body Part,** and Crush harms the holder alone.

<CommanderNote title="Before you frame a Large Titan">

The hardest standard fight in Phase 1, and hardest of all on a Squad with no Talents worth the name. Against template soldiers it drags: a kill in round four, one fight in five ending with no kill at all, and roughly twice the Critical Injuries a Medium Titan deals. Frame it knowing that, and give the Squad a reason to be somewhere else.

</CommanderNote>

<SectionHead id="sprinting-abnormal" eyebrow="Section 6">The Sprinting Abnormal</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Sighting report, outer formation">It did not stop for the flank. It did not stop for anything.</FieldQuote>

A Medium Abnormal that runs. The squad sees what any Focus Titan shows — Medium, two cards a round, Heave 3, its counts and states on your tracker, and its Behavior Table in the open.

<TitanDossier titan="sprinting-abnormal" id="sprinting-abnormal-dossier" />

<GmTable table="behavior-requirements" titan="sprinting-abnormal" />

- **It runs through people.** Run Past and Trample reach a holder at Distant. **Distant is out of a standing Titan's reach, not out of a running one's way.**
- **Its hands and jaws need you close.** Grab and Headlong Lunge need In Reach or On Body and roll the Medium Titan's *control* pool — it snatches at full stride, and a soldier leaps clear of a Titan that cannot stop.
- **Cut one leg and it is finished as a runner.** Run Past, Trample, and the Lunge all need both.
- **Clinging to it is risky, but not as risky as it looks.** Pitch Headlong is the smallest pool on its table.
- **No entry on any of these tables affects a horse.** Titans go for people.

<Subsection title="Its hidden values" id="abnormal-hidden-values">

Four bars on the record above: each Body Part's Toughness, its Nape Depth, its clock's length, and the ladder below. Each comes out of a Read, one success at a time, and stays revealed for the rest of the fight.

</Subsection>

<AbnormalLadder titan="sprinting-abnormal" />

<Subsection title="What its ladder means at the table" id="abnormal-ladder-at-the-table">

- **A blade at its Nape or a soldier On Body turns it first,** as on every ladder.
- **Then noise** — but only while nobody is hooked into it. A shout reaches past its quarry and past whoever is nearest.
- **Then whoever it is already running down,** until a Nape strike, a soldier On Body, or noise turns it. **A holder who gets away to Distant has led it off.**
- **Only then whoever is nearest.** It has no rung for reach and none for who just hurt it, so the cutters at its legs draw it only when it is running nobody down.
- **A fight against it begins with nothing holding its Attention,** so its first card turns to whoever has come closest — or to the lowest card when nobody has moved.

</Subsection>

<CommanderNote title="Running an Abnormal honestly">

Its ladder is what a Read is worth most against, because knowing it gives a player something to do: stop being its quarry by cutting its Nape, putting someone On Body, making noise, or riding clear. Reveal a rung the moment a Read buys it, in full, then run it exactly as written. **An Abnormal that plays by a ladder nobody can learn is just an unfair Titan.**

</CommanderNote>

<Subsection title="Any other Abnormal" id="other-abnormals">

An Abnormal breaks the standard pattern and runs from its own Behavior Table. Every Focus Titan rule applies, with these differences:

- **Its record lists every value in full,** and they may differ from its Size Class: Tempo 1–2, Nape Depth 3–4, Regeneration 2–4, Toughness 1–3, Attack Dice 3–12.
- **It may use its own ladder,** built from the closed list of rungs, beginning with hooked into its body and ending with nearest.
- **Its Toughness, Nape Depth, clock, and ladder are Read facts. Its Behavior Table is not.**
- **It causes the Abnormal Fear Roll.**

</Subsection>
