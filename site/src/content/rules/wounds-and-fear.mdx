<SectionHead id="health-and-harm" eyebrow="Section 1">Health and Harm</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Instructor's saying, Training Corps">It does not wear you down. It takes a piece and you go on without it.</FieldQuote>

<Subsection title="The row of boxes" id="the-row-of-boxes">

**<GlossaryTerm term="Health" />** is half of Strength plus Agility, rounded up. Attributes never rise, so Health never changes. Keep it as a row of boxes, each clean, marked by damage, or crossed off.

- **Each untreated <GlossaryTerm term="Critical Injury" /> crosses off one box.** Hold more than your Health and the extras cross off nothing.
- **Health lost** is what damage has marked.
- **<GlossaryTerm term="Current Health" />** is Health, less crossed-off boxes, less Health lost. At 0 you are <GlossaryTerm term="Down" />.

Health is small on purpose, because a <GlossaryTerm term="Titan attack" /> goes around it.

</Subsection>

<HarmTrack fig="Fig. 1" />

<Subsection title="The three kinds of harm" id="kinds-of-harm">

<CoreTable table="harm-kinds" />

**With no clean box,** a Critical Injury crosses off one marked by damage and your Health lost falls by 1. **Crossing off your last box** puts you Down and inflicts no further Critical Injury — only damage that brings you to 0, or damage taken at 0, gives one.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Titan attacks go around Health" id="titan-attacks">

A **Titan attack** is the harm a <GlossaryTerm term="Behavior Table" /> entry inflicts when a card resolves against you. **It is always a Critical Injury and never damage,** whatever your Health, and no entry names death.

Each entry lists its <GlossaryTerm term="Injury Location" /> (fixed or rolled), its <GlossaryTerm term="Injury Type" /> — Bite for a Titan's bite, Crush for everything else — and whether it can be lethal. It lands on **1 or more <GlossaryTerm term="Net Successes" />**; a soldier who cannot react cancels nothing.

<FactTable caption="Harm a Titan's body deals that is not a Titan attack" note="None rolls Attack Dice, none can be reacted to, and none takes the Net Success rider." columns={['Source', 'What it does']} rows={[
  ['The Grab\'s crush', 'A torso Crush Critical Injury that cannot be lethal.'],
  ['The lift and devour', 'Steps of the Grab, not attacks. The devour names death.'],
  ['Steam', 'Burn <em>damage</em>, so it reaches the injury tables only at 0 current Health. A roll of 0 does nothing.'],
  ['A falling Titan', 'A Crush Critical Injury that cannot be lethal, and <GlossaryTerm term="Pinned">Pins</GlossaryTerm> you.'],
  ['Corpse heat', 'A Burn Critical Injury at the start of each of your turns while Pinned under a <GlossaryTerm term="Corpse">corpse</GlossaryTerm>, and 1 Burn to each <GlossaryTerm term="Heave">Heaver</GlossaryTerm>.'],
]} />

So a soldier with no Health lost at all can be Down, or dying, from Titan attacks alone.

</Subsection>

<MarginNote>The Grab, the falling body, steam, and corpse heat: <a href="/guide/fighting-titans/#the-grab">Fighting Titans</a>.</MarginNote>

<Subsection title="Harm the GM names" id="harm-a-ruling-names">

A called roll's <GlossaryTerm term="Stakes">stakes</GlossaryTerm> can name harm, and only two kinds: **damage of 1 to 3** of its source's Injury Type, or **a fall**, low or high but never extreme. The GM states your current Health aloud with it, so the table knows beforehand whether failing puts you Down.

Never 4 damage or more — that is a musket's, and a musket is rolled. **No ruling ever hands out a Critical Injury, death, a <GlossaryTerm term="Death Roll" />, a <GlossaryTerm term="Scar" />, or <GlossaryTerm term="Grief" /> directly,** names a location, a row, or a time limit, or opens or closes a <GlossaryTerm term="Care window">care window</GlossaryTerm>.

</Subsection>

<CoreTable table="health-restore" />

<Subsection title="How to read an effect" id="effects">

Every row in this chapter names its effects from one closed list, so each has exactly one reading.

<CoreTable table="harm-effects" />

**Penalties add up** — Critical Injuries, lasting Stress Responses, Scars, <GlossaryTerm term="Circumstances" /> alike — and never take base dice below 1 or touch <GlossaryTerm term="Gear Dice" /> or <GlossaryTerm term="Stress Dice" />. **No row here damages gear,** and none causes a <GlossaryTerm term="Fear Roll" /> or any roll by a comrade.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="critical-injuries" eyebrow="Section 2">Critical Injuries</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="From a Cadet's diary">The surgeon asked which arm. I could not tell him.</FieldQuote>

A lasting wound tied to a location and a type. Its row says whether it puts you Down, whether it can kill and how fast, what it does while you hold it, and how long it takes to heal.

<IconKey title="The five Injury Types" items={injuryTypes().map((type) => ({ icon: type.icon, label: type.name, note: TYPE_NOTES[type.id] }))} />

<CoreTable table="injury-types" />

The type picks the row's name and applies its rider; everything else on the row is the same for every type. **No row here wounds the mind** — that is the second half of this chapter.

<StepFlow id="fig-injury" fig="Fig. 2" title="Gaining a Critical Injury" steps={criticalInjurySteps()} />

<CoreTable table="injury-location" />

<Subsection title="The four tables" id="the-four-tables">

One arm table for both arms, one leg table for both legs. Every table's first and last rows are open-ended, so every total finds a row however much <GlossaryTerm term="Worsening">worsening</GlossaryTerm> it carries. Instant death sits above what 2D6 alone can reach, so **a first wound at a location kills outright only when Net Successes lift the total.**

<InkVignette caption="A soldier braces a shoulder against a beam while a comrade cuts the sleeve away." label="A soldier cutting a comrade's sleeve away to reach a wound" art="guide-wounds-and-fear-injury" />

<Subsection title="Holding and treating" id="holding-and-treating">

You **hold** a Critical Injury from the moment you gain it until it heals, and every held one counts toward worsening whether treated or not. Arm and leg wounds are held **at a side**; a row with permanent effects is gained at most once per side, and those effects go on counting after it heals.

A successful <GlossaryTerm term="Treat Injury" /> makes it **treated**, which does exactly three things: stabilizes a lethal one, ends a Down row's hold, and gives back the Health box. It never removes the row's effects, never shortens its healing time, and never stops it counting toward worsening.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Lost limbs" id="lost-limbs">

<IconKey title="What a lost limb costs" items={[
  { icon: 'body-arm', label: 'An arm', note: 'One is a penalty. Both is the ban the ODM handles demand.' },
  { icon: 'body-leg', label: 'A leg', note: 'One makes the ground slow. Both end movement of every kind.' },
]} />

A side is lost on the **13 or more** row of the arm or leg table, and stays lost after the wound heals — a later roll of that row there uses its repeat row, so a limb is never lost twice. The grade applies for as long as you live, on top of the row's own effects and stacking with everything else: two lost legs dodge, Fly, and Ride at a **4-die penalty**.

<CoreTable table="lost-limbs" />

- **Cannot take** means the entry cannot be taken at all, and neither can anything needing its roll — an ODM move needs a Fly roll.
- **A move that cannot be made** leaves you at the Position you are placed at. A soldier forbidden every move may consent to be carried with Lift Comrade, and keeps their action.
- **No Gear Dice from an item** still rolls the entry with any other item it allows: both arms lost still takes the horse's dice on a mounted dodge.
- **Medical Retirement** is open, at the player's choice, to anyone who has lost both arms or both legs.

**Prosthetics.** A gear item, requisitioned at Limited Scarcity and fitted during a <GlossaryTerm term="Downtime" /> once that side's wound has healed — one per side, no roll, permanent. Your grade is then read against sides lost **less prosthetics of that kind**, so one lost arm with a prosthetic meets no grade. The row's own permanent effects stay regardless. A <GlossaryTerm term="Prosthetic" /> gives no dice and is never a weapon.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="down-and-dying" eyebrow="Section 3">Down and Dying</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="A Squad Leader, on the ride back">Count them on the horses, not on the ground. The ground lies.</FieldQuote>

<Subsection title="Down" id="down">

You are **Down** while your current Health is 0, or while you hold an untreated Critical Injury whose row says so.

<BoxPair>
<RuleBox title="What you can still do" icon="ph:hand">

- **Your turns still happen,** each with its move and no action, and each still counts for every rule that counts turns.
- **Your move** can only make the one change Fighting Titans allows a Down soldier.
- **You make Death Rolls.**
- **You can be helped:** treated, <GlossaryTerm term="Rally">Rallied</GlossaryTerm>, and lifted.
- You keep every Stress, Scar, Grief, and <GlossaryTerm term="Grabbed" /> state, and can still gain more.

</RuleBox>
<RuleBox title="What you cannot do" variant="stop" icon="ph:prohibit">

- **Take any action,** <GlossaryTerm term="Help" /> included.
- **<GlossaryTerm term="Push" />, Help, <GlossaryTerm term="Cover" />, or react.** A behavior you cannot answer lands.
- **Make any attribute roll but a Death Roll** — so never a Stress Response.
- **Make a Fear Roll.**

</RuleBox>
</BoxPair>

**Down ends the moment** your current Health is above 0 and no untreated Down row holds you. Only three things do that: treating or healing a Critical Injury, a revive, and a day passing.

**Turns are never given back.** Going Down costs you any action of this round's turn you still held, and a turn that begins while you are Down has its move only. Ending Down restores nothing already spent.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Lethal wounds and the Death Roll" id="time-limits">

A lethal Critical Injury carries a time limit, and each time it runs out you make a Death Roll.

<CoreTable table="time-limits" />

Away from a fight there are no turns, so a turn or end-of-fight limit runs out **once**, right after the care window for that harm — and a limit you survive out there becomes a **daily** one.

<FactTable caption="The Death Roll" columns={['', '']} rows={[
  ['Pool', 'Strength, plus one Talent that names the Death Roll, minus that wound\'s Death Roll penalty.'],
  ['Left out', 'Stress Dice, Help, the Push, Circumstances, gear, and every source of <GlossaryTerm term="Bonus Dice" />.'],
  ['Needs', '1 success. It is not an action and spends nothing.'],
  ['How many', 'One per Critical Injury; you choose the order when several run out together. <b>The first failure kills you,</b> and no more are made.'],
]} />

<CoreTable table="death-roll-outcomes" />

<MarginNote stamp="Read twice">Strength buys you nothing else in the mud. It is the one attribute that decides whether you get up.</MarginNote>

<TryRollButton pool={{ base: 3 }} pushable={false} label="Try a Death Roll" />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Dying" id="dying">

You die from a failed Death Roll, an instant-death row, or any rule that names death. A dead soldier leaves play, is replaced by promotion or a new character when the procedure ends, and every <GlossaryTerm term="Drive" /> that named them can never trigger again. The death is a Fear Roll trigger and it gives Grief.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="treating-wounds" eyebrow="Section 4">Treating Wounds</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual, on field medicine">Stop the bleeding, name the wound, write the time. Everything else waits for the wall.</FieldQuote>

**Treat Injury** is a Wits action taking Gear Dice from a medical kit — Wits alone without one. Declare one use and one patient before rolling.

<CoreTable table="treat-injury-uses" />

<FactTable caption="What changes the roll" columns={['', '']} rows={[

  ['A Burn', 'Needs a medical kit, or 1 medical unit of Squad Supply spent on the roll. Wits alone will not do.'],
  ['A Pierce (7 row up)', 'A <b>1-die penalty</b> on every treat use on it, everywhere.'],
  ['Circumstances', 'As on any roll. Standard in a fight unless something no rule prices moves it.'],
  ['Talents', 'Field Medicine adds dice, Sure Hands allows a second Push, Careful Nursing halves a comrade\'s remaining healing time.'],

**In a fight** it is an action, and in a Titan Engagement the patient must hold your Position. It can be Helped, Pushed, and Covered, and a failure can be tried again on a later turn.

<Subsection title="Care windows" id="care-windows">

Away from a fight, Treat Injury is rolled only in a **care window** — or as an **aftermath roll** when a fight ends. Each window has a scope: the soldiers who take part.

<CoreTable table="care-windows" />

Every living soldier in the scope who is not Down may make **one roll**, for any use and patient the window allows, with no Position requirement. **Help** comes from someone in the scope who has not yet rolled or Helped, and spends their own roll; **Covering** is open to anyone who could Help, even one who has rolled, and spends nothing. The players choose the order. **A failed roll waits for a later window.**

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Aftermath rolls" id="aftermath-rolls">

When a fight ends, each dying soldier gets **one** chance before the end-of-fight Death Rolls.

- **Patient:** anyone holding an untreated lethal wound that runs out now.
- **Treater:** one per patient — a soldier who is not Down and held the patient's Position or one step from it as the fight ended, or the patient treating themselves at the self-treatment penalty. In a Skirmish everyone who took part counts as sharing the Position.
- **A failure uses up the patient's attempt.** No second treater, and no other wound of theirs, reopens it.
- **Nobody Helps or Covers.** It can be Pushed, and it does not use up a roll in the care window that follows.

<MarginNote>Delaying the end of a fight buys your medic another roll and buys the Titans another card. That trade is yours to make.</MarginNote>

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Healing time" id="healing-time">

Healing is counted in **days**. A day passes at each Night Camp, on each of Downtime's seven infirmary days, and otherwise at the start of each session. **No day passes inside a Titan Engagement.**

Each time a day passes, in this order:

<ol>{dayStepsList().map((step) => (<li>{step}</li>))}</ol>

- **A lethal wound cannot heal while it is still lethal.** Its time stops at 1 day until <GlossaryTerm term="Stabilized">stabilized</GlossaryTerm>.
- **An untreated Pierce from the 7 row up never heals** until a Treat Injury roll succeeds on it. Death Rolls stabilize but do not treat.
- **A Burn's days are doubled** by its own rider.
- **When it heals** its effects end and an untreated one gives back its box. Permanent effects stay, and go on counting toward worsening.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="stress-responses-and-rally" eyebrow="Section 5">Stress Responses and Rally</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="From a Cadet's diary">I heard the order. I could not make my hands agree to it.</FieldQuote>

**<GlossaryTerm term="Resolve" />** is half of Instinct plus Empathy, rounded up, **+1 per Scar** and **−1 per point of Grief**. It counts against both rolls below and has no floor — enough Grief and it goes negative, raising both totals.

<MindPaths fig="Fig. 3" />

<Subsection title="The Stress Response" id="the-stress-response">

Only a Stress Die showing **1** causes one, and a roll never causes more than one. Resolve it after counting successes and before the roll takes effect: roll **D6 + Stress − Resolve**. No pool, no Push, no Help, no Circumstances.

<CoreTable table="stress-responses" />

- An **instant** result applies once, to the roll that caused it.
- A **lasting** result you hold, and it never touches the roll that caused it. You can hold several different ones.
- **Already hold that row?** Take the next row down, and again if you hold that too.

A lasting result ends when Rally clears it, when the Titan Engagement it was gained in ends, or when a day passes.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Rally" id="rally">

An Empathy action that talks a comrade back. **It never lowers Stress.**

- **Target:** a comrade holding at least one lasting Stress Response or a pending Fear result. They may be Down. Never yourself.
- **Needs 1 success, and each success clears one** result, your choice.
- **In a Titan Engagement** it is an action, and the target must be at your Position or one step away. It can be Helped, and retried on a later turn.
- **Away from one,** any soldier who is not Down may Rally, with no Help and no Covering — once per comrade, until they gain another result.

</Subsection>

<MarginNote>The whole table of Stress gains and losses: <a href="/guide/rules-of-play/#gaining-and-losing-stress">The Rules of Play</a>.</MarginNote>

<SectionHead id="fear" eyebrow="Section 6">Fear</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="A Squad Leader, after the gate">Half the squad saw it happen. That is the part I have to lead tomorrow.</FieldQuote>

A **Fear Roll** comes only from a horrifying event on this closed list. **The dice never cause one.** Roll **D6 + Stress − Resolve** and read the row: no pool, no Push, no Help, no Stress Response.

<CoreTable table="fear-triggers" />

<CoreTable table="fear-rolls" />

Every row is instant, and they escalate: **0 or less** costs nothing; **1–2** cost Stress, a penalty, or a Gas Roll; **3–4** spend your next action; **5–6** spend your next turn; **7 or more** spend it and give a Scar; **8 or more** spend the next two. From 6 up they also forbid Reactions until that turn has been spent, so a card then lands on 1 or more successes.

<Subsection title="Limits and the snapshot" id="fear-limits">

- **One Fear Roll per soldier per event,** however many triggers it matches. A Down soldier makes none, and a death during the steps after a fight causes none.
- **One snapshot per event.** Everyone rolls together, each total read from their Stress, Resolve, and Scars *as they stood when the event resolved*. Find every row, then make every Drive decision, then apply every result. **No result of the event changes another soldier's total for it,** and the order of rolls changes nothing.
- Grief from a death waits until every Fear Roll that death causes has resolved.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The GM's own trigger" id="the-gms-trigger">

One row is the GM's to read, and it is read rarely: an event at least as horrifying as a listed trigger, and not one a listed trigger already covers. **Never inside a Titan Engagement,** where the list alone governs, and never for an outcome a called roll staked. At most one such roll in a scene.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The event's Titan" id="the-events-titan">

An effect that acts on a Titan acts on **the event's Titan** — the one the trigger names. Where the trigger names none, it is the living Focus Titan you hold the closest Position to, earliest label on a tie. If that Titan is dead when the result applies, the nearest takes its place; if none is alive, the effect does nothing.

</Subsection>

<RuleBox title="What a Drive shrugs off" icon="ph:flame">

A Fear Roll's **result** is every effect of its row: Stress to you or to comrades, a spent action or turn, a ban on Reactions, the loudest mark, a forced step, a forced strike, a dropped Blade Set, a Gas Roll, a Scar. A Drive stops all of them. The Fear Roll still counts as made.

</RuleBox>

<SectionHead id="scars-and-grief" eyebrow="Section 7">Scars and Grief</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual, on veterans">The steady ones are not the ones who felt nothing. They are the ones who found where to put it.</FieldQuote>

<Subsection title="Scars" id="gaining-a-scar">

A Scar comes from **two things and nothing else**: a Fear Roll row with a Scar taking effect, and a lethal Critical Injury stabilized while you live. Each wound gives one at most once.

Roll D66 — again if you hold that Scar already. **No rule ever removes one.** Every Scar raises your **minimum Stress by 1** (your Stress rising to meet it at once) and your **Resolve by 1**, so a veteran always rolls Stress Dice and always resists better than a recruit. You hold at most {scarMaximum()}.

<CoreTable table="scars" />

</Subsection>

<InkVignette caption="A row of empty bunks, and one soldier still sitting on the edge of hers." label="A soldier sitting alone on a bunk in an empty barracks" art="guide-wounds-and-fear-grief" />

<Subsection title="Retirement" id="retirement">

**Five Scars and you must retire.** A soldier who has lost both arms or both legs **may**, at the player's choice — offered when the procedure ends and again at every Downtime, and declining is never final.

**<GlossaryTerm term="Retirement" /> never interrupts a procedure:** it happens at the last step of the fight, the day, or the care window it fell due in. Until then the soldier plays as normal. They leave as an instructor or a Squad contact, replaced like any loss, and every Drive that named them can never trigger again. **Retirement gives no Grief and is no Fear Roll trigger.**

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Grief" id="grief">

**Grief** is what a death leaves in the people who were there. Each point lowers your Resolve by 1, with no floor, and it does nothing else. You hold at most {griefMaximum()}.

<FactTable caption="Who gains it, and how much" columns={['', '']} rows={[
  ['Who', 'Every other living soldier who held a Position in that fight, took part in that Skirmish, or — for any other death — is in the Squad.'],
  ['How much', '<b>1 in total</b> per fight, per Skirmish, per day passing, or per event away from a fight, however many comrades died.'],

<FieldQuote cite="A Squad Leader, on the order of things">Nobody is dead until we have counted. Nobody goes home until we have counted twice.</FieldQuote>

<Subsection title="Soldiers who left, and soldiers still Pinned" id="left-and-pinned">

A soldier who left a fight still under way holds no Position, but is still in it for every rule in this chapter: their turns keep coming, so time limits keep running out, and every step above reaches them at the end. For treatment and Rally, **two soldiers who have both left count as sharing a Position** — and neither ever counts as near a soldier still in the fight.

**A fight does not end while a soldier lies Pinned.** Its end frees nobody, and anyone still Pinned when it ends with nobody standing dies under the body, counted for Grief.

</Subsection>

<RuleBox title="Squadmates" icon="ph:users-three">

A <GlossaryTerm term="Squadmate" /> uses every rule here, with three differences: no Drive, so it never shrugs off a Fear result; it never Pushes; and it rolls again on a Scar whose trigger is a Push or a Cover.

</RuleBox>
