<SectionHead id="expeditions" eyebrow="Section 1">Expeditions</SectionHead>

An <GlossaryTerm term="Expedition" /> is a chain of <GlossaryTerm term="Leg">Legs</GlossaryTerm> between <GlossaryTerm term="Waypoint">Waypoints</GlossaryTerm>. **You always reach the next Waypoint.** A failed Leg costs more rations and makes what waits there worse — nothing here stops the ride.

<Subsection title="The route" id="the-route">

Set once, before the Expedition, in the **<GlossaryTerm term="Mission Brief" />** the GM writes as Command, and never changed afterwards. It names the Waypoints in order with their kinds; each Leg's <GlossaryTerm term="Distance Band" /> and <GlossaryTerm term="Formation Post" />; the days Command orders a <GlossaryTerm term="Hard Ride" />; and the <GlossaryTerm term="Abnormal" /> the hazard tables mean.

A Brief holds {bounds.legs} Legs and {bounds.depots} Depot. The first and last Legs are Near, the second and second-to-last Near or Far, and only the ones between can be Deep.

<MarginNote stamp="Read twice">Distance Band, Post, and Pace belong to the route, not the moment. Once you ride out, no call at the table moves them.</MarginNote>

<CoreTable table="waypoint-kinds" />

At a **<GlossaryTerm term="Depot">Depot</GlossaryTerm>**, every soldier receives a full <GlossaryTerm term="Standard Issue" /> and <GlossaryTerm term="Squad Supply" /> is restocked. Reaching the **gate** ends the Expedition and begins <GlossaryTerm term="Downtime" />.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="What you ride out with" id="before-and-during">

A full issue before you leave, and then nothing until the next Depot.

- **Gas is a fight's business.** No <GlossaryTerm term="Gas Roll" /> on a Leg or at camp.
- **What is used up stays used up** — canisters, <GlossaryTerm term="Blade Set">Blade Sets</GlossaryTerm>, supply. The one exception is what a successful called search turns up.
- **In the saddle.** Everyone not <GlossaryTerm term="Down" /> and whose horse is not <GlossaryTerm term="Lame">lame</GlossaryTerm> rides each Leg; everyone dismounts at camp. The rest ride in the wagon.
- **A Leg and a Night Camp each have a start and an end,** so a once-per-fight <GlossaryTerm term="Talent" /> can also be used once in each. A <GlossaryTerm term="Drive">Drive's</GlossaryTerm> turn tests count neither.
- **Promotion and Retirement** wait until the Leg or camp they fell due in has ended.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The day" id="the-day">

Two Legs and a Night Camp — three Legs when Command orders a Hard Ride.

<StepFlow id="fig-day" fig="Fig. 1" title="A day on the route" steps={dayFlow()} />

<CoreTable table="pace" />

Every Leg ridden counts toward the day's Legs, a Leg ridden again after a retreat included.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Formation Posts" id="formation-posts">

The whole Squad rides at one **Post**, the one this Leg's route gives it, so it changes only at a Waypoint. It names the entry the Leg roll uses and what the Leg adds to its hazard.

<CoreTable table="formation-posts" />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Riding a Leg" id="riding-a-leg">

<StepFlow id="fig-leg" fig="Fig. 2" title="One Leg, post to Waypoint" steps={legFlow()} />

**What arriving costs.** A success spends {leg.successRations} ration and takes {leg.successHazard} off the hazard for each success beyond the first. A failure spends {leg.failureRations} and adds {leg.failureHazard}. A Hard Ride multiplies the rations by {leg.hardRideMultiplier}.

**A Titan Engagement a hazard begins** takes its <GlossaryTerm term="Anchor Rating" /> from the Waypoint this Leg leads to, and every living soldier takes part. If it becomes a retreat, the Squad falls back to the Waypoint this Leg started from and rides the Leg again from the beginning, with a fresh roll and a fresh hazard.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The hazard" id="the-hazard">

<MarginNote>What each total brings is the GM's, read from the Commander's copy and never softened or swapped. What the Squad can do about it is on this page.</MarginNote>

The GM rolls the hazard in the open at the end of every Leg, and again at every Night Camp. Both are table rolls: no pool, no <GlossaryTerm term="Circumstances" />, no Help, no Push, never retried.

<CoreTable table="hazard-count" />

**Three of those lines are yours.** Ride a good Leg and the hazard falls; fail one and it climbs; ride the Signal Relay Post and a flare buys 2 of it back, once a Leg, before the roll.

A roll the hazard calls for takes the Circumstances already in force when it was rolled — never a new step for the danger the hazard describes.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Scenes at a Waypoint" id="waypoint-scenes">

Between Legs the GM may frame a scene: an abandoned town searched, survivors found, a flooded ford crossed. It runs on **<GlossaryTerm term="Called roll">called rolls</GlossaryTerm>**, and a fight can start in one.

**A scene rides no Leg.** No Leg roll, no camp roll, no hazard, and no ration unless a stake names one. A retreat from a fight begun in a scene falls back exactly as a hazard's does, and the Leg ridden again takes the day's next slot.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The Straggler" id="the-straggler">

One hazard takes a soldier off the back of the formation, and **the Squad does not choose which** — the pick is a roll.

1. **Which kind.** Roll {straggler.die}: {straggler.squadmate} a <GlossaryTerm term="Squadmate" />, {straggler.character} a player character. If neither kind has anyone, the hazard becomes a plain attack on the formation.
2. **Which soldier.** Number them 1 upward in sheet order and roll {straggler.die}, re-rolling anything above the count.
3. **Endure,** needing 1 success. No Help, no Cover, Push allowed, never retried.
4. **On a failure,** a {straggler.injury} <GlossaryTerm term="Critical Injury" /> at a rolled location, which can be lethal. No <GlossaryTerm term="Attack Dice" />, no <GlossaryTerm term="Reaction" />, and no Net Success rider — and it counts as gained inside the fight that follows.
5. **Then the fight begins,** the victim at In Reach and everyone else at Distant.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The Night Camp" id="the-night-camp">

<StepFlow id="fig-night" fig="Fig. 3" title="A Night Camp, watch to dawn" steps={nightFlow()} />

**The camp roll** is Survive by one soldier not Down, needing 1 success — Help from up to 3, Push and Cover allowed, never retried, and an automatic failure if nobody can roll.

**<GlossaryTerm term="Camp Relief" />:** a successful camp roll with no hunger takes **1 <GlossaryTerm term="Stress" />** off every soldier. **The night** counts the day's last Distance Band, plus {leg.campRollFailed} if the camp roll failed. Titans do not walk at night — the only one that comes is an Abnormal.

**A day passes** at the end: a care window with <GlossaryTerm term="Field Repair" /> among its rolls, the <GlossaryTerm term="Death Roll">Death Rolls</GlossaryTerm> due, all damage healed, and a day of healing.

**A retreat at night** falls back overnight to where the day's last Leg started, and the camp goes on there — no new camp roll, ration, or night roll, and it still counts as the night. The morning rides that Leg again from the beginning.

</Subsection>

<RuleBox title="Hunger" icon="ph:bowl-food">

When a Leg or a camp must spend rations the Squad does not hold, **it spends everything it has and every soldier gains 1 Stress**, with no roll. A camp that meets <GlossaryTerm term="Hunger">hunger</GlossaryTerm> gives no Camp Relief.

</RuleBox>

<SectionHead id="downtime" eyebrow="Section 2">Downtime</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="A quartermaster's clerk, on the returning column">They come back thinner than they went out. We count the horses first, because the horses are easier to count.</FieldQuote>

**Downtime** runs from the gate that ends an Expedition until the next one begins. It spans **{days} days**, which pass at the infirmary; nothing else in it takes days. Every soldier in the Squad takes part.

<StepFlow id="fig-downtime" fig="Fig. 4" title="One Downtime, gate to gate" steps={downtimeFlow()} />

<Subsection title="The infirmary" id="the-infirmary">

First **one care window** with the whole Squad in scope — and no Field Repair in it, because inside the Walls gear is put right by Maintain Gear. Then **{days} days**, each resolved in this order:

<ol>
  {infirmaryDay().map((step) => <li>{step}</li>)}
</ol>

<MarginNote>The infirmary roll is 4 dice and nothing else: no Talent, no Gear, no <GlossaryTerm term="Stress Dice" />, no Bonus Dice, no Push, no Help. It is care, not heroics.</MarginNote>

Those days hold no care window of their own, so the first is Downtime's only one. A death on an infirmary day causes no <GlossaryTerm term="Fear Roll" />.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Downtime Actions" id="downtime-actions">

Each player character takes **one <GlossaryTerm term="Downtime Action" />**, in an order the players choose. Each <GlossaryTerm term="Squadmate" /> takes none and loses {squadmateRelief()} instead. Then the Squad takes **one <GlossaryTerm term="Squad Action" />**.

<SectionHead id="requisition" eyebrow="Section 3">Requisition</SectionHead>

**<GlossaryTerm term="Requisition" />** is how you get gear beyond Standard Issue. There are no prices: <GlossaryTerm term="Funding" /> decides what Command will consider, and the item's <GlossaryTerm term="Scarcity" /> and your roll decide whether you get it. **One per player character per Downtime,** and never before a campaign's first Expedition.

<CoreTable table="requisition-gate" />

<StepFlow id="fig-requisition" fig="Fig. 5" title="One ask, one answer" steps={requisitionFlow()} />

<Subsection title="The roll" id="the-requisition-roll">

Name one row the Funding gate allows and the soldier it is for, then roll **Persuade or Recall** — talking the quartermaster round, or citing the regulation and the officer who signed it. **It needs the row's Scarcity plus the <GlossaryTerm term="Ledger">ledger</GlossaryTerm>.**

Up to 3 comrades may Help: a player character by spending their own Downtime Action on it, a Squadmate for nothing, once a Downtime. Circumstances reach it like any roll — the GM may name a step for leverage you bring — but **no step ever changes what it needs**. Push and Cover as usual. Granted or refused, **that was your one ask**.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The ledger" id="the-ledger">

<BoxPair>
<RuleBox title="Command's patience" icon="ph:notebook">

The ledger starts at **0** each Downtime and counts the Requisitions Command has already granted the Squad. It is added to what the next roll needs.

Ask after three grants and a Limited item needs 2 successes for its Scarcity and **3 more** for the ledger. Order your asks accordingly.

</RuleBox>
<RuleBox title="Size Up on the quartermaster" icon="ph:eye">

Once a Downtime, **before any Requisition roll**, one soldier who is not Down — a Squadmate included — may roll Size Up. It is not a Downtime Action.

Needs 1 success, no Help, no Cover, Push allowed, never retried. Success makes the ledger count **1 lower for the whole Squad** for the rest of the Downtime.

</RuleBox>
</BoxPair>

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="What a grant gives" id="what-a-grant-gives">

An **item** belongs to the named soldier and is a <GlossaryTerm term="Kept item">kept item</GlossaryTerm>, which Standard Issue never takes away unless it is handed in at an exchange. <GlossaryTerm term="ODM Gear" /> or a horse replaces the soldier's own, which leaves play. **Squad Supply** is added above the stock and is not kept.

It arrives at the after-actions step — before prosthetics are fitted, so a limb that heals during the {days} days can take a Requisitioned <GlossaryTerm term="Prosthetic">prosthetic</GlossaryTerm> the same Downtime.

<CoreTable table="requisition-list" />

</Subsection>
