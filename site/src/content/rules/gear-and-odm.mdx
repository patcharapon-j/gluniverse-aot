<SectionHead id="gear-and-gear-dice" eyebrow="Section 1">Gear and Gear Dice</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="A quartermaster, handing over a harness">Signed for. Bring it back or do not come back.</FieldQuote>

A <GlossaryTerm term="Gear item">gear item</GlossaryTerm> is one of the things on this list, and nothing else is: ODM Gear, gas canisters, Blade Sets, horses, medical kits, tool kits, flintlock pistols, muskets, and prosthetic arms and legs.

Any other object you describe, a rope or a lantern or a wheelbarrow, adds no <GlossaryTerm term="Gear Dice" /> and counts as no item. It has a mechanical effect only where a rule names it, as a thrown cloak is named as a <GlossaryTerm term="Decoy">decoy</GlossaryTerm> for <GlossaryTerm term="Break Attention" />. Otherwise it works through <GlossaryTerm term="Circumstances" />: an object used well may count toward the step the GM names, so a rope and a grapnel might make a climb Easy. On a <GlossaryTerm term="Called roll">called roll</GlossaryTerm> made with an attribute alone, even a gear item that helps you counts that way and gives no Gear Dice.

<Subsection title="Ratings" id="ratings">

Every item except a gas canister and a prosthetic is **rated**.

- An item's **rating** runs from 1 to 3 and is set when you receive it. It never changes.
- Its **<GlossaryTerm term="Current rating">current rating</GlossaryTerm>** runs from 0 up to that rating, and it is the only number that moves.
- **The item's Gear Dice are its current rating.** A roll takes Gear Dice from at most one item, chosen before you roll if more than one qualifies.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Wear, and what 0 means" id="wear">

<MarginNote>Pushing, Covering, and the order a Pushed roll is resolved in: <a href="/guide/rules-of-play/#pushing-your-luck">The Rules of Play</a>.</MarginNote>

<StepFlow id="fig-wear" fig="Fig. 1" title="From a Push to a Jam" steps={wearFlow()} />

An item whose current rating is 0 **counts as not had** for every entry in the <GlossaryTerm term="Action Catalog" /> and adds no Gear Dice. Some items count as not had while they are whole, too, and the table gives each case.

<CoreTable table="gear-items" />

No ruling ever wears an item. A called roll's stakes can cost you a carried item, never a point of wear.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Getting it back" id="restoring">

A current rating rises only through a rule the item's own row allows, and never above the rating. There are four:

1. **<GlossaryTerm term="Field Repair" />,** below, for ODM Gear, a tool kit, and a firearm.
2. **Restocking** a medical kit from the Squad's medical supplies, in a care window.
3. **<GlossaryTerm term="Standard Issue" />,** which replaces the item rather than mending it. It never restores a firearm.
4. **The Maintain Gear Downtime Action,** which restores every rated item you hold.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Firearms" id="firearms">

A flintlock pistol and a musket are the gear items for Shoot, and Shoot belongs to <a href="/guide/skirmishes/">Skirmishes</a>. Their damage, their Injury Type, and which Foes you can fire at are Skirmish rules.

- **Received** only through a Requisition, and empty. Standard Issue never gives one and never restores one.
- **Rated** like any rated item, and worn out at 0. Only Field Repair and Maintain Gear bring one back.
- **Loaded or empty,** and your sheet records which. Only Reload loads a firearm, spending 1 unit of shot, and making a Shoot roll empties it. An empty firearm counts as not had, so Shoot cannot be taken with it. Outside a <GlossaryTerm term="Skirmish" /> and a <GlossaryTerm term="Titan Engagement" /> you may load one at any time for that one shot, and it stays loaded until it is fired. In a Skirmish, reloading a musket also spends your move.
- **No use in a Titan Engagement.** Shoot is never taken there, and a firearm gives Gear Dice to no roll made there.
- **Carried** as 1 item for a pistol and 2 for a musket. Either can be passed, taken, or shared out.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Prosthetics" id="prosthetics">

A prosthetic arm or leg is workshop work of the era: a hook or an iron hand on a socket, or a peg or a jointed wooden leg, fitted to one side you have lost.

- **Received** only through a Requisition, at Limited Scarcity, one per Requisition. Standard Issue never gives one and never takes one away.
- **Not rated.** It gives no Gear Dice, never wears, adds no die to any roll, and is never a weapon.
- **Fitted** during a Downtime, once the loss at that side has healed. What it does to the lost-limb grades is in <a href="/guide/wounds-and-fear/">Wounds & Fear</a>.
- **Carried as no item,** and never passed or taken. A fitted prosthetic leaves play with its soldier.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Kept items" id="kept-items">

An item a Requisition gave you is a **<GlossaryTerm term="Kept item">kept item</GlossaryTerm>**, and your sheet records which of yours are kept.

Standard Issue never takes a kept item away unless you hand it in. At any step that would replace it or make it leave play, you may hand it in and take the issue in its place; by default you keep it, exactly as if you had declined that exchange. It stays kept when it is passed, taken, or shared out. In every other way it is an item of its kind: it counts as items, it wears, it is restored as its row states, and a Blade Set rated above 1 is still ruined by any wear.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Field Repair" id="field-repair">

Field Repair is a **Wits** action that takes its Gear Dice from a tool kit. Without one it rolls Wits alone, unless Make Do applies. Gearwright adds dice to it.

- **Name the item before you roll:** {repair.items.slice(0, -1).join(', ')}, or {repair.items[repair.items.length - 1]}. A Blade Set, a horse, and a medical kit cannot be repaired, and the tool kit supplying the roll's Gear Dice is never the item it mends. A tool kit at 0 gives no Gear Dice, so it can be mended with another kit or with Wits alone.
- **It needs {repair.needs} success.** <GlossaryTerm term="Circumstances" /> reach it as they reach any attribute roll: the GM names one step, Standard if none, and a repair in the dark or in a downpour might be Hard. No step changes the {repair.needs} success it needs.
- **On a success,** the item's current rating rises by 1 for each success, up to its rating. ODM Gear raised above 0 is no longer <GlossaryTerm term="Jam">Jammed</GlossaryTerm>. On a failure, nothing happens.

**In a Titan Engagement,** the item is yours or belongs to a comrade at your Position, and two soldiers who have both left the fight count as sharing one Position. It spends your action, it can be Helped, Pushed, and Covered as usual, and a failure can be tried again on a later turn.

**Outside one,** Field Repair is rolled only in a care window held when a Titan Engagement ends, when a day passes during an <GlossaryTerm term="Expedition" />, or on the interim day, and only on an item belonging to a soldier in that window's scope. Restoring gear during <GlossaryTerm term="Downtime" /> belongs to the Maintain Gear Downtime Action instead.

- Each living soldier in the scope who is not <GlossaryTerm term="Down" /> may make **one Field Repair roll** in each such window, separate from their Treat Injury roll.
- Another living soldier in the scope who is not <GlossaryTerm term="Down" /> and is not rolling may **<GlossaryTerm term="Help" />** if they have not yet made or Helped a Field Repair roll in that window. Help is declared before the roll and spends that soldier's own repair for the window. At most 3 soldiers Help one roll.
- Anyone who could Help can **<GlossaryTerm term="Cover">Cover</GlossaryTerm>** the Push, even a soldier who has already rolled or Helped. Covering spends nothing.
- The players choose the order, with the usual roll-off if they disagree, and <GlossaryTerm term="Squadmate">Squadmates</GlossaryTerm> take part like anyone else. A failed roll waits for a later window.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="odm-gear-and-gas" eyebrow="Section 2">ODM Gear and Gas</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Instructor's saying, Training Corps">Gas is time. Spend it like you only have some.</FieldQuote>

<GlossaryTerm term="ODM Gear" /> supplies Gear Dice to four entries: the dodge, Break Attention, Fly, and <GlossaryTerm term="Leap Clear" />. A <GlossaryTerm term="Nape strike" />, a <GlossaryTerm term="Body Part strike" />, and Break Free take theirs from the <GlossaryTerm term="Blade Set" /> in your handles, never from the harness.

<BoxPair>
<RuleBox title="Strikes need a working harness" icon="ph:anchor-simple">

In a Titan Engagement, a Nape strike, and a Body Part strike made from On Body or Blind Spot, need your ODM Gear to count as had: **not Jammed, and <GlossaryTerm term="Gas Rating" /> above 0.** Jammed or dry, you cannot make them.

The strike still takes its Gear Dice from the Blade Set, and it is not ODM use, so it makes no <GlossaryTerm term="Gas Roll" />. Only a rule that names this requirement lifts it, as the <GlossaryTerm term="Grounded">grounded</GlossaryTerm> Titan's does. Outside a Titan Engagement the requirement does not apply at all.

</RuleBox>
<RuleBox title="Airborne" icon="ph:wind">

You are **<GlossaryTerm term="Airborne">airborne</GlossaryTerm>** while you are in the air on your own ODM Gear.

- **You become airborne** by making an ODM move, which in a Titan Engagement is a <GlossaryTerm term="Flight" />, or by a rule that calls for a Fly roll with your own harness and says the roll makes you airborne. Making a called roll does not by itself: that rule says whether a success, a failure, or both do. A Flight always does, however its dice fall.
- **At the Urban rating you are not airborne at Blind Spot.** You are anchored to a roof, so a <GlossaryTerm term="Jam" /> does not drop you.
- **You stop** when you make a move that is not an ODM move, when you fall, when you become <GlossaryTerm term="Grabbed" />, and when the Titan Engagement ends, where everyone still in the air lands with no fall. Outside a fight, you land when the procedure that lifted you ends, unless its rule names another point.
- **You are never airborne** while you are carried, while you are Grabbed, or while you are <GlossaryTerm term="Mounted">mounted</GlossaryTerm>. Running out of gas in the air does not bring you down.

</RuleBox>
</BoxPair>

<Subsection title="ODM moves" id="odm-moves">

An **<GlossaryTerm term="ODM move">ODM move</GlossaryTerm>** is a move you make on your own ODM Gear. Which Position changes need one, and which can be made on foot or mounted, is set by the <GlossaryTerm term="Anchor Rating" /> in <a href="/guide/fighting-titans/#positions-and-moves">Fighting Titans</a>.

- **In a Titan Engagement every ODM move is a Flight:** you roll Fly with this harness, the step happens whatever the roll gives, each success is 1 <GlossaryTerm term="Momentum" />, and no successes leaves you marked loudest. The rule is in <a href="/guide/fighting-titans/#the-flight">Fighting Titans</a>.
- It needs ODM Gear that counts as had, so Jammed or dry you cannot make one.
- A mounted soldier's ODM move dismounts them before it moves them.
- While <GlossaryTerm term="Overloaded" />, an ODM move also spends your action, and cannot be made once that action is spent.
- A soldier being carried makes none.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Using ODM Gear" id="odm-use">

You **use ODM Gear** in a round if, during that round, you make an ODM move, make a roll whose gear item is your own harness, or take an act a rule names as ODM use. That is what the Gas Roll asks.

Holding a Position without moving is not ODM use, even while airborne. Neither is a mounted charge, a roll made with a Blade Set, a horse, or a kit, every Nape strike included, nor being carried.

A Flight is ODM use twice over, since it is an ODM move and a roll with this harness, and it still makes **one** Gas Roll for the round, as any number of uses does. Momentum spent on a clean line makes none at all that round.

Outside a Titan Engagement there are no rounds, and ODM use there makes a Gas Roll only where the rule that calls for it says so. Travelling on horseback never spends gas, and the <GlossaryTerm term="Graduation Exam" /> makes no Gas Rolls at all.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Gas and the Gas Roll" id="the-gas-roll">

Your **Gas Rating** is the gas left in the canister fitted to your harness, from 0 to the {gas.full} a full canister holds. With no canister fitted it is 0. You can carry **spare canisters** too, each recording its own Gas Rating; one from Standard Issue is full, and a canister at 0 is discarded as it comes off, so a carried spare always has gas in it.

<StepFlow id="fig-gas" fig="Fig. 2" title="The Gas Roll, and running dry" steps={gasRollFlow()} />

A <GlossaryTerm term="Squadmate" /> never Pushes, so its Gas Roll is always {gas.dice} dice and its harness never Jams from wear.

**A Gas Roll a Fear Roll calls for.** A <GlossaryTerm term="Fear Roll" /> result can call for a Gas Roll at once. It is {gas.dice} dice on the canister fitted to your harness, read and applied as any other, and nothing happens if you have none fitted or it is already empty. It is not the round's Gas Roll and it is not ODM use, so you still make the round's own roll if you used the harness, with the dice that roll would have had.

**A canister about to change hands.** No canister moves before the gas it owes is rolled.

1. If you used ODM Gear this round and have not yet made that round's Gas Roll, you make it before your fitted canister can be passed or taken: when you declare that you mean to pass it, or, if you are Down, when a comrade declares that they mean to take it. It has the dice your rolls so far this round give it, and it is your Gas Roll for the whole round, whatever canister you fit later.
2. **If that roll empties the canister,** it is discarded. You have none fitted, nothing moves, and the pass or take is not made and spends nothing, so whoever declared it may still take an action that turn. If gas remains, the canister moves with its Gas Rating and the pass or take spends what it spends.
3. A soldier who dies after using ODM Gear this round makes that Gas Roll at once, before their fitted canister becomes a left item. A canister it empties is discarded and is never left.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Change Canister" id="change-canister">

**Change Canister** is not rolled, so it is never Helped or Covered. You need a spare in hand. In a Titan Engagement it spends your action, and Quick Refit lets you spend your move instead; outside one it spends nothing. You fit one of your spares and its Gas Rating becomes yours, and the canister you take off is kept as a spare unless it is empty. You can do it airborne.

</Subsection>

<MarginNote stamp="Read twice">Dry is not the same as Jammed. Dry, the harness is whole and one canister fixes it. Jammed, the harness is broken and only a repair or a new one will do.</MarginNote>

<Subsection title="Running dry and the Jam" id="running-dry-and-the-jam">

At **Gas Rating 0 you have run dry**, and at **current rating 0 your ODM Gear Jams**. Both make the harness count as not had, so neither lets you make an ODM move, a Nape strike, or a Body Part strike from On Body or Blind Spot, and neither adds a Gear Die. They part company here:

- **Running dry does not drop you.** Airborne, you hang at your Position until something else takes you out of the air. It ends the moment your Gas Rating rises above 0: Change Canister, Standard Issue, or taking a canister from someone and changing to it.
- **A Jam drops you.** If you are airborne when it Jams, you fall once the roll that Jammed it is finished. At the Urban rating a soldier at Blind Spot is anchored to a roof and is not airborne, so a Jam leaves them standing there. Your Gas Rating does not change, you still make the Gas Roll for the round it Jammed in, and none while it stays Jammed. It ends when Field Repair or a Standard Issue harness lifts the current rating above 0.

The Graduation Exam's training harness carries no canister, so it never runs dry.

</Subsection>

He is not mounted, so he dodges with the harness: 3 base dice, 1 Gear Die, 1 <GlossaryTerm term="Stress Dice">Stress Die</GlossaryTerm>.

<SectionHead id="blades" eyebrow="Section 3">Blades</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="A Squad Leader, on the rack">Two in the handles, the rest on your back. Count them before you count anything else.</FieldQuote>

A **Blade Set** is one pair of ODM blades, and the only unit in which blades are carried, counted, passed, and lost. Each set keeps its own rating.

<Subsection title="The handles" id="the-handles">

Your handles hold **at most one Blade Set**, the set in use.

- Only the set in your handles supplies Gear Dice, or counts as had, for the Nape strike, the Body Part strike, Break Free, Block, and Fight. Every other set you carry counts as not had until it is swapped in, so with empty handles you cannot make a Nape strike at all.
- When a Titan Engagement begins, empty handles take one of your carried sets.
- The set in your handles counts as no item. Every other Blade Set counts as 1.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Ruin" id="ruin">

When a Pushed roll whose gear item is the set in your handles is final, and at least 1 point of wear is left after Blade Discipline, **that Blade Set is ruined**. It is discarded at once and your handles are empty. A Blade Set's current rating never falls: ruin takes the place of wear, so a higher rating buys more dice and no more safety. Otherwise a set is lost only when it is dropped, passed, or named by a rule.

A Fear Roll result can drop the set in your handles. Your handles are empty, the set lies at your Position, nobody picks it up during the fight, and it is shared out with the other left items when the fight ends.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The swap" id="the-swap">

With empty handles, fit one carried Blade Set into them.

- **In a Titan Engagement,** once during each of your own turns, at any point in that turn, **spending your move.** It never spends your action, and a move can change no Position, so you refit where you stand and still strike this turn. What a ruined set costs you is the turn's <GlossaryTerm term="Flight" />, and the Momentum it would have earned. A turn spent in advance still happens, so you can swap during it, spending that turn's move.
- **Outside one,** at any time, as often as you like.
- A Down soldier cannot swap. A Squadmate swaps exactly as you do.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="horses" eyebrow="Section 4">Horses</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="From a Cadet's diary">They tell you the horse is issued to you. Nobody tells you it will know your whistle before you know its name.</FieldQuote>

Your horse is the one your sheet records, from Standard Issue, and **you can mount only your own horse**.

<Subsection title="Where the horse is" id="where-the-horse-is">

In a Titan Engagement a horse holds a <GlossaryTerm term="Position" /> of its own. While you are mounted it holds yours and moves with you. Once you dismount, it stays where you left it until you mount it again, and your sheet records that Position with the <GlossaryTerm term="Focus Titan" /> it is held relative to. A horse sent out as a riderless-horse decoy and taken leaves the fight, and holds no Position, gives no Gear Dice, and cannot be mounted until the fight ends. Horse Whistle is the one Talent that moves a horse, bringing it to your Position before a move that mounts it.

When the Titan Engagement ends, no horse holds a Position any longer. Each living soldier's horse is theirs again, lame or not, the decoy included; whoever was mounted stays mounted, and whoever was not stays dismounted. Outside a fight no horse holds a Position.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Mounting and dismounting" id="mounting">

You are **mounted** while you are riding your own horse, and a mounted soldier is never airborne. Neither mounting nor dismounting is rolled.

- **In a Titan Engagement** both happen as part of a move, one or the other, at the Position you hold before or after that move's change of Position. To mount, your horse must hold the Position you hold, compared relative to the Focus Titan recorded with it, and must not be lame, and you must not be airborne, Down, Grabbed, or carried. When you dismount, the horse stays at the Position you hold.
- **Outside one** there are no moves, except in a Skirmish, where a move may include one mount or dismount. You mount or dismount when the rule for the procedure under way says so. If none says anything, you may do either at any time, as long as the horse is yours and not lame and you are not airborne, Down, or carried.
- **A mounted soldier's ODM move dismounts them first,** and the horse stays behind. It is a Flight from there, as any ODM move is.
- **The charge.** A mounted step between Distant and In Reach may also mark you loudest on that Focus Titan. It is never rolled, spends nothing beyond the move, and is not ODM use, so it makes no Gas Roll. At the Open rating that step may be made **twice** in one move, in either order, so a rider can come in and get out again. It is still one charge, and marks you loudest at most once.
- **Made airborne from the saddle,** by an ODM move or by a Fly roll whose rule says so, you are dismounted first, with no fall, and the horse stays at your Position.
- **A soldier who has lost both arms** mounts and dismounts only with a comrade's help.

**Forced dismounts.** Your horse going lame, or you going Down, dismounts you and you fall from a horse. Being Grabbed dismounts you with no fall. The horse stays at the Position each time.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="What a horse rates" id="what-a-horse-rates">

<MarginNote>A Funding 3 horse and a Funding 3 harness are both rated 2, so a mounted dodge rolls the same pool either way. What differs is the bill: wear and a fall against gas and a Jam.</MarginNote>

- **The dodge, Ride, and Leap Clear,** only while you are mounted on it. Horsemanship adds its dice to that dodge, and Ride is the Leg roll on a Hard Ride.
- **Break Attention,** while you are mounted on it or while it holds the Position you hold, compared relative to the Focus Titan recorded with its Position.
- Otherwise the horse counts as not had for that entry.

Mounted, you may take a dodge's or a Break Attention's Gear Dice from your ODM Gear instead, one item per roll. That roll is ODM use, and it does not make you airborne. A Pushed roll made with the horse wears **the horse**, not your harness, and does not by itself make your Gas Roll three dice. Sure Seat can ignore 1 point of that wear.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Lame" id="lame">

A horse worn to 0 is **<GlossaryTerm term="Lame">lame</GlossaryTerm>**. It cannot be mounted, adds no Gear Dice, and counts as not had for every entry, and a soldier mounted on it is dismounted and falls from a horse. Standard Issue exchanges a lame horse unless you decline the exchange, and Maintain Gear ends lameness. Nothing else restores a horse: Field Repair cannot mend one. On an Expedition, every soldier who is not Down and whose horse is not lame is mounted on each Leg and dismounted at each <GlossaryTerm term="Night Camp" />.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="falling" eyebrow="Section 5">Falling</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual, on the ground">The ground has killed more of us than the hands have. It simply gets less credit.</FieldQuote>

A fall inflicts **damage**, and it is never a Titan attack.

<Subsection title="When you fall" id="when-you-fall">

You fall only for one of these reasons, and no other:

- **Your ODM Gear Jams while you are airborne,** once the roll that Jammed it is finished. At Urban you are not airborne at Blind Spot, so the Jam leaves you on the roof.
- **You become Down while airborne,** once the harm that made you Down, and any <GlossaryTerm term="Critical Injury" /> it gave, is fully resolved.
- **You become Down while mounted,** a fall from a horse, once that harm is resolved.
- **Your horse goes lame while you are mounted,** a fall from a horse, once the roll that wore it is finished.
- **The comrade carrying you falls,** for any reason at all, or is Grabbed while airborne, or dies while airborne or mounted. You fall from their band, at the same moment.
- **A rule or a ruling names it.** Another rule names a fall and its band. A called roll's stakes can name one too: the GM names low or high before the dice, never extreme, in a Titan Engagement as well as outside one, and low if no band is named. The GM states your current Health with those stakes.

A <GlossaryTerm term="Grabbed">Grab's</GlossaryTerm> hold ends the airborne and mounted states before its crush, so a soldier that crush puts Down meets neither Down trigger. Running dry, landing through a move, and being set down are never falls. **A fall never causes a second fall** for the same soldier.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="After the fall" id="after-the-fall">

Which Position a fall leaves you at is in <a href="/guide/fighting-titans/#where-a-fall-lands">Fighting Titans</a>. A fall changes no rating, no Gas Rating, and no item you carry. It is not a behavior, so no <GlossaryTerm term="Reaction" /> answers it, and it is not a Fear Roll trigger.

<SectionHead id="carrying" eyebrow="Section 6">Carrying</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="A Squad Leader, before a sortie">Everything you take, you carry. Everyone you take, you carry too.</FieldQuote>

You can carry **{carryingLimit()} items** before you are <GlossaryTerm term="Overloaded" />. Attributes never rise, so the limit never changes.

<CoreTable table="carrying" />

<Subsection title="Overloaded" id="overloaded">

While you carry more items than your limit, **every ODM move you make also spends your action**, and you cannot make one at all once that action is spent. Nothing else changes: no roll, no Gas Roll, no Reaction, and no move that is not an ODM move. Outside a Titan Engagement being Overloaded does nothing unless the rule for the procedure under way says otherwise.

A soldier who has lost one leg carries the same weight on every move on foot: each one spends their action as well, and none can be made once the action is spent, with the same exception outside a fight.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Shedding load" id="shedding-load">

You can **drop** any carried item that counts as 1 or more items, other than a comrade, and a dropped item leaves play. You can **set down** a comrade you carry, who then holds your Position, and a carried soldier who is not Down can **stop being carried**, holding their carrier's Position.

In a Titan Engagement you shed load at any point during your own turn, as often as you like, spending neither your move nor your action. Outside one, at any time. A Down soldier cannot shed load, an airborne carrier cannot set anyone down, and nobody leaves an airborne carrier.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Carrying a comrade" id="lift-comrade">

**Lift Comrade** is not rolled. In a Titan Engagement it spends your action, or happens as part of your move with Rescue Ride while mounted; outside one it spends nothing.

- **The comrade** holds your Position and is Down, or is not Down, consents, and is forbidden to move by the both-legs grade. They must not be Grabbed, <GlossaryTerm term="Pinned" />, or already carried, and a Pinned soldier is never lifted.
- **You** must not be Down, must carry no other comrade, and must not be carried yourself.
- **Outside a Titan Engagement,** the comrade takes part in the same procedure as you rather than holding your Position: the soldiers that procedure's rule names, or every soldier of the Squad on the same Expedition, or every soldier of the Squad when none is under way.

**While you carry them,** they hold your Position and move when your move moves you. They are never airborne on their own harness, and being carried is not ODM use. Their turns still happen: their move changes nothing, and a carried comrade who is not Down keeps their action for entries that need no move. They count against your limit as 5 items plus every item they carry, and those items stay theirs, counting against their own limit too; only they can drop or pass them, though while they are Down anyone at that Position, you included, can take one. Strong Back removes the 5 and not their items. You carry at most one comrade, a carried soldier carries nobody, and carrying goes on after the fight ends.

**Carrying ends** when you set them down or they leave; when you go Down while neither airborne nor mounted, setting them down at your Position; when you go Down airborne or mounted, where carrying lasts until your own fall lets go of everything and they fall with you from the same band; when you are Grabbed, setting them down at your Position, and they fall if you were airborne; when you fall for any other reason, and they fall with you; when you die, setting them down at the Position you held, and they fall if you were airborne or mounted; when you retire, with no fall; when they are Grabbed, with no fall; when you are Pinned, where they are Pinned too, each with your own crushing Critical Injury at your own rolled location and your own pinning Body Part; and when they die. Outside a Titan Engagement nobody holds a Position, so a comrade set down there holds none.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Passing and taking" id="passing-and-taking">

<CoreTable table="passing-items" />

**Passing** an item spends your action in a Titan Engagement and nothing outside one. You must not be Down, and the receiver must be a living comrade at your Position, who may themselves be Down or Grabbed. Two soldiers who have both left the fight count as sharing one Position. Outside a fight the receiver takes part in the same procedure as you, as for Lift Comrade. One item moves, and from then on it counts against the receiver's limit, and against their carrier's too if they are carried. No rule lets you pass an item to a comrade at another Position.

**Taking** an item spends your action in a Titan Engagement and nothing outside one, and you must not be Down. You can take one item from a comrade who is **Down and not Grabbed**, anything they could have passed you themselves, or, in a Titan Engagement only, one of a dead comrade's left items where they lie. Both need you at the same Position. A fitted canister you take leaves them at Gas Rating 0 with nothing fitted and reaches you as a spare. Outside a fight, left items are never taken: they are shared out. When the players disagree about who takes what, or in what order, they use the usual roll-off, and the Down comrade's player has no veto.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="When a soldier dies or retires" id="death-and-retirement">

A dead soldier's **ODM Gear, horse, and fitted prosthetics leave play**. Everything else is left for the Squad: spare canisters, the fitted canister as a spare if it still has gas, every Blade Set including the one in the handles, every kit, and every firearm.

- **Where they lie.** A soldier who dies in a Titan Engagement while holding a Position and not Grabbed leaves their items at that Position until the fight ends, recorded with the Focus Titan it is held relative to. Otherwise the items lie at no Position.
- **During the fight,** a left item can be taken only with Take Item, by a soldier at the Position where it lies.
- **Sharing out.** When the procedure the death happened in ends, at the moment promotion is timed and before any promotion, the players give each item not already taken to a living soldier who took part. For a Titan Engagement that means anyone who held a Position at any point during it. They choose together, with the usual roll-off. Every item nobody takes leaves play.
- **Left behind.** The items of a soldier who dies left behind when a Titan Engagement ends are never shared out: they leave play with the ground they lie on. An item that soldier passed to a comrade before the ending is the comrade's and is untouched.
- **Retirement** takes a soldier's gear out of play with them. A promoted Squadmate keeps its own.

A soldier who dies or retires while carried stops being carried, and a carrier who dies or retires sets their comrade down. Squad Supply belongs to the Squad, so no death and no Retirement takes any of it away.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Standard Issue" id="what-you-are-issued">

**Medical supplies** have two uses, both outside a Titan Engagement. <RuleBox title="What a ruling can do to gear and supply" icon="ph:gavel">

The GM rules on gear only before the dice, and only in these ways.

- **A called roll's stakes** may name a fall, low or high as the GM names and never extreme, or low if none is named; or one carried item lost; or 1 unit of one kind of Squad Supply spent.
- **A successful called search** of a place the GM named with the stakes may find Squad Supply: at most **1 unit per success**, of one kind that place could plausibly hold. A place yields Squad Supply to **one** called search only, whatever that search rolls, and everyone else present searches it by Helping that roll. A place is one building, wagon, cache, or the like the Squad can search in one go. A Waypoint scene yields to **at most two** called searches however many places it holds. No called search happens in a Titan Engagement or a Skirmish, so a place at hand is searched once the fighting stops.
- **An object that is not a gear item** may count toward the Circumstances the GM names for a roll, and Field Repair takes Circumstances like any attribute roll.

Everything else is applied as written: wear, which only a Pushed Gear Die showing 1 causes; Gear Dice, which only a rated gear item gives; what an object counts as for carrying; the Gas Roll and the fall roll, neither of which takes Circumstances; the fall bands and tables; Standard Issue and when it arrives; and every other spend and restock of Squad Supply. **No ruling ever hands out a gear item.** One comes only from Standard Issue, a Requisition, or a rule that moves an item between soldiers.

</RuleBox>
