<SectionHead id="dice-and-successes" eyebrow="Section 1">Dice and Successes</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual, first page">A soldier does not argue with the dice. A soldier decides what to do about them.</FieldQuote>

Every roll works the same way: build a pool of six-sided dice, roll it, and count the sixes.

<Subsection title="The dice are final" id="the-dice-are-final">

1. **Roll only when a rule says so:** when you take an action whose <GlossaryTerm term="Action Catalog" /> entry is rolled, or when a rule or table result calls for a roll.
2. **Results stand.** The GM never overrides a die or table result, never has it rolled again, and never ignores it. A roll changes only through a <GlossaryTerm term="Push" /> or a rule that names the change.
3. **No dice by judgment.** The GM never adds or removes dice for how hard a task looks. Dice enter or leave a pool only through the pool steps, <GlossaryTerm term="Bonus Dice" /> sources, and penalties in this chapter, or through a rule that names them.
4. **Trying again.** In a <GlossaryTerm term="Titan Engagement" />, you can attempt the same action on a later turn. Outside one, the rule that called for the roll says whether and when you can try again. If it says nothing, the roll cannot be tried again unless that rule calls for it again.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Dice" note="Six sides, four kinds" id="dice">

- Every die has six sides. **D6** means roll one die. **xD6** means roll x dice and add them together: only table results and other rolls that add dice up use it, never a dice pool. **D66** means roll two dice, reading the first as tens and the second as ones, for a result from 11 to 66.
- A dice pool holds three kinds of die: <GlossaryTerm term="base dice" />, <GlossaryTerm term="Gear Dice" />, and <GlossaryTerm term="Stress Dice" />. Give each kind its own colour so they stay apart during a Push. Bonus Dice are base dice.
- **Every 6 is a success,** whatever kind of die shows it.
- A Titan's attack rolls <GlossaryTerm term="Titan Dice" />. They belong to no pool, succeed on a 5 or 6, never Push, and never take Bonus Dice, Help, Gear Dice, or Stress Dice.

<DiceKey />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Attribute rolls" id="attribute-rolls">

A roll is an **attribute roll** if its rule names an attribute. Rolled actions, <GlossaryTerm term="Reactions" />, <GlossaryTerm term="Death Roll">Death Rolls</GlossaryTerm> (which name Strength), and attribute rolls a rule calls for are all attribute rolls. Only attribute rolls use a dice pool, count successes, and can be Pushed.

Other rolls, such as a <GlossaryTerm term="Gas Roll" /> or a roll on a table, roll exactly the dice their rule states. They add nothing to those dice and cannot be Pushed.

</Subsection>

What each step needs beyond the drawing:

- **Name the roll.** Every attribute roll is made for one Action Catalog entry, unless a rule calls for an attribute directly (below). Most entries are actions. A few are rolls that are not actions, and the Catalog marks them: the dodge, the block, and the Death Roll are never taken as actions, but a <GlossaryTerm term="Talent" /> can name them like any other entry.
- **Talent.** A roll can use at most one Talent that adds dice, and only one that names the roll's entry. If the Talent sets a condition for that entry, its dice join only a roll that meets it: Horsemanship adds its dice to a dodge only when you are mounted and dodge with the horse's Gear Dice. Talents that bend a rule instead of adding dice apply whenever their trigger happens, and stack with each other and with the dice-adding Talent.
- **Penalties.** Every penalty comes from a named rule, such as a result on a table in <a href="/guide/wounds-and-fear/">Wounds & Fear</a>, and applies after the Bonus Dice cap.
- **Gear Dice.** An item worn down to 0 adds no dice. If more than one item qualifies, choose one before rolling. If the entry allows no gear item, the roll has no Gear Dice.
- **Stress Dice.** They are part of every attribute roll except the rolls on the table below.

<MarginNote>Talents and the Catalog in full: <a href="/guide/making-your-soldier/">Making Your Soldier</a>.</MarginNote>

<BoxPair>
<RuleBox title="Rolls called by attribute" icon="ph:user-focus">

A rule can call for an attribute roll without naming a Catalog entry. That roll uses the attribute alone: no Talent dice, since an untrained soldier still rolls their full attribute. Every other step still applies: Bonus Dice from any source that covers the roll, Gear Dice only from a gear item the calling rule names, and Stress Dice.

</RuleBox>
<RuleBox title="Actions outside the Catalog" icon="ph:question">

An action with no Catalog entry can change only one of the values the rules name, such as your Position or the dice on a comrade's roll. You name the value. The action then uses a Catalog entry that changes it, or, where the rule that creates the value names an attribute roll, that attribute alone. An entry used this way is that entry for every purpose: its attribute, its gear, and any Talent that names it. An action that changes no such value has no effect on play.

</RuleBox>
</BoxPair>

The Death Roll has no Stress Dice, so it never causes a <GlossaryTerm term="Stress Response" />, and it cannot be Helped. A roll leaves out part of the pool only when its own rule says so and it appears on this table. A roll's own procedure can still forbid Help or a Push without appearing here, as the Graduation Exam's Trials do.

<CoreTable table="roll-exceptions" />

<Subsection title="Counting successes" id="counting-successes">

1. The roll is final once you have stopped Pushing, or cannot Push. Count every die showing a 6: each is one success.
2. The roll succeeds if it has at least as many successes as it needs. A roll needs **1 success** unless its rule names another number: a Nape strike, for one, needs the Titan's <GlossaryTerm term="Nape Depth" />. A Reaction needs no number of its own, because its successes cancel an attack's. A failed roll does only what its rule states for a failure. If the rule states nothing, the failure has no effect.
3. Successes beyond what the roll needs do only what that roll's rule lists. Some rules add successes up across several rolls instead, such as <GlossaryTerm term="Toughness" />.
4. **Each success is used once.** A success that counts toward what the roll needs cannot also pay for another effect. The one exception is a Reaction, whose successes cancel against several attacks from the same Titan or <GlossaryTerm term="Foe" /> in the same round.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="pushing-your-luck" eyebrow="Section 2">Pushing Your Luck</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual">Every Cadet learns to count their gas. Every veteran learns when to spend it anyway.</FieldQuote>

<MarginNote stamp="Read twice">You can Push a success too. One 6 will not reach a Nape Depth of 4.</MarginNote>

A Push trades <GlossaryTerm term="Stress" />, and sometimes wear on your gear, for a second chance at successes. You can Push after a failed roll or after a successful one.

<Subsection title="How a Push works" note="Five steps, in order" id="the-push">

</Subsection>

Bonus Dice are base dice, so a Push rolls them again unless they show a 6. A Gear Die showing 1 stays locked in the pool. The limit of one Stress Response holds however many Stress Dice show a 1, and however many Pushes a Talent allows.

<InkVignette caption="A soldier hangs on taut ODM lines below a Titan's Nape, gas venting from her canister." label="A soldier on ODM lines beneath a Titan's Nape" art="guide-rules-of-play-push" />

<MarginNote>Gas Rolls, wear, Jams, and horses: <a href="/guide/gear-and-odm/">Gear & ODM</a>.</MarginNote>

<BoxPair>
<RuleBox variant="stop" title="You cannot Push when" icon="ph:prohibit">

- any Stress Die showed a 1 on the roll, or after an earlier Push of it;
- the roll's rule forbids a Push, as it does for a Death Roll and the first two Trials of the <GlossaryTerm term="Graduation Exam" />;
- the roll is not an attribute roll;
- you have already Pushed it as many times as you are allowed: once, unless a Talent allows more;
- a state or result you hold forbids it, as being <GlossaryTerm term="Down" /> does.

Each extra Push a Talent allows follows the same five steps.

</RuleBox>
<RuleBox variant="gas" title="Pushing burns gas" icon="ph:wind">

If you Push a roll whose gear item is <GlossaryTerm term="ODM Gear" />, your Gas Roll that round is **three** dice instead of two. It is three however many of that round's rolls you Pushed with ODM Gear. A Push after your Gas Roll for the round has been made does not change it.

A Pushed dodge made with ODM Gear counts, even though a Reaction is not an action. A Pushed dodge made with your horse while mounted does not: its Gear Dice showing 1 wear the horse, not ODM Gear, and it does not by itself make the Gas Roll three dice.

</RuleBox>
</BoxPair>

<Subsection title="Gear Dice and wear" id="gear-dice-and-wear">

If the roll was Pushed, each Gear Die showing a 1 when the roll is final wears the gear item that supplied it by one point. Gear Dice are never rolled again, so these are the 1s from the first roll. A roll that was not Pushed causes no wear, even when Gear Dice show 1. What wear does depends on the item: ODM Gear worn down to 0 <GlossaryTerm term="Jam">Jams</GlossaryTerm>.

</Subsection>

<MarginNote stamp="In order">A Stress Response is resolved before the roll's effect, and wear comes last.</MarginNote>

<Subsection title="Finishing a roll" note="Four steps, in order" id="finishing-a-roll">

When a roll is final, resolve it in this order:

1. **Count successes.**
2. **Resolve any Stress Response** on the table in <a href="/guide/wounds-and-fear/">Wounds & Fear</a>, using your Stress after any Push, unless the roll's own rule names another resolution, as the Graduation Exam does. That roll still has its Stress Response, and still cannot be Pushed after it: only how it is resolved changes. If the result changes the roll or the action, apply that change now. A changed success count is the roll's count for every later use of it, including a Reaction's cancelling against later attacks.
3. **Apply the roll's effect.**
4. **Apply wear** from Gear Dice showing 1, if the roll was Pushed.

</Subsection>

<MarginNote>When a comrade Covers, the Stress is theirs, not yours. Say it out loud before anyone picks up a die.</MarginNote>

<Subsection title="Covering" id="covering">

To <GlossaryTerm term="Cover" /> a comrade is to take on the Stress of their Push.

**Who can Cover.** A soldier who meets all of these:

- They are not the Pushing soldier, and they are not Down.
- They are a player character: a <GlossaryTerm term="Squadmate" /> never Covers.
- In a Titan Engagement, they are at the same <GlossaryTerm term="Position" /> as the Pushing soldier or one Position step away, the same requirement as Help.
- Outside a Titan Engagement, they qualify to Help that roll under the rule that called for it. If that rule allows no Help, no one can Cover the roll.
- No state or result they hold forbids it.

**When.** After the Push is declared and before any die is rolled again. Only one soldier can Cover a Push, and the Pushing soldier can refuse the offer. If more than one comrade offers, the Pushing soldier chooses one. A Push that would roll no die again, because every base die and Stress Die already shows 6, cannot be Covered.

**What it takes.** The Covering soldier gains 1 Stress. Covering is not an action and spends neither the move nor the action. Outside a Titan Engagement it also spends nothing: it needs only the eligibility the calling rule gives Help, not what Help spends.

**Effect.** The Pushing soldier gains no Stress and adds no new Stress Die. Nothing else about the Push changes: their Stress Dice are still rolled again, a Stress Die showing 1 still gives *them* a Stress Response, and their Gear Dice showing 1 still wear their gear. The Covering soldier makes no roll, so Covering never gives them a Stress Response.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="stress" eyebrow="Section 3">Stress</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="From a Cadet's diary">My hands stopped shaking on the third Titan. I think that is worse.</FieldQuote>

Stress is your mounting fear and adrenaline: it makes you more capable and more likely to break.

Every attribute roll gets Stress Dice equal to your current Stress, except a roll with a Stress Dice exception, such as the Death Roll. A Stress Die showing 6 is a success like any other, and a Stress Die showing 1 sets off a Stress Response. Stress carries over between Titan Engagements, <GlossaryTerm term="Expeditions" />, and sessions, and changes only through the triggers on the table below.

<InkVignette caption="A soldier on a rooftop grips her handles in shaking hands as a Titan's shadow crosses the street below." label="A soldier steadying her hands on a rooftop" art="guide-rules-of-play-stress" />

<MarginNote>Scars, Resolve, and retiring: <a href="/guide/wounds-and-fear/">Wounds & Fear</a>.</MarginNote>

<Subsection title="Minimum Stress" id="minimum-stress">

- Your minimum Stress equals your number of <GlossaryTerm term="Scars" />. With no Scars, it is 0.
- Stress never drops below your minimum. Any loss stops there.
- If you gain a Scar while your Stress is below the new minimum, your Stress rises to it immediately.

So a soldier with Scars always rolls at least that many Stress Dice. Each Scar also raises <GlossaryTerm term="Resolve" /> by 1, and Resolve counts against Stress Response and <GlossaryTerm term="Fear Roll" /> results. A soldier with five Scars must retire.

</Subsection>

<MarginNote>Night Camps and Downtime: <a href="/guide/expeditions-and-downtime/">Expeditions & Downtime</a>.</MarginNote>

<Subsection title="Gaining and losing Stress" id="gaining-and-losing-stress">

Stress changes only through a trigger on this table. The GM never raises or lowers it without one.

<CoreTable table="stress-changes" />

- **Both at once.** A Nape kill that also ends the Titan Engagement sets off both losses, and each applies in full.
- **Holding a Position** is the test for being in a Titan Engagement. The loss when it ends goes to every soldier who held a Position at any point, including one who left before the end: a Squad that retreats still gets it, however many rounds its soldiers took to leave. The loss for a Nape kill goes to every soldier who holds a Position at the moment of the kill, not only the one who struck. A soldier who has already left holds no Position and gets nothing from the kill.
- **Rally.** A comrade's <GlossaryTerm term="Rally" /> clears your Stress Response but does not lower your Stress.
- **Downtime.** During <GlossaryTerm term="Downtime" />, your <GlossaryTerm term="Haven" /> lets you lower Stress and <GlossaryTerm term="Grief" /> through <GlossaryTerm term="Downtime Actions" /> such as Visit Haven.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Stress Responses and Fear Rolls" id="stress-responses-and-fear-rolls">

- Only a Stress Die showing 1 triggers a **Stress Response**, and a roll causes at most one. A roll with no Stress Dice, such as a Death Roll, never causes one. The Stress Response table, and how Resolve counts against it, are in Wounds & Fear.
- The dice never trigger a **Fear Roll**. It comes from a horrifying event on the closed list of triggers in Wounds & Fear.

</Subsection>

<SectionHead id="bonus-dice-and-help" eyebrow="Section 4">Bonus Dice and Help</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="A Squad Leader, before the gate opens">Nobody cuts alone. If you can reach them, you can help them.</FieldQuote>

Bonus Dice are extra base dice from Help, Openings, and the situations on this table.

<CoreTable table="bonus-dice" />

1. **Listed sources only.** Nothing that is not on this table adds Bonus Dice.
2. **Declare before rolling.** Every source is declared before the dice are rolled. None can be added afterwards, not even before a Push.
3. **Cap.** A roll gets at most **4** Bonus Dice from all sources together.
4. **Choosing under the cap.** If more Bonus Dice are available than the cap allows, you pick which sources to use. Unused sources are not spent: Openings stay on the Titan, and a comrade whose Help is not used keeps their action.
5. **Bonus Dice are base dice.** They succeed on a 6, a Push rolls them again unless they show a 6, and a 1 on one does nothing.
6. **Penalties come after the cap.**

<InkVignette caption="One soldier braces on a chimney and steadies a comrade's line as she swings in toward the Nape." label="A soldier steadying a comrade's ODM line" art="guide-rules-of-play-help" />

<Subsection title="Help" id="help">

A comrade can <GlossaryTerm term="Help" /> by adding 1 die to your roll. Up to 3 comrades can Help one roll. A **comrade** is any other soldier in the <GlossaryTerm term="Squad" />, player character or Squadmate.

**Requirements.** A comrade can Help only if all of these are true:

- They are not the soldier rolling, and they are not Down.
- They are a player character, or a Squadmate whose action list includes Help.
- The roll allows Help: the table of rolls with exceptions does not rule it out, as it does for the Death Roll and the performance roll, and the roll's own rule does not forbid it.
- They declare the Help before the roll.
- In a Titan Engagement, they have an unspent action this round, and they are at the same Position as the roller or one Position step away. A turn or an action spent in advance, by a Reaction or a result, begins its round spent.
- Outside a Titan Engagement, they meet the requirement stated by the rule that called for the roll.
- No state or result they hold forbids it.

<MarginNote>Position steps for each <GlossaryTerm term="Anchor Rating" />: <a href="/guide/fighting-titans/">Fighting Titans</a>.</MarginNote>

**Effect.** Each helper adds 1 Bonus Die, and Help dice count toward the Bonus Dice cap. The helper does not roll: they cannot Push the roll, gain no Stress from it, and suffer no Stress Response from it.

**What it spends.** In a Titan Engagement, the helper's action. You can Help outside your own turn, such as on a comrade's turn or during a comrade's Reaction, as long as your action is unspent this round. If your own turn comes later in the round, it has only its move.

**Outside a Titan Engagement** there are no Positions and no rounds, so Help is allowed only where the rule that calls for the roll allows it. Every such rule, whether an Action Catalog entry, a Chase, an Expedition rule, or a Downtime rule, states who qualifies to Help and what Help spends. If the rule says nothing about Help, the roll cannot be Helped or Covered. The limits under Effect still apply.

</Subsection>

<FieldReport id="example-emil" title="Emil at the cap">

<SectionHead id="turns-actions-and-reactions" eyebrow="Section 5">Turns, Actions, and Reactions</SectionHead>

<FieldQuote cite="Survey Corps manual">A Titan does not wait its turn. Neither do you.</FieldQuote>

A Titan Engagement runs in rounds, and every soldier's time in it is counted in turns. <a href="/guide/fighting-titans/">Fighting Titans</a> sets the order of play within a round, including initiative cards, <GlossaryTerm term="Tempo" />, and <GlossaryTerm term="Wings" />.

<Subsection title="Turns and actions" id="turns-and-actions">

- **Your turn.** Each round, every player character takes one **turn**, made up of one **move** and one **action**. Fighting Titans says how a turn is ordered and what a move can do. As a baseline, a move changes your Position by one step. Being <GlossaryTerm term="Overloaded" /> makes every ODM move spend your action as well.
- **Actions.** An action is either Help or one Action Catalog entry, other than the entries the Catalog marks as not an action.
- **Once per round.** Your move and your action can each be spent once per round, and cannot be saved for a later round. Both refresh at the start of each round, except a move or action already spent in advance, which begins its round spent.
- **Declining.** You may decline your move, your action, or both when your turn comes up. Whatever is left unspent after your turn stays available until the round ends, but only for Help and Reactions, and a Reaction needs both the move and the action. A move held alone can do nothing once your card has passed. Whatever is held is lost when the round ends.
- **Spending a turn.** When a rule spends one of your turns, such as a Reaction or a result in Wounds & Fear, it spends your earliest turn, starting with this round's, whose move and action are both unspent. If this round's turn has any part spent, the rule spends the next such turn, skipping any turn whose move or action is already spent in advance.
- **Spent at the start of the round.** A turn spent in advance, by a Reaction or a result, begins its round with its move and action already spent. An action a result spends in advance without its move, such as a "next action spent" result, begins its round spent, and the turn keeps its move. Every rule that asks whether your move or action is spent this round reads that state, not what you did during the round. So:
  - if your turn was spent in advance, you cannot Help that round, cannot make a Reaction with that turn, and hold nothing to decline;
  - if only your action was spent in advance, you cannot Help that round or make a Reaction with that turn, and can hold only the move.
- **What can forbid.** A state or result can forbid you to Push, Help, Cover, or make a Reaction only by naming which of the four it forbids. That is the only way a state or result forbids them, and the usual requirements, bans on particular rolls, and Squadmate limits still apply. Down forbids all four. Being <GlossaryTerm term="Grabbed" /> forbids Help, Covering, and Reactions.
- **Not actions:** Reactions, Pushing, Covering, declaring Bonus Dice sources, and spending Openings on the roll they join.

</Subsection>

<InkVignette caption="A Titan's hand sweeps through a courtyard as a soldier fires her anchors and pulls clear." label="A soldier dodging a Titan's sweeping hand" art="guide-rules-of-play-reaction" />

<MarginNote>The Grab and its countdown: <a href="/guide/fighting-titans/">Fighting Titans</a>.</MarginNote>

<Subsection title="Reactions" id="reactions">

A **Reaction** is a dodge or block you make when a Titan or a Foe acts against you, outside your own turn. Against a Titan, that moment is when one of its cards resolves a behavior against you and its <GlossaryTerm term="Attack Dice" /> have been rolled.

- A Reaction is an attribute roll with a full pool, rolled for its own Catalog entry. It can be Helped and Pushed like any other attribute roll.
- A Reaction **spends one whole turn** of yours: both a move and an action.
- **When you can react.** Whether or not you have acted this round, you can make a Reaction if both of these are true:
  - you have not already made a Reaction against that Titan, or that Foe, this round;
  - no state or result you hold forbids it.

  No Reaction is made against an attack that scored 0 successes, so you keep your Reaction for a later attack.
- **Which turn it spends.** Your earliest turn, starting with this round's, whose move and action are both unspent:
  - If neither the move nor the action of this round's turn has been spent, the Reaction spends this round's turn.
  - Otherwise it spends your next turn in full, and whatever is left of this round's turn can still be used on your card, if it has not yet come.
  - If that next turn already has its move or action spent, it spends the turn after, and so on.
  - When a Titan's Grab lands on you, and your dodge against that card, or the earlier dodge whose successes cancelled against that card, spent a turn later than your first counted Grab turn, the Grab gives that later turn back.
  - A turn spent in advance still happens when it comes up, and counts as one of your turns for any rule that counts turns, such as a Grab. It begins its round with no move and no action, so you cannot Help or hold anything that round.
- **When the Titan Engagement ends.** Turns and actions spent in advance, by a Reaction or a result, exist only inside the Titan Engagement where they were spent. When it ends, every one that has not yet come up is cancelled. You owe nothing, and start your next Titan Engagement with every turn unspent.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Attack and Reaction" note="One procedure for every attack" id="attack-and-reaction">

An attack's successes are its <GlossaryTerm term="Severity" />, and what a Reaction leaves of them are its <GlossaryTerm term="Net Successes" />. Every attack a Reaction can answer, from a Titan or from a person, is resolved by this one procedure. Fighting Titans adds only what belongs to Titans, and Skirmishes only what belongs to people.

<BoxPair>
<RuleBox title="Against a Titan" icon="ph:person-simple-run">

The only Reaction is a **dodge**, because Titans cannot be blocked. A dodge is an Agility roll that takes its Gear Dice from ODM Gear, or from your horse while you are mounted, one item per roll. The Titan's attack roll is its behavior's Attack Dice, rolled as Titan Dice.

One Reaction covers every card that Titan plays that round:

- You make at most one Reaction against each Titan per round.
- When one of its cards resolves a behavior against you and scores at least 1 success, you choose whether to dodge. If you don't, every success is net against you, and you can still dodge a later card from that Titan this round.
- Once rolled, the dodge's successes, counted after step 2 of <a href="#finishing-a-roll">Finishing a roll</a>, cancel against the card it answered and, separately, against every later card that Titan resolves against you that round. Each card lands or whiffs on its own Net Successes.
- Cancelling against a later card does not use the dodge's successes up.
- A landed card that inflicts a <GlossaryTerm term="Critical Injury" /> adds 1 to that roll for each Net Success beyond the first. A Grab, a knock loose, and a Stress gain take nothing extra.

</RuleBox>
<RuleBox title="Against a person" icon="ph:sword">

In a <GlossaryTerm term="Skirmish" />, the Reaction is a dodge or a **block**, as the attack allows. Blocking is possible only against an attack whose rules allow it: a Foe's Fight attack in a Skirmish. No Titan allows it.

The Reaction cancels as the procedure states. One Reaction covers every attack that Foe makes against you that round, cancelling against each separately, and a landed attack deals its weapon's damage plus 1 for each Net Success beyond the first.

Skirmishes gives which attacks each Reaction answers, a Foe's Guard, and the ambush.

</RuleBox>
</BoxPair>
