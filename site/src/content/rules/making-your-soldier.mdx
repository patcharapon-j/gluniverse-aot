<SectionHead id="what-a-soldier-is" eyebrow="Section 1">What a Soldier Is</SectionHead>

Every player character is a Training Corps graduate in the Survey Corps. Three procedures make one: the <GlossaryTerm term="Lifepath" />, always allowed, and the <GlossaryTerm term="Template Build" /> and <GlossaryTerm term="Free Build" /> when the campaign allows them.

**Talents are a soldier's only trained abilities.** A roll is an attribute, plus at most one Talent naming the entry, plus <GlossaryTerm term="Bonus Dice" />, <GlossaryTerm term="Gear Dice" />, and <GlossaryTerm term="Stress Dice" />.

<Subsection title="Attributes and derived values" note="Rated 2 to 5" id="attributes">

Attributes are rated **2 to 5**, and only your Specialty's key attribute can reach **6**. **They never rise after creation** — a soldier grows through Talents and Scars.

<Subsection title="Before you roll" id="before-the-lifepath">

Record the <GlossaryTerm term="Campaign Year" /> (845 to 850) if the campaign has none, and vote on whether to run the <GlossaryTerm term="Graduation Exam" />. At session zero the GM also records, once and for good, whether the campaign allows the Template Build, the Free Build, or both, so one table can mix rolled and built soldiers.

**The GM plays no part in the Lifepath** beyond reading tables with you. Every result comes from a roll, a row, or a choice the row offers.

**Shared choices** (the Exam vote, the Campaign Year, how many Squadmates to start with and their templates) are settled by a D6 roll-off between the proposals when players disagree, highest taking it.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<MarginNote>Every row with its full story: <a href="/compendium/origins/">Compendium, Origins & Lifepath</a>.</MarginNote>

<FinePrint title="the Origin and Enlistment rows, and the caps">

<Subsection title="Origin" note="Step 1" id="origin">

Set every attribute to **2**, then roll D66 on the Origin table, re-rolling a row whose condition the Campaign Year does not meet. Add 1 to each of its **two attributes**, take level 1 in one of its **two Talents**, and record one of its **two Havens**: what you have to return to, and what Visit Haven uses to lower your Stress and Grief. A row's <GlossaryTerm term="Canon Tie" /> is optional and changes nothing in play.

<CoreTable table="origins" />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Why You Enlisted" note="Step 2" id="why-you-enlisted">

Roll D66, add 1 to that row's attribute, and record that row's <GlossaryTerm term="Drive" />, or any other row's. A Drive needing a named comrade names one when the Squad forms.

<CoreTable table="why-you-enlisted" />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Training Years" note="Steps 3 to 5" id="training-years">

Each year: roll D66 on its events for **+1 attribute, 1 Talent level, and Merit** (which can be negative), then make the year's **performance roll** for more.

<BoxPair>
<RuleBox title="Attribute cap" icon="ph:user-focus">

**Nothing goes above 5 before Graduation.** A point that would is placed on another attribute below 5, your choice.

</RuleBox>
<RuleBox variant="stop" title="Talent cap" icon="ph:prohibit">

**No Talent above level 2 at creation,** and no rule Talent above its maximum.

- If neither of an event's Talents can take the level, take it in any Talent on that year's curriculum that can.
- If the only one that can take it names only entries no rule calls for, take that level or one in any curriculum Talent that can.

</RuleBox>
</BoxPair>

A Lifepath soldier gains exactly **5 Talent levels**: 1 Origin, 3 events, 1 Specialty.

<Subsection title="The performance roll" id="the-performance-roll">

The higher of the year's two performance attributes, **alone**. No Talent dice, Bonus Dice, Gear Dice, or Stress Dice, no Push, no Help, and never a second attempt.

<SectionHead id="the-graduation-exam" eyebrow="Section 3">The Graduation Exam</SectionHead>

An optional prologue: three <GlossaryTerm term="Trials" /> played out after every Cadet's third-year event and before Graduation. Their Merit replaces the third performance roll, so it helps set each <GlossaryTerm term="Class Rank" />. **Built soldiers take no Trial** and earn no Exam Merit, whatever the vote.

<InkVignette caption="A Cadet swings between pines toward the nape of a wooden Titan dummy running on its rail." label="A Cadet on ODM lines closing on a wooden Titan dummy" art="guide-making-your-soldier-exam" />

<FinePrint title="the Exam's conditions and its three Trials">

<Subsection title="Using the Exam" id="using-the-exam">

- **The vote.** Taken before the Lifepath begins. If the players run the Exam, every Cadet created at the same time takes it; a replacement character decides before rolling their own Lifepath and takes it alone.
- **When.** After every Cadet has applied their third year's event, before Graduation.
- **What it replaces.** The Merit from all three Trials, after Stress Response costs, replaces the third performance roll. The third year's event still happens, with its attribute point, Talent level, and Merit.
- **Built soldiers take no Exam.** They take no Trial and earn no Exam Merit, whatever the vote. Only Cadets rolling the Lifepath count in each Trial's roll order and in the squad field exercise's Help.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="How the Exam is played" id="how-the-exam-is-played">

<FactTable caption="Exam conditions" columns={['', '']} rows={[

  ['Order', 'Rolled, never chosen: a D6 each before every Trial, highest first, ties re-rolled.'],
]} />

<CoreTable table="exam-issue" />

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="The three Trials" id="the-three-trials">

<CoreTable table="exam-trials" />

The ODM balance test and the Titan dummy course cannot be Pushed, Helped, or Covered.

**The squad field exercise** lets each Cadet pick one of six rolls and need **3 successes** for 1 Merit. It can be Pushed, and **Help is fixed**: each Cadet Helps exactly one roll, the one after them in the order, the first Helping the last. Any Cadet who could Help may **Cover** a Push until they have made their own roll. Merit comes only from your own roll.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="drives" eyebrow="Section 4">Drives</SectionHead>

A Drive is what keeps a soldier fighting. When its trigger is met, it lets you **shrug off one <GlossaryTerm term="Fear Roll" /> result per session** — the result simply does not take effect. Every Drive is a Why You Enlisted row, written as a trigger beginning "When I ...".

<FinePrint title="what a Drive's triggers test, and naming a comrade">

<Subsection title="Trigger tests" id="trigger-tests">

Every trigger is met by something you did, or where you put yourself.

<FactTable caption="The three tests" columns={['Test', 'Met when']} rows={[
  ['Your own state', 'You hold the named <b>Position</b> — but only if your own <em>move</em> put you there — or you are airborne on your <em>own</em> ODM Gear. It never looks at Attention, a comrade, or your own harm.'],
  ['Your most recent turn', 'You took one of the named acts on your last turn in this Titan Engagement, this round or an earlier one.'],
  ['Since that turn began', 'You took one at any point from the start of your last turn until now — Help, Cover, a dodge, or a Push on a comrade\'s turn all count.'],
]} />

**Acts** are Catalog entries, plus Pushing a roll. A called roll or an improvised act counts as its named entry; a called roll on a bare attribute is no act.

**Where they work.** The two turn tests need turns, so they work in a Titan Engagement or a <a href="/guide/skirmishes/">Skirmish</a> and nowhere else. Position triggers need a Titan Engagement. A flight trigger works anywhere.

**Whom an act counts on.** Whoever it was used on, unless the row narrows it. A **named comrade** row counts only Help, Cover, or an entry taken on them. A **Grabbed or Down comrade** row counts only an act already used on a comrade who was, or now is, <GlossaryTerm term="Grabbed" /> or Down — Help, Cover, Treat Injury, Lift Comrade, Pry Loose, or a strike on the holding hand. An intention never counts.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Named comrades" id="named-comrades">

A named comrade is any other living soldier in the Squad, chosen when the Drive is recorded. If there is nobody to name, record a Drive that needs none instead.

**If your named comrade dies, retires, or leaves, the Drive can never trigger again.** Promotion does not count as leaving. At the start of the next session, pick a new Drive from any row. No attribute point comes with it.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="specialties-and-talents" eyebrow="Section 5">Specialties and Talents</SectionHead>

<MarginNote>Every Specialty and every Talent in full: <a href="/compendium/specialties/">Compendium, Specialties</a> and <a href="/compendium/talents/">Talents</a>.</MarginNote>

<FinePrint title="what a Specialty grants">

<Subsection title="Specialties" id="specialties">

<CoreTable table="specialties" />

Chosen at Graduation or at a build's Specialty step — not rolled, no requirement, never changed. It grants three things: the **key attribute** (the only one that can reach 6), the Graduation **swap and floor**, and **1 Talent level from its list**, to a maximum of level 2. A built soldier takes the first and last only.

**It never grants permission.** Every soldier can attempt every entry and hold any Talent, including one from another Specialty's list; a soldier without the Talent still rolls their full attribute. The list limits only where that one granted level can go. The **general list** of twelve Talents about temperament and survival is open to anyone, but a Specialty's level never goes into one.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<FinePrint title="how dice Talents and rule Talents apply">

<Subsection title="Talent levels" id="talent-levels">

Talents run **level 0 to 3**, and no higher than **2 at creation**. A rule Talent maxes at level 1 unless it says otherwise. A soldier gains **5 levels** at creation; after that, levels come only from XP and the Train Downtime Action, which these rules do not yet cover.

A Talent's description only sums it up. Its type, maximum level, the entries it names, its condition, and a rule Talent's trigger, effect, and limit are the rule.

</Subsection>

<BoxPair>
<RuleBox title="Dice Talents" icon="ph:dice-five">

Adds **base dice equal to its level** to a roll made for an entry it names.

- **A roll uses at most one.** If several qualify, you pick.
- **Conditions** narrow it: Horsemanship helps a dodge only when mounted with the horse as its gear item; Ground Work only against a grounded Titan.
- A Talent that does not name the roll's entry adds nothing, however close the action looks.
- **No Talent ever changes a step of Circumstances.** One that helps against bad conditions adds dice instead.

</RuleBox>
<RuleBox title="Rule Talents" icon="ph:scroll">

Applies its effect every time its trigger occurs, within its limit, and **stacks** with other rule Talents and with the dice Talent.

- **"You declare" triggers** fire before requirements, targets, pool, and cost are checked — so the effect can change those checks. If the use still does not qualify, nothing is spent.
- It changes **only** what its effect states: no Bonus Dice, no Stress, no extra roll.
- One that changes something the whole Squad shares applies **once** for that use, however many soldiers hold it.

</RuleBox>
</BoxPair>

**Limits.** *Once per Titan Engagement* means once between one fight's start and end, and never outside one — and a Skirmish, <GlossaryTerm term="Leg" />, or <GlossaryTerm term="Night Camp" /> that begins inside another procedure is its own, with its own use. *Once per Leg, Night Camp, Skirmish, or Downtime* works the same way for Talents only those can trigger.

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="actions" eyebrow="Section 6">Actions</SectionHead>

The <GlossaryTerm term="Action Catalog" /> is the complete list of named actions and rolls, and the list Talents name. **Every attribute roll is made for one entry.**

<FinePrint title="the five kinds of entry">

<Subsection title="The five kinds of entry" id="kinds-of-entry">

<FactTable compact columns={['Kind', 'What it is']} rows={[

</Subsection>

<FinePrint title="gear an entry needs, acts outside the Catalog, and improvising">

<Subsection title="Entries that need gear" id="entries-that-need-gear">

With none of an entry's gear items — including an item worn to 0, or <GlossaryTerm term="Jam">Jammed</GlossaryTerm> — the entry says which applies: **attribute alone** (no Talent dice, no Gear Dice, everything else unchanged) or **not possible**. A soldier whose last <GlossaryTerm term="Blade Set" /> is gone cannot make a Nape strike, however it is described; one with no medical kit takes Treat Injury on Wits alone.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Called rolls" id="called-rolls">

An action the steps leave unresolved is the GM's to rule on: it happens, it cannot be done, or it is a **<a href="/guide/rules-of-play/#called-rolls">called roll</a>**.

The entry is the closest of the nine a called roll can use — Spot, Size Up, Survive, Persuade, Endure, Sneak, Recall, Ride, Fly — or a bare attribute when none fits. A bare-attribute call takes no Talent dice and no Gear Dice, wears nothing on a Push, and counts a helpful object toward the Circumstances instead. A call named for **Fly or Ride does** take that entry's Gear Dice.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Improvising in a fight" id="in-a-fight">

In a Titan Engagement or Skirmish the steps above are not used. Your action is Help, an entry taken as written, or an **improvised act** — and which of the three is part of the GM's ruling, made before the pool is built.

**With a model entry.** The GM names the Action you could take there whose effect your act imitates — never a Reaction, roll, or option. The act spends your action, is made exactly as that entry is made, meets every requirement and restriction it carries, and on a success gives **only** that entry's effect at that entry's size.

A supply wagon rolled past a Titan is Break Attention. A lantern swung from a rooftop is Draw Attention, and never from Distant. A chimney dropped on a leg is a Body Part strike. A rope thrown to a comrade is Help or Lift Comrade. An object hurled at a person is a Shoot at damage 1 and Crush. A dive behind a stack is a move, or nothing at all — never a dodge, and never an excuse for an easier one later.

**With no model entry,** and changing nothing the fight tracks, it is a called roll. It spends your action and never gives an effect an entry has.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="squadmates" eyebrow="Section 7">Squadmates</SectionHead>

A <GlossaryTerm term="Squadmate" /> is a named soldier who rides with you. Any player can direct one, Titans target it like anyone else, and it can be promoted into a player character. Those serving now are the <GlossaryTerm term="Squad Pool" />.

Two more soldiers mean two more turns a round and more Help on every strike — so Titans die sooner and you face fewer of their cards. They reach a Grabbed comrade in time, turn a Titan's head, and screen whoever holds its Attention. They are also comrades a Drive can name and a Squad can grieve.

<FinePrint title="the Squadmate stat block, and which rules apply">

<Subsection title="The stat block" id="the-stat-block">

<CoreTable table="squadmate-templates" />

Nine templates, one per Specialty: key attribute 4, four at 3, one at 2 — **18 points**, a new player character's total. One dice Talent at level 1, and Health and Resolve from its ratings.

It records everything a player character does **except** Origin, Haven, Drive, Canon Tie, Merit, Class Rank, and XP. It takes Standard Issue and tracks gear exactly as you do; since **it never Pushes**, Push wear never lowers its ratings.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Which rules apply" id="which-rules-apply">

<CoreTable table="squadmate-rules" />

A Squadmate can take every entry a player character can, meeting the same requirements, and uses the same steps for acts outside the Catalog. Its only Reaction is the dodge. **It never Pushes and never Covers.**

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<MarginNote>Wings in a fight, and Squadmates on no Wing: <a href="/guide/fighting-titans/">Fighting Titans</a>.</MarginNote>

<FinePrint title="Wings, directing, and promotion">

<Subsection title="Wings and directing" id="wings-and-directing">

In a Titan Engagement a Squadmate can ride one player character's <GlossaryTerm term="Wing" /> — one Squadmate per Wing — acting immediately after that character's turn, even when that turn was spent in advance.

**Any player may propose what a Squadmate does.** On a Wing, that character's player decides; off one, a D6 roll-off settles a disagreement. **The GM never directs a Squadmate,** though the GM may give one a voice.

</Subsection>

<InkVignette caption="Six riders in column pass a burned farmhouse beyond the Wall, cloaks snapping in the wind." label="A column of six Survey Corps riders beyond the Wall" art="guide-making-your-soldier-squad" />

<Subsection title="The starting Squad" id="the-starting-squad">

The Squad aims for **6 soldiers**, so you start with **6 minus the number of player characters** Squadmates: four players, two Squadmates. Choose each one's template and name it.

**Fewer is allowed,** down to none, and costs two things: fights run about three times deadlier for player characters, and a player whose character dies with no living Squadmate has nobody to promote.

</Subsection>

<Subsection title="Promotion" note="When a player character dies or retires" id="promotion">

1. **When.** As the procedure ends — a fight's last end step, a day passing, a care window closing — or at once if none is under way.
2. **Who.** Retirements resolve first. Then every player who lost a character picks a living Squadmate at the same time, a D6 roll-off settling any clash, repeating until everyone has one or none is left.
3. **Conversion.** It keeps its name, Specialty, attributes, Talent, gear, and every point of harm exactly as they stand — nothing is recalculated, and its template Talent counts as the Specialty level. Then roll D66 on the Origin table, on Why You Enlisted, and once on each Training Year's events, taking **1 Talent level from each** and no attribute points. Record Merit and Class Rank as none, and Rank as Private. It is now a player character and leaves any Wing.
4. **Nobody left?** Build a new character by any allowed procedure. They join at the next Waypoint, the next Titan Engagement, or the next Downtime — whichever comes first.

</Subsection>

</FinePrint>

<SectionHead id="building-without-the-lifepath" eyebrow="Section 8">Building Without the Lifepath</SectionHead>

A built soldier is the steady soldier every Titan in this book was made to face, with the player choosing what they carry. A rolled soldier is that same soldier on average — with a chance to come out ahead, and a chance to come out worse.

<FinePrint title="the build steps, and their limits">

<Subsection title="The steps" note="Nine steps, in order" id="the-build-steps">

Both builds run the same way and differ only at step 3.

1. **Campaign Year,** if the campaign has none. No Exam, whatever the vote.
2. **Specialty.** One of the nine.
3. **Attributes.** **Template Build:** your Specialty's <a href="#the-stat-block">Squadmate template</a> ratings, unchanged: the ratings only, not its Talent. **Free Build:** one of two shapes, **4·3·3·3·3·2** or **4·4·3·3·2·2**, placed in any order that puts a 4 on the key attribute. Either way: 18 points, nothing below 2 or above 4, and no swap, floor, or Top 10 bonus.
4. **Origin.** Choose a row the Campaign Year allows. Take a Haven, its Canon Tie if you want it, and **1 Talent level**, but none of its attribute points.
5. **Drive.** Any row's, with no attribute point.
6. **Training Years, for the story.** Name one event row per year if you like. They give nothing.
7. **Talents.** 1 level from your Specialty's list, then 3 in any Talents.
8. **Merit and Class Rank:** none. Never Top 10.
9. **Finish and join the Squad** as a <a href="#finishing">Lifepath soldier</a> does.

</Subsection>

<BoxPair>
<RuleBox title="Talent limits" icon="ph:student">

- **5 levels:** 1 Origin, 1 Specialty list, 3 free.
- No Talent above level 2, and **at most one Talent at level 2**.
- No rule Talent above its maximum.

</RuleBox>
<RuleBox variant="stop" title="What only the Lifepath reaches" icon="ph:prohibit">

A built soldier always has 18 points and a key attribute of 4. A Free Build with 2s on both Strength and Agility has **Health 2**: two untreated Critical Injuries and they are Down, and a Squad of four such soldiers loses about four times as many soldiers a fight.

Only the Lifepath gives a key attribute of 5 or 6, a Top 10 Class Rank, or a place in the Exam. A player who wants that chance rolls.

</RuleBox>
</BoxPair>
