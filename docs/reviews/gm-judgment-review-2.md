## New findings by file

### 1. `docs/rules/04-gear.md` section 4.6 and `data/gear/falls.yaml`, with ADR-0024 limit 17

#### C1. Critical: inside a Titan Engagement, Chapter 4's Height procedure gives a fall a ruling names the extreme band, against ADR-0024 limit 17 and decision 9-30

- **Location:** `docs/rules/04-gear.md` line 322 (*When a soldier falls*, **Named by a rule or a ruling**: "low or high as the GM names from the fall's height, never extreme") against lines 328 to 335 (*Height*: steps 1 to 4, which end "raise the band one step, to at most **extreme**", and whose only carve-out is the closing sentence "**Outside a Titan Engagement,** the rule that makes the soldier fall names the band, or the GM does for a fall a ruling names, low or high only"); `data/gear/falls.yaml` `triggers` `rule-named.height` and `height.steps`, `height.outside_titan_engagement`; ADR-0024 limit 17; decision batch 9, 9-30; `docs/rules/01-core-rules.md` line 38; `docs/rules/03-harm-and-mind.md` line 69.
- **Problem:** 9-30 capped a ruling's fall at low or high so that harm a ruling names never reaches 4 damage, and ADR-0024 limit 17 states that cap flatly, with no exception for a fight. Chapter 4 states the cap twice, but only the *outside a Titan Engagement* paragraph tells the Height procedure to stop reading Positions. Inside a Titan Engagement, steps 3 and 4 still say to take the band from the soldier's Position relative to the fall's reference Titan and then raise it for Giant Forest or a Large Titan. The chapter gives no reason to read a ruling's fall differently from any other rule-named fall there, and the two named rule-named falls inside a fight, `knock-loose` and letting go, do use the Height steps ("with the band Chapter 4's height steps give"; "They fall from that height"). So a fall staked on a called roll made at Blind Spot against a Large Titan reads high, raised to extreme. The YAML supports the benign reading, because `rule-named.height` is a per-trigger field like `down-mounted`'s `from-a-horse`, but the chapter, which is what a GM reads at the table, contradicts itself and defeats the limit. This is the harm round 1's M3 was raised to close, still reachable in the one place the fix did not go.
- **Scenario:** Round 4 in the Urban district. Private Brandt, at Blind Spot on a standard Large Titan with 3 current Health, wants to cut the strap of a hanging signboard so it swings into the Titan's eye. The GM rules it is no Catalog entry, calls a roll on Agility, names it Harsh, and stakes a fall. Reading section 4.6's **Named by a rule or a ruling** bullet, the GM names high. Reading *Height* two paragraphs later, the same GM finds Blind Spot is high and a Large reference Titan raises it one step, to extreme. The extreme band deals 2, 2, 3, 3, 4, or 4 damage: by simulation, 4 damage one time in three, against high's ceiling of 3. On a 4 she is at 0 current Health, Down, with an immediate Crush Critical Injury that is lethal or instant death 15.3% of the time on a first Critical Injury at a rolled Injury Location. One failed called roll has done the musket's work, which is exactly what 9-5's ceiling exists to prevent.
- **Fix options:**
  1. Give *Height* the exception it is missing: make step 1 read "A fall a rule or a ruling names takes the band that rule or the GM names (`triggers`, `rule-named`), and no later step changes it", parallel to step 2's fall from a horse, and move the low-or-high cap out of the closing "Outside a Titan Engagement" sentence so that it binds everywhere. Mirror it in `falls.yaml` `height.steps` as a first step.
  2. Keep the Height steps for a fall a *rule* names inside a Titan Engagement and carve out only a ruling's: add to line 322 "inside a Titan Engagement as well as outside one, the band a ruling names is low or high and the Height steps do not raise it", and add the same sentence to `falls.yaml` `rule-named.height`.
  3. If the extreme band is wanted for a ruling's fall from a Large Titan, that reverses 9-30 and needs a decision and an ADR-0024 edit, not a chapter fix; log it as an open question with this scenario and Chapter 3's "never 4 damage or more" corrected alongside.

### 2. `docs/rules/01-core-rules.md` section 1.1, with Chapter 2 section 2.9 and Chapters 5 and 7

#### M1. Major: whether a called roll can be made inside a Titan Engagement or a Skirmish is never stated, and four rules answer it differently

- **Location:** `docs/rules/01-core-rules.md` line 28 (item 1, which names no context), line 47 (item 4, whose first clause is "In a Titan Engagement or a Skirmish, a soldier can attempt an action again on a later turn"), line 51 ("outside a Titan Engagement can be Helped as the GM rules"), line 72 (1.3 step 1, *Acts no rule covers*); `docs/rules/02-character-creation.md` line 422 ("a called roll meets one only where they count turns: in a Titan Engagement, or in a Skirmish") against lines 613 and 621 (*Outside a fight* gives the called roll, *In a fight* gives the improvised act); `data/core/circumstances.yaml` `applies_to` and `in_titan_engagement.reaches`; `docs/rules/05-titan-engagement.md` and `06-standard-titans.md`, which never use the words "called roll"; `docs/rules/07-playtest-rules.md` line 509 (the closed list of what a soldier can do in a Skirmish, which does not include one).
- **Problem:** 9-27 routed every act a soldier describes in a fight to an improvised act with a model entry, and Chapter 2 section 2.9 now splits cleanly into *Outside a fight* and *In a fight*. But nothing says that a called roll is therefore an outside-a-fight instrument. Chapter 1 item 1 states the trigger for a called roll with no context at all; item 4 gives a retry rule that names both fights; the sentence after item 6 says Help is available "outside a Titan Engagement", which reads as though a called roll inside one exists and merely cannot be Helped; and Chapter 2 line 422 says outright that a called roll can meet a Drive's turn test in a Titan Engagement or a Skirmish, which is only true if called rolls happen there. Meanwhile Chapters 5 and 6 never mention one, and Chapter 7's Skirmish action list is closed against one. A GM cannot tell whether the answer is "an act in a fight is always an improvised act, so no" or "an improvised act is for acts that copy an entry, and anything else is still a called roll". The stakes menu, the one-cost-per-failed-roll rule, 9-35's Health statement, and the fall band of Critical C1 all have different readings depending on the answer.
- **Scenario:** Round 2 of a Titan Engagement in a burning district. Private Wendt says he is hauling the granary doors shut so the Titan cannot follow the civilians through. No entry imitates that: it is not Break Attention, not Draw Attention, not a strike, not Help. The GM reaches for an improvised act and finds no model entry that fits, since 9-27 closes the set to six Titan Engagement actions and eleven that work anywhere. The GM then reaches for a called roll on Strength alone and finds Chapter 2 section 2.9 telling them that the called roll belongs to *Outside a fight*. Whichever way the GM jumps, the next question is unanswered: does the roll cost the action, can a comrade Help, and if the stake is a fall, which band?
- **Fix options:**
  1. Say it once in Chapter 1 item 1 and in `dice-pool.yaml` `called_roll`: a called roll is made outside a Titan Engagement and a Skirmish; in a fight an act that is not an entry taken as written is an improvised act (section 1.9), and an act no entry can model either happens, cannot be done, or is described with no roll. Then correct Chapter 2 line 422's turn-test sentence, which would no longer have a case, and drop item 4's first clause into the improvised act's rule where it belongs.
  2. Or say the opposite, that a called roll may be made in a fight for an act no entry can model, and then state, in one paragraph, what it spends (the action), whether it can be Helped, that its fall is low or high (Critical C1), and that 9-35's Health statement applies.
  3. Either way, add the answer to the Rulings paragraphs of Chapters 5 and 7, which today list what the GM rules in a fight and do not mention the called roll at all.

### 3. `docs/rules/05-titan-engagement.md` section 5.4, with Chapter 1 section 1.9, Chapter 2 section 2.9, and `data/character/action-catalog.yaml`

#### M2. Major: an improvised act modelled on the Nape strike has no defined outcome when it reaches the Nape Depth, so rolling better gives less

- **Location:** `docs/rules/05-titan-engagement.md` lines 436 to 448 (*Improvised acts*: "on a success produces only the model entry's effect, at that entry's size" and "Only a Nape strike taken as its entry is written kills a Titan; an act modelled on the Nape strike never does"); `docs/rules/01-core-rules.md` line 302; `docs/rules/02-character-creation.md` lines 621 to 628; `data/character/action-catalog.yaml` `uncatalogued_actions` `in_a_fight`; `data/engagement/titan-harm.yaml` `nape_strikes` (`kill` and `falls_short`); decision batch 9, 9-27, item 3.
- **Problem:** 9-27 made the model entry an entry of kind `action` the soldier could take in that fight, and `nape-strike` is one of the six such entries in a Titan Engagement. 9-27 then says in terms that an act modelled on the Nape strike "is made with its requirements and needs and never kills". The Nape strike has exactly two outcomes in `titan-harm.yaml`: reaching the Nape Depth kills, and falling short turns each success into an Opening. Removing the kill leaves the first outcome empty. The rules never say what an improvised act modelled on the Nape strike does when its successes reach the Nape Depth, and they cannot fall back on `falls_short`, because the strike did not fall short. The result is a rule that pays worse for rolling better: at Nape Depth 4, three successes make three Openings and four or more make nothing at all. This is the only one of the six entries with this shape, because every other one's effect counts successes rather than testing a threshold.
- **Scenario:** Round 3, Anchor Rating Urban, a standard Medium Titan grounded by a Broken leg. Private Brandt holds Blind Spot, does not hold Attention, has working ODM Gear and a Blade Set. She says she is levering the cornice of the clock tower down onto the Nape rather than cutting it. The GM rules it an improvised act and names the Nape strike as the model, which carries the Blind Spot requirement, the Attention restriction, and the need of 4. She declares the grounded Titan's 2 Bonus Dice: Strength 4, Clean Cut 1, 2 Bonus Dice, 1 Blade Set die, 1 Stress Die. By simulation she reaches 4 or more successes 25.4% of the time and falls short with at least 1 success 67.7%. On the 25.4% the table has no rule to apply. A Rookie with no Bonus Dice reaches it 12.5% of the time, and a Veteran at the cap 55.1%, so the undefined band is the common case for a good striker, not an edge.
- **Fix options:**
  1. State the outcome once, in `action-catalog.yaml` `in_a_fight` and in Chapter 5 section 5.4: an act modelled on the Nape strike that reaches the Nape Depth creates Openings as a strike that falls short does, one per success, and never kills. This keeps the effect at the model entry's size and removes the perverse step.
  2. Take the Nape strike out of the model-entry set, in the same three places: the model entry is an entry of kind `action` the soldier could take in that fight, other than the Nape strike, since only a Nape strike taken as written kills and no other outcome is defined for it. The examples in Chapter 5 already point every Titan-facing improvisation at Break Attention, Draw Attention, or the Body Part strike.
  3. If neither is wanted, say plainly that such an act produces nothing on reaching the Nape Depth, so at least the table has an answer, and note the step in the design note.

### 4. `docs/rules/07-playtest-rules.md` section 7.1, with Chapter 1 section 1.1 item 6 and `data/expedition/route.yaml`

#### M3. Major: the Mission Brief the GM writes sets three of the Leg Hazard roll's five modifiers, which two rules list as applied as written, and the route's length and Depots are unbounded

- **Location:** `docs/rules/07-playtest-rules.md` line 35 (the Rulings paragraph: the GM rules on "the route, its Waypoints, its Formation Posts, and its Hard Ride days") and line 47 (the Brief names the Waypoints, each Leg's Distance Band and Formation Post, and the Hard Ride days), against `docs/rules/01-core-rules.md` line 49 (item 6: the GM applies as written "the hazard and Night tables and their modifiers") and `docs/rules/07-playtest-rules.md` line 164 (the *Decided* note: "the Leg roll, the hazard and Night tables and their modifiers, rations, and the Straggler's random victim stay as written"); `data/expedition/route.yaml` `route.mission_brief_fields` and `depot`; `data/expedition/hazards.yaml` `leg_hazards.modifiers`; ADR-0009 as amended; decision batch 9, 9-15 and 9-17.
- **Problem:** The hazard roll is D6 plus five modifiers: the Distance Band, the night camps made, the Formation Post, the Pace, and the Leg roll's outcome. The Brief sets the first three outright, because the route names each Leg's Distance Band and Post and the days Command orders a Hard Ride, and it sets the fourth indirectly by naming how many Waypoints there are. So the closed sentence "their modifiers stay as written" and the open sentence "the GM rules on the route, its Posts and its Hard Ride days" describe the same numbers and cannot both be read as they stand. Nothing bounds the route either: `mission_brief_fields` caps neither the number of Legs, where the interim route rolls D3+3, nor how many Waypoints are Depots, where the interim route allows none. Overrun begins a Titan Engagement with every soldier at In Reach and two Background Titans. The Squad is now fighting a near-guaranteed worst-row Titan Engagement every Leg, on rows nobody softened or swapped, and the playtest log records nothing to distinguish it.
- **Fix options:**
  1. Reword the two closed sentences so they name what actually stays closed: in Chapter 1 item 6 and Chapter 7 line 164, read "the hazard and Night tables, their rolls, and the modifiers a Leg's own outcome and the night camps made supply" and add to Chapter 7 line 35 that the route sets the Distance Band, the Formation Post, and the Pace, which the hazard roll then reads as written.
  2. Bound the route in `route.yaml` `mission_brief_fields` and in Chapter 7's route list, at the counts the interim route uses: 4 to 6 Legs, Distance Bands that climb to Deep and back, and at most one Depot before the gate, with anything further marked unmeasured, as 9-16 marks an added Requisition row.
  3. Add the framed route to 9-17's playtest log fields beside the framed start: the number of Legs, each Leg's Distance Band and Post, the Hard Ride days, and the Depots, so the retune can read an authored Expedition apart from a rolled one. This is the smallest fix and the one OQ-167 already has a shape for.
