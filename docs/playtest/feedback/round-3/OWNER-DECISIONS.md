## The last round of questions, as settled

1. **Skirmish.** Held for the report. If firearms stop frightening anyone, the lever is the Skirmish
   rider, 1 to 2 per Net Success beyond the first, ahead of the musket's base damage.
2. **Frenzy.** Plus 1 at each round end, capped at 3, added to the behavior roll. The simulator moves
   the rate and the cap; the shape is settled.
3. **Retarget, not downgrade.** A Titan that cannot reach its fixation turns on the next candidate down
   the Attention Ladder who meets the entry, and its Attention moves with it.
4. **Prompt timeout.** The default is to wait. A live table has the GM's "roll it for them" control as
   the escape hatch, and auto-rolling by default would quietly restore the problem the prompt card
   exists to fix. An optional timeout setting is offered for asynchronous tables, off by default, 60
   seconds when switched on.
5. **Zones.** The derived version: zone plus attachment produces the four Position names, so every
   Behavior Table requirement and the Attention Ladder keep working unedited.
6. **Field size.** 13 hexes by default, 7 for a corridor fight, 19 for a set piece.
7. **Stride.** Small 1, Medium 2, Large 2, Sprinting Abnormal 3, as starting values for the simulator.
8. **Wrecking.** Only the `wreck` effect, and it takes one anchor-rating step off the Titan's own zone.
   Striding through a zone does not wreck it.
9. **Sequencing.** Batches A, F, B, C and D run in one cycle and go to the table together. Batch E's
   design and art start in parallel, because Codex art and the board have the longest lead times, but
   its rules and its retune wait until A and F have been played: E changes the tempo of every fight and
   its numbers have to build on A and F's measured ones, not on a guess.
