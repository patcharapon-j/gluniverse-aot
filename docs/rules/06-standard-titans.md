# Chapter 6: Standard Titans

This chapter gives the Titans a Squad fights in Phase 1:
- the standard Small, Medium, and Large Titans, each a stat block and a Behavior Table;
- one Abnormal, the Sprinting Abnormal, with its own Behavior Table and Attention Ladder;
- the ADR-0014 figures each of them was measured against.

It builds on:
- **Chapter 3:** Critical Injuries with their Injury Locations and Injury Types, the Closing Hand Scar, and the `abnormal` and `first-titan-engagement` Fear Rolls.
- **Chapter 4:** falls, airborne, and mounted.
- **Chapter 5:** the interim setup table, the stat block and Behavior Table format, the Next Behavior and card procedures, the Attention Ladder and its tests, harming Titans, Read, and the Grab.

Later rules this chapter only refers to:
- **Not yet written:** Mission Briefs and Operation Frames, which may name an Abnormal; Shifters, which use Intent Tables (ADR-0013) and are not Phase 1; Research Points earned from Reads and from surviving Abnormals.

## 6.1 Using a Titan

### Which Titan appears

The GM may name the Focus Titan and any Background Titans as framing: any Titan in this chapter, standard or Abnormal. A Titan the GM names is never rolled, and no `medium_abnormal` roll is made for it (Chapter 5, section 5.1; decision batch 9, 9-15). Otherwise:

`index.yaml` (`standard_titans`) names the **standard Titan** of each Size Class. Whenever Chapter 5's interim setup table gives a Size Class for a Focus Titan or a Background Titan, that Titan is the standard Titan of the class, with one exception:
- **The Abnormal roll.** When the Focus Titan's Size Class roll gives Medium, roll D6 on `medium_abnormal` (`data/engagement/engagement-setup.yaml`; Chapter 5, section 5.1). On a 6 the Focus Titan is the Sprinting Abnormal instead.
- **Background Titans** that the setup table gives are always the standard Titan of their class, and never roll on `medium_abnormal`. A rule that begins a Titan Engagement may still name an Abnormal as a Background Titan (Chapter 5, section 5.10).

An **Abnormal** also appears when the rule that begins a Titan Engagement names it, or when the GM names it as framing (`abnormals`, `enters_play`). When an Abnormal starts a Titan Engagement as a Focus Titan, or becomes one, every soldier holding a Position makes the `abnormal` Fear Roll (Chapter 3).

The setup table's Abnormal roll makes the Sprinting Abnormal the Focus Titan of 1 Titan Engagement in 12 that the table sets up (decision batch 4, OQ-104). Section 6.6 reports what that adds per Titan Engagement.

### Reading a stat block

A stat block gives the fields of Chapter 5's format (section 5.4):
- **Size Class and Abnormal.**
- **Tempo:** cards per round.
- **Nape Depth:** successes one Nape strike needs.
- **Regeneration clock:** segments.
- **Heave rating:** the heave count at which its body, living or a corpse, frees every soldier it pins (Chapter 5, section 5.7). A standard Titan takes its Size Class's, and an Abnormal lists its own (decision batch 8, 8-9).
- **Stride:** the zones it moves toward its Attention holder on each of its cards that resolves a behavior (Chapter 5, section 5.5; decision batch 16, 16-16). A standard Titan takes its Size Class's and an Abnormal lists its own; Chapter 5's Stride table (section 5.4) renders every Titan's from `data/titans/`. A grounded Titan's is 0.
- **Body Parts:** in stat block order, with each one's Toughness. The order breaks Regeneration ties and decides which arm Grabs.
- **Attention Ladder:** standard, or the Abnormal's own.

A standard Titan's numbers are public. An Abnormal's Toughness, Nape Depth, Regeneration clock length, and ladder are hidden until a Read reveals each (Chapter 5, section 5.8), and the tracker shows only its clock's filled segments until the length is revealed. This chapter prints those values only in a GM section (section 6.5).

### Reading a Behavior Table

Each row of a rendered Behavior Table gives:
- **D6:** the result that rolls it. The behavior roll is D6 plus the Titan's Frenzy, so a frenzied Titan reaches the lower rows of this column and the upper ones only when the roll cannot be taken (Chapter 5, sections 5.4 and 5.5). Thrash is never rolled.
- **Behavior and tier.**
- **Targets:** the holder of the Titan's Attention, or the holder and everyone else at their Position who is not Grabbed.
- **Holder's Position:** the entry's Position requirement. "Any" means all four Positions.
- **Body Parts used:** the kinds the entry needs unbroken. "arm" or "leg" needs one of that kind unbroken, and "both legs" needs both. Every entry in this chapter that uses a leg needs both legs: one Broken leg grounds the Titan (Chapter 5, section 5.7), and a grounded Titan does not walk, run, or stride. `titans.py` fails an entry that uses one leg.
- **Attack Dice:** how many Titan Dice the entry rolls when a card resolves it. The GM rolls them in the open, and the successes are the card's Severity, which each target's dodge cancels one for one (Chapter 5, sections 5.4 and 5.5). The line under each table is the Titan Dice legend.
- **Effects,** in order. Each Critical Injury names its Injury Type, its Injury Location, or "rolled location" for Chapter 3's D6 roll, and whether it can be lethal. An arm or a leg reads "side rolled": Chapter 3's D6 gives the side, odd left and even right. A Bite is Bite, and every other Critical Injury here is Crush (decision batch 7, 7-5; OQ-137). A Grab effect starts Chapter 5's Grab procedure, whose harm is the torso crush, a Crush Critical Injury that cannot be lethal (section 5.9; `data/harm/health.yaml`, `titan_attacks`). A knock-loose fall's damage is Crush too (Chapter 4). Every effect cell that can harm names its type.
- **Fallback:** what happens instead when the holder does not meet the Position requirement.
- **Grab:** yes for an entry whose effects include a grab effect. That is the mark the Closing Hand Scar reads (`index.yaml`, `grab_mark`).

Below each table, one line per entry says what the behavior looks like. The line decides nothing; the row does. Each line names only the Body Parts its row uses, and shows nothing its row's Positions rule out, so the words never show a Titan doing something its Broken parts or the holder's Position forbid. `titans.py` fails any line that names the Titan's arm, leg, or eyes when its row does not list that kind, that shows the Titan seeing a holder its row allows at Blind Spot, or that shows it touching a holder its row allows at Distant.

Every procedure step comes from Chapter 5: the Next Behavior roll with Frenzy added, no back-to-back repeats and move-up past Broken parts, the card, the retarget, the fallback and its own retarget, Thrash, and dodging. No entry names death. A Titan kills only through:
- a lethal Critical Injury, by its Death Roll or an instant-death row, which a lucky roll's net success rider can reach (Chapter 3);
- the Grab's devour step (Chapter 5);
- a fall one of its effects causes, such as a knock-loose, or a Critical Injury that leaves an airborne or mounted soldier Down. The fall's damage can bring Health to 0 and inflict a Critical Injury that is lethal (Chapter 4).

In play, Thrash used to be the behavior each table resolved most: 31% of the standard Small Titan's resolved cards, 41% of the standard Medium Titan's and of the standard Large Titan's, and 34% of the Sprinting Abnormal's at their reference starts. Nearly all of it came from a holder standing where the rolled entry could not reach, most often a Nape striker who fell short at Blind Spot, so a Titan with a soldier at its Nape mostly thrashed. **Decision batch 13 is aimed squarely at that**: a Titan whose Attention holder cannot meet the rolled entry now retargets down the Attention Ladder to a soldier who can, and falls back only when nobody in the fight qualifies, so those four figures are stale and the retune replaces them (Chapter 5, section 5.6). Each table's Thrash line is still written for that Titan's body. What Thrash's share settles at is now a figure the retune reports rather than a consequence accepted and not tuned away (`tuning.yaml`, `verdicts`, `thrash_share`; decision batch 4, OQ-101, as amended by decision batch 13, 13-9).

An entry is marked as a Grab by its grab effect alone, with no field of its own (decision batch 4, OQ-100).

The kill-share constraint's "any state" means every previous behavior with every count of Broken eyes, arms, and legs, and the three standard tables are built to that reading (decision batch 4, OQ-101).

An entry that uses a leg needs both legs, so a Titan grounded by one Broken leg cannot resolve it (decision batch 4, OQ-109).

When an effect kills its target, that target receives none of the card's later effects, and every other target still does (Chapter 5, section 5.5; decision batch 4, OQ-107). No entry in this chapter gives a target any effect after one that harms them, so no table here reaches the case.

> **Design note (OQ-101):** The tables follow what Titans do in 845 to 850, in the order the tiers need:
> - **Terrorize:** a Titan stops dead with a fixed, empty grin, snaps at the air, or walks at a soldier. What it will do next shows in its body, so each table has one telegraph.
> - **Control:** it swats at soldiers, rolls and heaves to shake off whatever clings to it, or pins them under its bulk.
> - **Kill:** it bites, or it grabs and eats.
>
> Titans go for people, not pain and not animals, so every entry acts on soldiers, and none on a horse.
>
> Read strictly, the Chapter 6 constraint says no state of previous behavior and Broken parts may push the kill share past one half at Frenzy 0; Frenzy 1 to 3 climb past it by design (Chapter 5, section 5.4). The Chapter 5 reference table would fail that reading (4 results in 6 with both arms Broken after Shake Off), so every table here was built to pass it. On the standard tables the kill results are 5 and 6, a control entry at 3 uses a Body Part kind only if the entry at 6 uses it too, and the entries at 4 and 5 use none. That layout is why several entries use no Body Part, and why their lines describe acts that need none. Giving those entries the part their first lines described breaks the limit:
> - **Small, Lurch Closer with a leg:** 0.67, after Scrabble with both arms and both legs Broken.
> - **Medium, Snap Short with an arm** (it was a groping hand): 1.00, after Shake Off with the eyes and both arms Broken.
> - **Medium, Shake Off with an arm:** 1.00.
> - **Large, Shrug Off with an arm:** 0.67. **Large, Crush with a leg:** 0.83.

## 6.5 Abnormals

An **Abnormal** is a Titan whose behavior breaks the standard pattern and that uses its own Behavior Table (glossary). Every Chapter 5 rule applies to it as to any Focus Titan, with these differences, all already in Chapter 5's rules and the Chapter 6 constraints:
- **Stat block:** it lists every value in full. Values may differ from its Size Class row, within Tempo 1 to 2, Nape Depth 3 to 4, Regeneration 2 to 4, Toughness 1 to 3, and Attack Dice 3 to 12. The glossary sets Nape Depth and Regeneration by Size Class, or by an Abnormal's own stat block (decision batch 4, OQ-106).
- **Ladder:** it may name its own Attention Ladder, built from the closed `tests` list, beginning with hooked-into-its-body and ending with nearest, as every ladder does, so a Nape striker who falls short draws its next behavior on every ladder (Chapter 5, section 5.6; decision batch 4b, 4b-1). A Grab and a decoy override it exactly as they override the standard ladder.
- **Hidden values:** its Toughness, Nape Depth, Regeneration clock length, and ladder are Read facts. Its public counts and state changes show its Toughness over time.
- **Fear:** the `abnormal` Fear Roll when it starts a Titan Engagement as a Focus Titan or becomes one.

This chapter prints each Abnormal's hidden values only in a GM section, which players do not read, so a Read at the table is how they learn them (decision batch 4, OQ-108). Its Behavior Table, which is not a Read fact, stays public.

### The Sprinting Abnormal

A Medium Abnormal that runs. At the table everyone sees what any Focus Titan shows: it is Medium, it draws two cards a round (Tempo 2), its Heave rating is 3, its Stride is 3, one more than any standard Titan's, and its Body Parts' counts and states and its clock's filled segments are on the tracker. <!-- BEGIN RENDERED: behavior-text sprinting-abnormal -->
- **Run Past.** It runs past on long, pounding legs without slowing, and everyone beside the soldier feels the ground jump.
- **Veer.** Its head lolls and its whole body tips one way, and the way it tips shows where it will go next.
- **Trample.** It runs straight through the soldier, and its feet come down wherever they come down.
- **Pitch Headlong.** It flings its whole body forward and rolls over, a slow, heaving roll that scrapes off whatever clings to it.
- **Grab.** A hand shoots out at the soldier and closes around them.
- **Headlong Lunge.** It closes the last of the gap in one long stride and snaps at the soldier with its whole head.
- **Thrash.** It spins and rolls on the spot, twisting every way at once.
<!-- END RENDERED: behavior-text sprinting-abnormal -->

What its table means at the table:
- **It runs through people.** Run Past and Trample work against a holder at Distant or In Reach, so a soldier on horseback in another zone is not out of its path: Distant is out of a standing Titan's reach, not out of a running one's way (Chapter 5, section 5.2). Run Past strikes the holder and everyone at their Position **in the holder's zone** only, not every soldier elsewhere on the field (Chapter 5, section 5.4; decision batch 16, 16-18). With a Stride of 3 it covers most of the Standard field on one card before it resolves the entry. Trample's leg Critical Injury cannot be lethal; a rider who goes Down from it falls (Chapter 4).
- **Its hands and jaws need you close.** Its Grab and Headlong Lunge need In Reach or On Body, and the Lunge falls back to Pitch Headlong. Both roll 6 Attack Dice, the standard Medium Titan's control pool rather than its kill pool of 9: it snatches and lunges at full stride, and a soldier leaps clear of a Titan that cannot stop.
- **Cut one leg.** Run Past, Trample, and Headlong Lunge each need both legs. One Broken leg drops it to the ground (Chapter 5, section 5.7): it can no longer run past, trample, or lunge, and it only veers, pitches over, and grabs, while Nape strikes against it gain the grounded dice. Four strikers who leave its legs alone still kill it sooner and take less harm under the rules as they stand, an open question on every table (section 6.6, *Four strikers and no cutters*; OQ-112).
- **Clinging to it is risky.** Pitch Headlong knocks loose the soldier holding its Attention when they are On Body or at Blind Spot, though its slow roll rolls only 3 Attack Dice.
- **No entry affects a horse.**

The Sprinting Abnormal's Behavior Table and Attention Ladder stand within the closed tests list and the tier rules (decision batch 4, OQ-102), with its ladder's top two rungs in the order every ladder takes, hooked-into-its-body first (decision batch 4b, 4b-1). Its values are held against Chapter 6's bar, read row against row in both Squad sheet orders (decision batch 4, OQ-103). Its leg entries need both legs (decision batch 4, OQ-109).

> **Design note (OQ-102, OQ-103, OQ-109):** Between 845 and 850 the Abnormals the Survey Corps meets break the pattern: they ignore soldiers close to them and run for somewhere else, and the Survey Corps rides out in a wide scouting formation to see them coming. In manga chapter 22 (anime episode 17) one runs past the formation's outer riders toward its centre, and riders bring it down by cutting its legs, then its Nape. The Sprinting Abnormal is that runner.
> - **Its body is a runner's.** Two cards a round (Tempo 2), and three of its six entries need both legs, so cutting one leg is how a Squad stops it running, as in canon.
> - **It is not a Shifter.** An earlier draft singled out riders, snatched soldiers from the saddle and the air, and leapt after flyers. Those are the Female Titan's acts in the same chapters, a Shifter's choices, and Shifters are not Phase 1 (ADR-0002, ADR-0013). This table has no entry that reaches Distant with a hand or jaws, and nothing that picks out a rider.
> - **The bar:** an Abnormal is not tuned to a target, so this chapter holds it against its own bar in `tuning.yaml` (`abnormals`), at the start any rule that names it gives: every soldier mounted, with the `abnormal` Fear Roll. Each limit is a standard Titan's measured figure, and every limit is read one way, row against row, as decision batch 3b reads every table. Each of its rows is set against the standard Medium and Large Titans' rows with the same support, or their reference rows where they have none. It must not be trivial: a median kill in round 2 or later and at least the Medium Titan's Critical Injuries in every row, and more deaths than the Medium Titan at the reference start. Its two ladder rows, the standard ladder in place of its own and its earlier ladder, are run beside the bar and reported, not judged: they measure what its ladder is worth, not the creature this chapter publishes (round 3 retune, R7). It must be winnable: a median kill by round 4, and no more fights that end with no kill than the Large Titan's row (decision batch 5, 5-10). And it has a ceiling: no more deaths per fight than the Large Titan's row. A limit holds within sampling when the figure is past it by no more than 2 standard errors at 120,000 fights a row. A row past that on one seed is run again on a second seed in the same order, and it fails only if the figure pooled over both runs is past 2 standard errors (decision batch 4b, 4b-4). Every row is run in both Squad sheet orders, the player characters listed cutters first and then strikers first, each twin in the same order, because under Chapter 5's last tie-break neither order may choose anything (decision batch 4, 4-4). Section 6.6 checks each part.

<!-- BEGIN RENDERED: ladder sprinting-abnormal -->
1. **hooked-into-its-body:** The soldier holds on-body relative to the Titan, or holds the hooked-by-strike flag for it.
2. **loudest-or-brightest:** The soldier holds the loudest flag for the Titan.
3. **current-holder:** The soldier holds the Titan's Attention and a Position other than Distant. A holder who is at Distant, mounted or on foot, does not meet it (decision batch 4, 4-3). A Down soldier can meet it.
4. **nearest:** The soldier holds the closest Position any soldier in the set being tested holds, in the order on-body, in-reach, blind-spot, distant. As the highest rung met, it is met by the candidates at the closest Position any candidate holds; as a lower rung, by the tied soldiers at the closest Position any of them holds. Among candidates at Distant it counts no zones: every soldier in another zone holds the same Position, and ties go on to the holder and card steps (decision batch 16, 16-17). A Down soldier can meet it.

What its ladder means at the table:
- **A blade at its Nape or a soldier On Body turns it first.** A Nape striker who has just struck, whatever the result, comes before a soldier On Body, as on every ladder (ADR-0010). A striker who falls short draws its next behavior, whoever else is making noise (decision batch 4b, 4b-1).
- **Then noise.** While no one is hooked into it, a soldier holding the loudest flag, which only Draw Attention sets and only from a Position other than Distant, outranks everyone below (Chapter 5, section 5.6; decision batch 5, OQ-113).
- **Then the one it is already running down.** The soldier who holds its Attention keeps it while they are On Body, In Reach, or at Blind Spot, until a Nape strike, a soldier On Body, or noise turns it. A holder who flies to Blind Spot is still its quarry, and a comrade standing closer does not pull it away. A holder who gets away to Distant, mounted or on foot, is no longer its quarry: they keep its Attention only while they are the nearest.
- **Only then whoever is nearest.** When nothing holds its Attention (when the fight begins, when a decoy's hold or a Grab ends, or when its holder dies, leaves, is Grabbed by another Focus Titan, or holds no Position), or when its holder is at Distant, it takes the soldiers On Body, then In Reach, then at Blind Spot, then Distant. Ties at the same distance go to its holder, then to the lowest card. A tie no card can break leaves nothing holding its Attention until its next card: a fight begins with every soldier at Distant and no cards dealt, so, since no rung of this ladder picks out one of them, a Squad starts with nothing holding it, and its first card turns to whoever has come closest, or to the lowest card when no one has moved. When it enters as a Background Titan at the background-clocks end step no cards choose either; when it enters mid-round, after a flare fills its clock, that round's cards break the tie (decision batch 4, 4-3 and 4-13; decision batch 4b, 4b-3 and 4b-6).
- **It ignores reach and pain.** It has no rung for soldiers in its reach or for soldiers who have just hurt it. The cutters at its legs draw it only when it is running no one down and they are the nearest.
- **Riders** at Distant draw it only as the nearest, like anyone else, since Draw Attention needs a closer Position (decision batch 5, OQ-113). A rider at Distant is never kept as its quarry, so a rider who rides clear has led it off, and it goes for the nearest.
- **Down soldiers** meet current-holder and nearest. A holder who goes Down anywhere but Distant stays its quarry until something above turns it; a Down holder at Distant, and any other Down soldier, draws it only when no one is closer.

> **Design note (OQ-102):** Canon's runner pays no heed to the soldiers in its reach or to the riders cutting at its legs, and once it has picked its way it runs on without turning aside. So the ladder drops the standard ladder's rungs for reach and for pain, and names current-holder above nearest: the soldier it last turned on keeps it, while they are not at Distant, until a Nape strike, a soldier On Body, or noise turns it (Chapter 5, section 5.6, *Abnormal ladders*; decision batch 3e; decision batch 4, 4-3). Like every Titan it turns first on what digs into it: an anchor in its body, or a blade at its Nape, which ADR-0010 counts as hooking in, and every ladder begins with that rung (decision batch 4b, 4b-1). The runner heads for where the most soldiers are, and the closed `tests` list has no rung for that, so loudest-or-brightest, one rung below, stands in for what draws it away; a runner's test would need a Chapter 5 decision (OQ-102).
> - **Why current-holder.** Every Sprinting Abnormal figure was first measured on a Titan that kept its holder, because the nearest rung, as then written, tied every candidate and let the holder step keep them. Decision batch 3e made nearest narrow to the closest soldiers. Without current-holder the same ladder leaves its quarry for whoever stands closer, like a standard Titan without reach or pain. With its kill entries at a fixed need of 3 that Titan killed more soldiers than the standard Large Titan, with helpers or a screen, by 8 to 13 standard errors; with them at a fixed need of 2 it still fails the ceiling narrowly, by 2.4 standard errors in two of the six supported rows read (section 6.6). Naming the rung keeps the runner canon shows, and a Read reveals a rung a player can use: stop being its quarry by making noise, cutting its Nape, putting someone On Body, or riding clear to Distant.
> - **Why not at Distant, and why a fight starts with nothing holding it.** Before decision batch 4 a fight's first evaluation, with every soldier at Distant and no cards dealt, gave its Attention to the soldier listed first on the Squad sheet, and current-holder kept that soldier for the fight, so whoever wrote the sheet chose its quarry: listing the strikers first instead of the cutters broke its ceiling with helpers by 15.8 standard errors. A quarry who stayed mounted at Distant drew only Stress and a Trample the horse could dodge while the others cut unharmed, which halved its Critical Injuries. Now a tie no card can break leaves nothing holding its Attention until its first card, and a rider who rides clear has led it off, as canon's riders lead Titans away from a formation. Parking a silent rider at Distant buys nothing: with a rider listed first who never leaves the saddle and never makes noise, it deals 0.177 deaths and 1.38 Critical Injuries per fight, against the standard Medium Titan's 0.082 and 0.95 to the same Squad (`tools/probes/batch-4/abnormal_b4.py`, 120,000 fights, to the 12-round horizon; decision batch 4, 4-3). No one in that row takes Draw Attention, so the rung order of decision batch 4b leaves it as measured, and since decision batch 5 no rider at Distant can take it (OQ-113).
> - **Why a blade at its Nape comes before noise.** Until decision batch 4b this ladder put loudest-or-brightest first. A rider who stayed in the saddle at Distant and took Draw Attention every turn then drew 1.93 of its cards a fight, 0.55 of them while a comrade who had just struck its Nape was passed over, and that comrade cut again; none of the cards that reach a rider at Distant harm a mounted soldier there. The Squad that gave up a cutter to shout beat the baseline on every measure: 71.2% killed by round 3, 3.1% with no kill within 12 rounds, 0.77 Critical Injuries, and 0.052 deaths per fight, against 69.1%, 7.6%, 0.86, and 0.079 (12,000 fights a row, to the 12-round horizon; `tools/probes/batch-4b/loud_b4b.py`). For as long as the rider shouted, no striker who fell short held its Attention, which is ADR-0010's one hard restriction, and ADR-0010 promises that a striker who falls short draws the Titan's next behavior on every ladder. Decision batch 4b made every ladder begin with hooked-into-its-body (Chapter 5, section 5.6; 4b-1): the same rider then drew 1.69 cards a fight and none while a hooked comrade stood, and the Squad did worse than the baseline on every measure, 65.6%, 7.4%, 0.91, and 0.081. Decision batch 5 then required a Position other than Distant for Draw Attention, so a rider who shouts from the saddle at Distant is no longer a legal policy (OQ-113). Canon's runner turns on a blade at its Nape before it turns toward noise.
> - **Earlier ladders.** Loudest-or-brightest then nearest kept no hooked rung and no quarry, and went for whoever was closest, usually the cutters at its legs. Section 6.6 reports the table with that ladder and with the standard ladder.

## Example: a telegraphed Grab

*The numbers in this example are for illustration only. The rows follow the YAML files at the time of writing; if the files change, the files govern.*

The Squad fights Focus Titan A, the standard Medium Titan, in zone 7 of a Standard field, a Wooded zone, in round 2.

**The field.**
- **Private Oskar Brandt** is on the Ground in zone 7, so In Reach, and holds the Titan's Attention.
- **Private Renate Vogt** is on the Ground in zone 7, In Reach, with Agility 3, ODM Gear 2, Stress 1, and the Closing Hand Scar.
- **The Titan's Next Behavior** is hidden, and nothing is Broken.

**Round 2, the Titan's card.** The ladder keeps Oskar: both soldiers meet only nearest person in reach, and the current holder keeps a tie. He is in the Titan's zone, so it does not stride. The Next Behavior is revealed as Fixed Grin, result 1.
- **Can it happen?** Its eyes are not Broken, and it works at any Position, so it resolves against Oskar with 3 Attack Dice. The GM rolls them in the open: 5, 1, and 3, one success.
- **Oskar's choice.** It deals only Stress, so he keeps his turn and does not dodge. The success is net, so Fixed Grin lands, and he gains 1 Stress.
- **The telegraph.** The GM rolls the new Next Behavior: a 6, Grab. Fixed Grin is the previous behavior and both arms are unbroken, so Grab can be rolled. Its telegraph effect reveals it to every soldier at once.

**Round 3.** Oskar draws 4, Renate 7, and the Titan 12.
- **Card 4: Oskar** makes an ODM move in zone 7 from the Ground to A's Blind Spot, an attachment step a Wooded zone allows, and as the Flight's first step it costs nothing. Every ODM move is a Flight, so he rolls for Fly and scores 2 successes, which give him 2 Momentum, the cap a Wooded zone's 2 anchors set (Chapter 5, section 5.2). The step would have happened on no successes too, though he would have come in loud. He still holds Attention, which changes only when the Titan's card comes up, so he cannot strike the Nape. He strikes the eyes instead and scores 1 success: their count is 1 of Toughness 2, and he holds the just-hurt flag.
- **Card 7: Renate** stays In Reach and strikes the left leg for 1 success, a count of 1, which gives her the just-hurt flag too.
- **Card 12: the Titan.**
  - **Attention.** No one is On Body or holds the hooked-by-strike flag. Renate is the only soldier In Reach, so she takes Attention on the second rung. She is in its zone, so it does not stride. The flags stay until this card's Next step.
  - **The Grab.** It needs In Reach or On Body and an unbroken arm, so it resolves against Renate with 9 Attack Dice. The GM rolls them in the open: 6, 5, 5, 4, 3, 2, 2, 1, and 1. That is 3 successes, so the Grab's Severity is 3.
- **Renate's dodge.** Her turn this round is spent, so the dodge spends her round 4 turn.
  - **The Scar.** Grab's effects include a grab effect, which marks it as a Grab, so the Closing Hand gives her a 1-die penalty on this dodge.
  - **Pool.** Agility 3 minus 1 makes 2 base dice, plus 2 Gear Dice and 1 Stress Die.
  - **First roll.** The base dice show 6 and 2, the Gear Dice 6 and 3, and the Stress Die 4: 2 successes.
  - **The Push.** No Stress Die shows 1, so she Pushes and her Stress rises to 2. The base die showing 2 comes up 6, and her two Stress Dice show 5 and 3.
  - **Result.** Her 3 successes cancel the Grab's 3, leaving no Net Successes, so it whiffs against her. No Stress Die shows 1, so there is no Stress Response, and no Gear Die shows 1, so nothing wears.
- **Next.** Grab becomes the previous behavior, and Oskar's and Renate's just-hurt flags clear. The GM rolls a new Next Behavior and records it face down: this card had no telegraph, so it stays hidden.
