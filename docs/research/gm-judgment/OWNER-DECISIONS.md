# GM judgment: owner decisions (2026-09-16)

Research: `ripple-audit.md` and `difficulty-mechanics.md` in this folder.

## Direction

The owner makes GM judgment valid. In their words: GM judgment "is what TTRPG is and what makes it flexible and creative and makes TTRPG special, which is having the GM help adjudicate things. We can trust the GM." This replaces Chapter 1 section 1.1 "The dice are final" rule 3 (no dice by judgment) and the no-discretion principle of ADR-0003.

The two research files disagreed on how the GM expresses difficulty (the ripple audit said needs first, the difficulty research said dice). The owner chose dice.

## Answers

| 1 | Where do GM rulings stop? (recommended: everywhere except the rolls the simulator measures in a Titan Engagement) | **Everywhere, including Titan Engagements.** The core assumption during an Engagement is Standard, but the GM may adjust difficulty ad hoc to represent other things (for example flying in the rain) without the rules having to list every possible detail. |
| 2 | Does the GM set difficulty with a dice ladder or with needs 1 to 3? | **Dice ladder.** |

## Recommendations from the research that the owner did not contradict

- Results stand for every roll. The GM never adjusts or fudges a result after the dice are rolled (both reports reject Coriolis p. 250).
- Success requirements (needs) come only from numbers in the fiction: Nape Depth, Toughness, Grit, Watch, Scarcity, a Titan's or Foe's successes. The GM never raises a need.
- You always keep at least 1 base die. Gear Dice and Stress Dice are never removed by the ladder (Coriolis p. 49, Alien p. 43).
- ADR-0003 is superseded by a new ADR. ADR-0014 targets are measured at Standard with no GM adjustments.
- The Parley against Military Police Troopers succeeds about 2% of the time even with a Push. Fix it regardless.

## Points for the decider to interpret

- **Answer 1 scope.** Rulings reach Titan Engagements for difficulty. Decide whether that includes Titan Dice pools, Behavior Table results, Attention, and the closed lethality tables, or only the soldiers' own rolls. The owner's example (flying in the rain) is a soldier roll.
- **Answer 3.** The Coriolis ladder is seven steps, +3 to -3 (Coriolis p. 49). Decide how the + steps interact with the Bonus Dice cap of 4.
- **Answer 6.** "Left to GM discretion" reads as no hard once-per-scene cap. Decide whether to keep the cap as guidance only.
