# Titans act from Behavior Tables, not ordinary turns

A Titan is modeled as a natural disaster rather than a combatant: it has no normal turn and acts from a Behavior Table. How a Titan dies is set by ADR-0007, and how the Squad works together against it by ADR-0010. We chose this over tactical turn-by-turn titan combat and over abstract single-roll resolution because it keeps the Titan World feel: read it, work around it, set it up, then kill.

## Considered Options

- Tactical combat where the Titan is a combatant with turns: rejected, it turns titans into big humans and loses the disaster feel.
- One team roll per Titan Engagement: rejected, too thin for the game's headline pillar.

## Amended

After the final design review (2026-09-14), removed wording that required breaking Body Parts before any kill and that gave each Squad member a fixed job. Both contradicted ADR-0010.

After decision batch 8 (2026-09-15, the playtest packet feedback round 1; ADR-0019): the table picks the behavior, and the entry then rolls its Attack Dice in the open. That is the parents' shape (a Xenomorph's signature attack and a Coriolis creature's both roll a fixed pool after a table picks them) and it changes nothing here: the Titan still has no turn, chooses nothing, never Pushes, and acts only from the Behavior Table, so it is still a disaster to be read and worked around, not a combatant. A whiffed card still resolves a behavior for the flags and the Next Behavior.

After decision batch 13 (2026-09-20, the owner's decisions on playtest feedback round 3, item 12; OQ-192, OQ-193): two changes to what the table does with a rolled entry, and neither gives a Titan a choice. First, **a Titan that cannot reach its fixation retargets**. When the Attention holder does not meet the rolled entry's Position requirement, the entry no longer falls to the table's fallback, which is Thrash in every table written but two. The Attention Ladder is evaluated again over only those soldiers who do meet the requirement, that soldier becomes the Titan's Attention holder, and the entry resolves against them; only when no soldier in the Titan Engagement meets it does the behavior fall back as before (`data/engagement/behavior-procedure.yaml`, `resolving_a_card`, `choose`; ADR-0010, as amended). The first session measured what the old reading did: with Attention on a soldier at Distant or Blind Spot a Medium Titan could not Bite or Grab at all, and two cards in three became Thrash, so the better the Squad played the more inert the monster became. Retargeting is what a predator does, it uses the ladder the game already has, so the choice stays deterministic and the GM still picks nothing (ADR-0024, limit 8), and no Behavior Table is re-authored for it. The fallback is retargeted in its turn, over the candidates who meet its own Position requirement, so the standard Large's Crush and the Sprinting Abnormal's Pitch Headlong are reachable rather than tested against the holder the rolled entry has already failed against (round 3 review 1, M1). The alternative, keeping the target and downgrading the entry, is kept in the file as the fallback if retargeting measures badly.

Second, **Frenzy**. Each Focus Titan holds a counter from 0 to 3, starting at 0 when it becomes a Focus Titan and rising by 1 at a round-end step, and its behavior roll is D6 plus Frenzy, with a total above the table's highest result reading as that result (`data/engagement/titan-format.yaml`, `frenzy`; `data/engagement/round.yaml`, `end_steps`, `frenzy`). Because every Behavior Table is already ordered terrorize to kill by result, this walks a Titan up its own table as a fight drags, which gives the fight a shape it did not have and gives the Squad a reason to commit rather than kite. The ordering is stated rather than left to reading: the Next Behavior is rolled in advance of the card that resolves it, so **the roll takes the Titan's Frenzy at the moment of the roll** and nothing re-reads it, which keeps limit 14 (a roll stands as it fell) and keeps a Titan's one hidden roll a single event. A Titan's first Next Behavior is therefore a plain D6, and the escalation reaches the table one card late. Neither change gives the Titan a turn, a choice, or a Push: it still acts only from its table, and it is still a disaster to be read and worked around. Both are measured in one retune with the Health change of ADR-0005 (ADR-0014, as amended).

Amended after round 3 review 1 (2026-09-21, C3; `docs/reviews/round-3-review-decisions.md`): the move-up rule's wrap from 6 to 1 stands at Frenzy 0 and does not apply at Frenzy 1 or more, where an illegal result at 6 turns back down the table. Without that, every face Frenzy carried past 6 wrapped to result 1 whenever the entry at 6 was the previous behavior or needed a Broken arm, so a Titan at Frenzy 3 grinned more often than one at Frenzy 0, which inverts the escalation this amendment exists to produce. Every Frenzy 0 share is unchanged, so Chapter 6's tables and the constraint they were authored to stand.

Amended after the round 3 retune (2026-09-21, R1; `docs/reviews/round-3-retune-decisions.md`): Frenzy rises at the end of every even-numbered round of the Titan Engagement, not every round; the cap of 3 stands. Decision batch 13 (13-10) named the rate and the cap as starting values the simulator moves, and the retune moved the rate: at one rise a round, Frenzy 1 sat on the round-3 card, the round the kill target names, so every Squad paid it and the Medium deaths band read 0.174 against 0.08; no rate or cap landed that band with retargeting on (the least Frenzy that keeps this shape read 0.089), so R2 re-set the band and R1 chose the rate on the rule's own purpose, which is to leave a Squad that kills on time alone and to eat a Squad that grinds. At the halved rate Frenzy 1 is on the round-4 card, Frenzy 2 on the round-6 card, and Frenzy 3 on the round-8 card, where the retreat clock fills.
