# Humans roll; Titans do not

People fight in a Skirmish, a scene of soldiers against Military Police, bandits, or a Garrison picket. A human attack is rolled: a soldier rolls a full pool, and a foe rolls its Attack dice as base dice and nothing else. The attack's successes are its Severity, and it lands unless the target's Reaction, Block with a Blade Set or Dodge with ODM Gear or the horse, scores that many successes; there are no opposed rolls (Chapter 1, section 1.1). Humans can be blocked, which Titans cannot (ADR-0015). Human harm is damage typed by the weapon (Cut, Pierce, or Crush), never a Critical Injury dealt directly (ADR-0005 stays Titan-only), so a Health pool is what makes a human fight survivable, and a landed musket ball is as dangerous as a Titan's kill entry. Foes act by a closed targeting rule and break by Grit, so the GM chooses no target and no retreat (ADR-0003). Bare hands never kill: Crush damage puts a foe out cold, and only Cut or Pierce kills, so killing a person is a choice made by drawing a blade or a gun. Recorded after the owner's playtest packet feedback (2026-09-15, decision batch 7, 7-17).

## Considered Options

- Opposed cancellation, as Alien RPG Evolved and Coriolis: The Great Dark resolve close combat: rejected, because Chapter 1 forbids opposed rolls and the needs-equals-successes shape reuses ADR-0015 and Chapter 1's Reaction rule unchanged.
- Human attacks that inflict Critical Injuries directly, as Titan attacks do: rejected, because a Health pool in front of the tables is what lets a Skirmish end in flight or surrender instead of a grave, and Cut and Pierce reach the tables through damage at 0 Health.
- GM-chosen targets and morale: rejected under ADR-0003 item 8.
- Human fights less lethal than Titan fights: rejected by the owner; a musket in 845 to 850 is the Uprising's register.

## Amended

After decision batch 8 (2026-09-15, the playtest packet feedback round 1; ADR-0019): the title and the first sentence are history. Titans roll too, under ADR-0019, and humans share its shape: the attacker's successes are the attack's Severity, each Block or Dodge success cancels one, the attack lands on 1 or more net successes, and damage is the weapon's base plus 1 per net success beyond the first, so a partial Reaction cuts damage (8-4). A Foe's Guard dice are its cancelling roll, rolled as base dice, never Pushed, and spending nothing; the ambush still denies the Reaction. The musket's damage is 4, so a landed ball at net 1 puts a Health 4 Rookie at 0, Down with the immediate Pierce Critical Injury; the first paragraph's "as dangerous as a Titan's kill entry" now means that. The first Considered Option is withdrawn: opposed cancellation is adopted, Chapter 1 section 1.1 no longer forbids it, and section 1.9 defines the procedure once for Titans and people (8-5). Everything else stands: typed damage and never a direct Critical Injury from a person, Health in front of the tables, foes by closed rules, blocking a person but never a Titan, and bare hands that never kill. Burn joins the human types through a Bandit's firebrand and a flare fired at a person (8-7). The file keeps its name and title so that batch 7's references stay readable.

After decision batch 8, item 8-30 (2026-09-15, OQ-155): "only Cut or Pierce kills" is withdrawn. Burn kills too: a Foe brought to 0 Health by Cut, Pierce, or Burn damage is dead, and Crush alone puts a Foe out cold. The principle stands as written in its own words: killing a person is a choice made by drawing a weapon, and bare hands are the exception because they are what a soldier uses when they mean not to kill; a firebrand to the face and a flare fired at a person are that choice made with fire, and Burn kills a soldier at 0 Health through a lethal Critical Injury, so fire that only stunned a Foe would be the one asymmetry between the sides' damage. The first-human-kill trigger reads the three types.
