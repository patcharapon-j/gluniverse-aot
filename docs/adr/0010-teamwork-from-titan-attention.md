# Teamwork comes from Titan Attention, not assigned roles

There are no assigned roles in a Titan Engagement. A Focus Titan fixes its Attention on one target, chosen each time it acts from its Attention Ladder. The standard ladder follows canon, where Titans go for people and hooks rather than pain: hooked into its body, nearest person in reach, just hurt it (until its next card), loudest or brightest, nearest. The Blind Spot is anchored to terrain, not hooked into the Titan, but making a Nape strike counts as hooking in, so a striker who falls short draws the Titan's turn.

The only hard restriction is that a soldier holding Attention cannot strike the Nape. A lone soldier gets past it with Break Attention, shifting Attention onto a decoy for the Titan's next card. Everything else pushes toward teamwork with soft levers: Help, Openings, Covering, swapping initiative cards, and Squad Tactics, with no flat penalty for acting alone.

Expeditions are the deliberate exception: the whole Squad rides at one assigned Formation Post that changes only at Waypoints, because a riding formation is a standing order rather than the chaos of a fight.

## Considered Options

- Roles assigned per scene, as in Coriolis: The Great Dark's delve roles: rejected, because hectic combat and sudden incapacitation mean soldiers must change jobs moment to moment.
- No structural restriction at all: rejected, because the most capable soldier would bait, disable, and kill alone.
- "Just hurt it" at the top of the ladder: rejected in the final design review as unfaithful to canon.
- Formation Posts held by individual soldiers: rejected in the final design review, because a hazard would hit one isolated soldier with no teamwork possible.

After decision batch 13 (2026-09-20, the owner's decisions on playtest feedback round 3, items 11 and 12; OQ-191, OQ-192): the Attention Ladder is now read for a second purpose, and Attention moves with it. When a Focus Titan's card finds that the Attention holder does not meet the rolled entry's Position requirement, the Ladder is evaluated again over only those of the Titan's candidates who do meet it, and the soldier that evaluation returns **holds the Titan's Attention from that moment** and is what the entry resolves against (`data/engagement/attention.yaml`, `evaluation`, `retargeting`; `changes`, `retarget`; ADR-0001, as amended). It is the Ladder's own evaluation and its own tie-breaks over a narrowed candidate set: no new rule of selection is added, the candidate rules are untouched, the choice stays deterministic, and the GM still picks nothing (ADR-0024, limit 8). Only when no candidate meets the requirement does Attention stay where it is and the behavior fall back. This makes one soldier's safe Position stop being everyone's safe Position, which is the cover-your-comrade tension this record exists to produce: a striker who stands where the Titan cannot hurt them no longer parks the whole fight. The rungs, the first rung that no shout can outbid, and the Nape strike's Attention restriction are all unchanged as rules, but what a failed striker draws is narrowed by this; see the round 3 review amendment below.

The same batch writes down, without changing them, three facts this record already implied and nobody could find. A soldier at **Blind Spot is anchored to terrain behind the Titan and is not on it**: it is a place in the world, not a place on the monster, which is why steam at a Regeneration fill misses them, why the Urban Terrain Trait can say they are on a roof, and why the Anchor Rating decides whether a Blind Spot exists at all. **Making a Nape strike counts as hooking into the Titan for the Ladder only**: the striker's Position does not change, they are still at Blind Spot, and because the entry needs a soldier who does not hold the struck Titan's Attention, a striker who draws it onto themselves cannot strike again until it moves, which is this record's engine working exactly as designed. And **an action never changes a Position**: only the soldier's own move does, plus the closed list in `data/engagement/positions.yaml` (`changes_to_position`). Attention is not a Position, so a retarget moves nobody.

After round 3 review 1 (2026-09-21, C6; `docs/reviews/round-3-review-decisions.md`): the batch 13 paragraph's closing sentence overstated what was unchanged. Retargeting narrows the batch 4b promise that a Nape striker who falls short draws the Titan's next behavior on every ladder. The striker still draws the Titan's **Attention** on every ladder, since every ladder's first rung is hooked into its body and the struck-first step keeps them, and they draw its next behavior **when that behavior's Position requirement includes Blind Spot**. When it does not, the Titan retargets down the ladder to a comrade it can reach, its Attention moves to that comrade, and the striker, no longer holding Attention, may strike again on their next turn. On the standard Medium, whose result 1 and result 4 entries alone reach Blind Spot, the striker takes the behavior 2 rolls in 6 at Frenzy 0 and 1 in 6 at Frenzy 1 to 3. The price of a failed strike is therefore paid by whoever is in the Titan's hands' reach, which is the cover-your-comrade tension this record exists to produce, and the rerun reports the share of failed strikes whose striker takes the next resolved behavior and the striker's second-strike rate against the lone-strike band (ADR-0014). If that band breaks upward, the held alternative is that a retarget never moves Attention off a soldier holding the hooked-by-strike flag and the entry falls back instead; it is held beside downgrading (decision batch 13, 13-9) and is not taken now.
