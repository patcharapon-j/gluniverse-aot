Wings of Freedom · One-Shot · Year 850, five days after Trost

Before the Gate Closes

A three-act introduction for one GM and three to five players

Survey Corps — Eastern Command — Karanes

Relay Station 18 has missed two flare reports. You are to ride out, confirm its status, recover its route records, and be back inside Karanes before the gate closes at sunset. Command has called it a courier run.

It is not a courier run. The station was hit around midday. Four soldiers tried to walk west to an abandoned hamlet and only two got there. One is still inside the station, bleeding out on a cot. And something is working the ground between the two places — something fast, that does not stop, and does not behave.

This one-shot exists to do three things: put the table inside the mood of the Walls era, teach the Titan Engagement in play rather than in a lecture, and end with everyone exhausted and alive — or mostly alive — and wanting another session.

At a glance

Table

1 GM, 3–5 players. Characters already made through the Lifepath — fresh graduates of the 104th, Eastern Division.

Length

3½ to 4 hours, including a thirty-minute rules walk-through at the start.

Teaches

The dice pool, Push and Cover, Stress and Fear, and one full Titan Engagement per act — the round, Positions, the Flight, the Attention Ladder, the Nape, and the Grab.

Contains

Every rule, table, stat block, boxed text and NPC you need. Nothing else has to be open on the table.

Part Zero

Before You Play

Thirty minutes of setup buys you three hours of good play. Do not skip the dice colours.

What you need at the table

  • Six-sided dice in four colours. One colour for base dice, one for Gear Dice, one for Stress Dice, and one the players never touch for the Titan Dice the GM rolls. This is not decoration. A Stress Die showing 1 does something no other die does, and a player has to see it at a glance.
  • Twenty cards numbered 1 to 20 for the initiative deal, or a D20 — re-roll any number already dealt that round.
  • Tokens for Openings, for Momentum, and for the flags a Titan is holding against each soldier.
  • The GM’s tracker — one line for the Engagement, one row per Titan. There is a blank one in Part Six.
  • A printed copy of Part Five for the GM and the player card for everyone else.
  • Character sheets already filled in. This one-shot assumes every player walked their soldier through the Lifepath before the session — Origin, why they enlisted, three Training Years, the Graduation Exam, a Specialty, a Drive.

If somebody turns up without a soldier. Give them a Template Build: take a Specialty’s template array, choose an Origin, a Drive, and their Talents. It takes ten minutes and produces a legal, playable soldier. Do not run this one-shot with a blank sheet at the table — a soldier with no Drive and no Origin has nothing to be afraid of losing, and this scenario runs on exactly that.

The Squad, their gear, their tactics

They graduated eleven days ago. They were posted east, to Karanes, while the rest of their year went to Trost. Whatever they have heard about Trost, they heard it second-hand. None of them has ever held a Position in a Titan Engagement, and every one of them will make a first Titan Engagement Fear Roll the first time it happens. Let that land.

Squad Tactics — pick two, together, before Act I

A Squad holds two Squad Tactics. Each can be used once per Titan Engagement, when its condition is met, and is declared by the player the tactic names. The GM never uses one and never decides whether its condition is met beyond its written text.

Mood, tone, and the table

The reference is the first season: ordinary soldiers, impossible odds, and the long silence of open ground. Three rules of thumb carry most of it.

Titans have weight, not intent

Describe mass, wrongness, and the delay between what a Titan does and what it seems to mean. Never say it wants, it decides, or it is waiting for. The Behavior Table has already decided; your job is to describe the body doing it. “Its head comes round a half-second after its shoulders” is frightening. “It’s stalking you” is not.

Read the Ladder out loud

The Attention Ladder is public. Work down it aloud, rung by rung, every single time: “Anything hooked into its body? No. Nearest person In Reach — Mila. Anyone just hurt it? Kade did. So it turns to Kade.” The dread in this game comes from watching the machinery choose, and being able to see, one rung early, that it is about to choose you.

Announce the name flatly

When a card resolves a behavior you say its name, its tier, and who it is against, then you roll the Titan Dice in the open. No build-up. “Grab. Kill tier. Against Voss.” Six black dice hitting the table say more than a paragraph would. Describe the fiction after the dodge, when you know what happened.

Lines, veils, and the exit

This scenario contains being eaten alive, crush injuries described in clinical detail, a person bleeding to death while the players decide what to do about it, and the death of a sympathetic NPC. Say so before you start. Ask the table for anything they want left off the page, and ask whether anyone wants the injury descriptions kept short and mechanical rather than vivid — both settings work, and the tables read the same either way.

Anyone may call for a scene to be rewound or cut at any point, no reason required. Nothing in this one-shot is worth a bad evening.

What the GM decides — and what the GM never decides

This system draws that line unusually hard, and holding it is what makes the Titans feel like weather rather than like a person playing against the table. Put this list where you can see it.

You decide

  • What the world is: who is there, what it looks like, what happens when nobody is rolling.
  • The Anchor Rating of a fight, as framing, before anything is rolled.
  • One step of Circumstances per roll, named with the stakes and before Bonus Dice.
  • Whether an act needs a roll at all — and if it does, the stakes, chosen from the closed menu.
  • When a scene is over and the next one starts.

You never

  • Pick who holds a Titan’s Attention. The Ladder picks.
  • Re-roll, adjust, or ignore a Titan Die, a table result, or a Next Behavior. Not once.
  • Choose a Titan’s behavior, its target, or which fallback it takes.
  • End a Titan Engagement. Only the two ending tests do.
  • Grant, spend, or take a point of Momentum, or wreck an Anchor.
  • Pick an Injury Location, a side, an Injury Type, or a row on any harm table.
  • Soften or swap a hazard row you rolled in the open.
  • Change a roll’s entry, Circumstances, or stakes once the dice have left your hand.

Why this matters tonight. Your players are new. The first time the Ladder turns the Titan onto the one soldier who was doing everything right, they will look at you. Point at the ladder. It was never yours. That single moment is the whole pitch of the system, and you only get it if you have been reading the rungs aloud since round one.

Three rules that matter more than the track list

  • Silence is a cue. Two of the entries above are an instruction to turn the music off. Both are the scariest moments in the scenario. Do not fill them.
  • Never change the music mid-fight. A track change tells the table that the beat has ended. If you swap songs while a Titan is still on the board, the fight stops feeling dangerous, whatever the dice are doing.
  • Start on the words, not after them. Every cue is written to begin as you start reading, so the first line of boxed text lands on the first bar. Starting a track and then drawing breath wastes the best three seconds it has.

If you would rather not cue by hand

  • Queue only the six bold rows — the two fights, the two silences, the retreat and the ending — and leave everything else on one long ambient loop. You lose very little.
  • Put the player card and the cue sheet on the same side of the table as your phone. A GM reaching across the map to skip a track is a GM who has stopped narrating.
  • If a track ends and you are mid-sentence, let it end. Dead air in the middle of a Titan Engagement is free tension.

Part One

The Rules You Need

Everything this scenario touches, and nothing it does not. Teach sections 1 to 5 up front in ten minutes; teach the rest at the moment it first happens.

1 · The roll

Build a pool, roll it, count the sixes. Every 6 is a success. A roll needs 1 success unless its rule names a number.

Building the pool, in order
StepDiceNotes
1. Attributebase — 2 to 5The one attribute the action’s Catalog entry names. 6 only if it is the key attribute of your Specialty. Exactly one attribute per roll.
2. Talentbase — 0 to 3One Talent that adds dice and names this entry, if its condition is met. Talents that bend a rule instead of adding dice all stack.
3. Bonus Dicebase — cap 4Only from the listed sources, declared before the dice. Over the cap, you choose; what you leave out is not spent.
4. Penalties− base onlyApplied after the cap. They add up. Never below 1 base die. Never removes Gear or Stress Dice.
5. Gear Dicegear — 1 to 3The current rating of one item the entry allows. At current rating 0 the item counts as not had.
6. Stress DicestressYour current Stress, every attribute roll. A Stress Die showing 1 sets off a Stress Response.

Finishing a roll — four steps, always in this order

  1. Count every 6. The number needed never moves. The GM cannot raise or lower it.
  2. Resolve any Stress Response, at your Stress after the Push. One per roll at most.
  3. Apply the effect, or the one staked cost.
  4. Apply wear: 1 point if the roll was Pushed and any Gear Die shows 1.

Each success is used once — except a Reaction’s, which keeps cancelling for the rest of the round against that same enemy.

Titan Dice are different. The GM’s Titan Dice succeed on 5 or 6. They never Push, never take Bonus Dice, Help, Gear Dice, Stress Dice, or Circumstances, and are never re-rolled or adjusted. Roll them in the open, in their own colour, every time.

One step, named by the GM with the stakes and before Bonus Dice are declared, for the situation the roll is made in. Rain, dark and a snapped strap are one judgement, not three steps.

  • It is fixed once named. No Talent, Push or Cover changes it, and nothing changes it after the dice are rolled.

3 · Push and Cover

  1. Declare the Push after seeing the result, before anything is applied.
  2. Gain 1 Stress and add 1 Stress Die to the pool — unless a comrade Covers.
  3. Re-roll: every base die that is not a 6; every Gear Die showing 2 to 5; every Stress Die showing 2 to 5. A Gear Die showing 1 is locked and never re-rolled. Sixes stay.
  4. Count again and finish the roll.
  5. Wear: if any Gear Die shows 1 when the roll is final, the item wears 1 point — one point however many 1s show. Any wear ruins a Blade Set.

You cannot Push when

  • any Stress Die showed a 1, on the roll or after an earlier Push of it;
  • the roll’s own rule forbids it — the Death Roll does;
  • the roll is not an attribute roll: Gas Rolls, fall rolls, Fear Rolls, Stress Responses, hazard rolls, and every roll on a table;
  • you have already Pushed it once — unless a Talent allows more;
  • you are Down.

Covering

A player character who is not Down, is not the Pusher, and holds the same Position or one step away may take the Stress instead. Declared after the Push, before any re-roll. One Coverer, and they may refuse.

The Coverer gains 1 Stress; the Pusher gains none and adds no Stress Die. Everything else stands — a 1 on a Stress Die is still the Pusher’s Stress Response, and their gear still wears. Covering is not an action and costs nothing else. A Squadmate never Covers and never Pushes.

The ODM tax. Pushing any roll whose gear item is your ODM Gear makes that round’s Gas Roll three dice instead of two — however many of the round’s rolls you Pushed with it.

4 · Stress, Stress Responses, and Fear

Stress goes up

  • +1 you Push and are not Covered
  • +1 you Cover a comrade’s Push
  • +1 you fail a called roll whose stakes were Stress
  • as named by a table result or a Talent

Stress goes down

  • −1 a Titan Engagement ends, for any reason, including a retreat
  • −1 a Nape strike kills a Titan — for everyone still in the fight
  • −1 a Skirmish ends
  • −1 a successful camp roll at a night camp with food

The two Engagement reliefs stack: a kill that ends the fight gives −2.

Minimum Stress is your number of Scars, and nothing lowers you below it. The dice never cause a Fear Roll.

6 · The round

  1. Wings. Assign each living Squadmate to one player character’s Wing, or to none. Once assigned they stand, and change only if a soldier died, left, went Down or Grabbed, or a Focus Titan entered or died since the last wings step.
  2. Deal. One card to each living player character, one to each Squadmate not on a Wing, and as many cards as its Tempo to each living Focus Titan. Down, Grabbed, carried, and departed soldiers are all still dealt a card — their turns still happen.
  3. Swap. Two soldiers who hold the same Position or one step apart, neither Down nor Grabbed, may exchange cards. Each soldier swaps at most once a round. A Titan’s card is never swapped.
  4. Play. Cards come up lowest first. On a soldier’s card they take their turn, and then their Wing Squadmate takes its turn. On a Titan’s card, resolve the card.
  5. End steps, in order: Gas Rolls → fill every Regeneration clock by 1 → fill every Background clock by 1 and resolve full ones, then fill the retreat clock → anyone who made no ODM move loses all their Momentum → the round ends and everything unspent is lost.

A turn

  • One move and one action, in either order, never split. The move happens at one point in the turn, wholly before or wholly after the action.
  • A Reaction spends a whole turn — your earliest turn with both parts still unspent. A turn spent in advance still comes up; it simply arrives already spent.
  • Once per round, never banked. What you decline is gone, except that you can still Help and still React.
  • Not actions: Reactions, Pushing, Covering, declaring Bonus Dice, spending Openings, spending Momentum, swapping cards, using a Squad Tactic, Mount or Dismount, Swap Blade Set (spends your move).

Attack and Reaction — one procedure for every attack in the game

  1. The attack rolls first and finishes. Its successes are the Severity — the same number for every target. A Titan never Pushes.
  2. Each target decides, knowing the Severity. The reacting side Pushes last.
  3. Each Reaction success cancels one. What is left is that target’s Net Successes. The attack lands on 1 or more.
  4. No Reaction cancels nothing, so every success is net. An attack that rolled 0 whiffs against everyone, including anyone who could not have reacted.

One Reaction per enemy per round. Its successes keep cancelling against every later attack that enemy makes on you this round — they are never used up. Titans cannot be Blocked. A dodge is the only answer.

7 · Positions, the Flight, Momentum, Anchors

The four Positions — held separately against each Focus Titan
PositionMeans
DistantOut of a standing Titan’s reach.
In ReachWithin reach of its hands — on the ground or on anchors beside it.
On BodyHooked into it or standing on it, anywhere but the Nape.
Blind SpotOut of its sight with the Nape in reach, anchored to terrain, not to the Titan. The only Position a Nape strike can be made from.
  • One Position per Focus Titan and per corpse. Holding one is the test for being in the fight at all.
  • A move changes one named Titan’s Position by one step — and a move is the only way you change your own.
  • Close to one, loose from the other. Coming to On Body or Blind Spot on one Titan makes any other Titan’s Position In Reach.
  • A fall from the body lands you In Reach. From further out, your Position holds.
  • Letting go is the only way off a Titan without ODM Gear: use your move, and fall.

Sparse Act I

Scattered trees, a ruined farmstead, a supply wagon. Anchors 1.

  • Distant ↔ In Reach — on foot, mounted, or ODM
  • In Reach ↔ On Body — ODM only
  • On Body ↔ Blind Spot — ODM only
  • No route from In Reach straight to Blind Spot. You reach the Nape by way of the body.

Terrain Trait: the first Anchor wrecked in the fight is not lost. One good tree survives.

Urban Act III

Streets and rooftops. Anchors 3.

  • Distant ↔ In Reach — on foot or ODM. Not mounted.
  • In Reach ↔ On Body — ODM only
  • On Body ↔ Blind Spot — ODM only
  • In Reach ↔ Blind Spot — ODM only

Terrain Trait: a soldier at Blind Spot is anchored to a roof and is not airborne, so a Jam does not drop them.

The Flight — every ODM move in a fight

  • Roll Fly (Agility, Gear Dice from your own ODM Gear). The roll needs nothing: the step happens whatever the dice say. The roll decides what the move was worth.
  • Each success is 1 Momentum, up to the Anchors left.
  • No successes and you come in loud: you set the loudest flag on the Titan the move named, from any Position — Distant included.
  • Not Flights: letting go, a step on foot, a mounted step, being carried, a Fear Roll’s forced step. A move made under a retreat is one.

Momentum — spend 1 for

  • Carry — one more Position step on this move, along steps your kind of move could make. Spendable more than once.
  • Bite — +1 die on a strike or Break Attention this turn against the Titan the Flight named.
  • Brace — +1 die on your next dodge this round.
  • Quiet — you set no flag this turn, including the loudest flag a bad Flight would set.
  • Clean Line — no Gas Roll for you this round.

Spent in your own turn, any amount at once. Carry is spent as part of the move; everything else is declared before the roll it names. You lose all of it at the end of any round in which you made no ODM move.

Anchors — the field’s own resource

  • Set by the Anchor Rating. Never restored.
  • They are every soldier’s Momentum cap. When Anchors fall, everybody above the new cap loses the excess at once.
  • A behavior with a wreck effect destroys 1, landed or whiffed. A falling Titan destroys 1 where it lands.
  • At 0 Anchors nobody holds Momentum — but Flights still fly and no step row changes. A flattened field never closes the route to the Nape.

8 · The Titan’s card, step by step

Work left to right across the Titan’s tracker row. Never skip a step, never reorder them.

  1. Dead? If the Titan has died, its card does nothing.
  2. Holding? If it holds a Grabbed soldier, the card resolves nothing. No Ladder, the Next Behavior is kept, the flags stand. Stop.
  3. Decoy? If a decoy holds its Attention, the card resolves nothing and the hold loses a card. The Next Behavior becomes the previous behavior and you roll a new one. If the hold has no cards left it ends — evaluate the Ladder. The flags stand either way. Stop.
  4. Attention. Evaluate the Attention Ladder, aloud. If nobody holds it, the card resolves nothing and the Next Behavior is kept. Stop.
  5. Choose. Take the Next Behavior. If the Titan does not meet its Body Parts, it is Thrash. Otherwise, if the Attention holder does not meet its Position requirement, take its fallback — and if that fallback is Thrash, is the previous behavior, or fails either test, it is Thrash. Thrash always works, and is the only entry that can follow itself.
  6. Roll and announce. Say the name, the tier, and the targets. Roll the entry’s Attack Dice in the open as Titan Dice — 5s and 6s are successes. That is the card’s Severity, the same for every target. An entry with no Attack Dice rolls nothing and harms nobody.
  7. Reactions. If the roll scored 0, the card whiffs against everyone and no Reaction is made. Otherwise each target dodges, or cancels with a dodge already made against this Titan this round. Net Successes of 1 or more mean it lands.
  8. Effects. For each target it landed on, apply the effects in order, target by target in card order. Read the Net Successes only where an effect names them. telegraph and wreck apply whether it landed or whiffed.
  9. Next. The card resolved a behavior, landed or whiffed. It becomes the previous behavior, the decoys-in-a-row count returns to 0, every flag for this Titan clears, and you roll a new Next Behavior in secret. If the behavior had telegraph, reveal the new Next Behavior to everyone.

Rolling the Next Behavior

Roll D6 in secret and find the entry whose results include it. That entry can be rolled if it is not the previous behavior and the Titan has the Body Parts it lists. If it cannot, take the entry at the next higher result — after 6 comes 1 — skipping entries already checked, and test that one the same way. If no entry can be rolled, the Next Behavior is Thrash. Rolling never checks Positions or Attention; those are only checked when a card comes up.

This is why a Titan gets more dangerous as you break it: the entry sitting just above a Broken-limb entry starts inheriting its share of the die.

10 · Cutting a Titan down

Titans have no Health. They have Body Parts that break, a Nape that kills them, and a Regeneration clock that undoes your work.

Body Part strike action · Strength + Blade Set

  • Reach: eyes from On Body or Blind Spot; arms and legs from In Reach or On Body. From On Body or Blind Spot you need working ODM Gear, unless the Titan is grounded.
  • Each success adds 1 to that part’s count. At its Toughness the part moves one state toward Broken — Intact, Wounded, Broken — and the count resets to 0. Successes past Broken each become an Opening.
  • A strike with at least 1 success sets the just-hurt flag.

A grounded Titan — the single biggest swing in the game

  • Nape strikes against it gain 2 Bonus Dice.
  • Nape and Body Part strikes need no working ODM Gear.
  • Every Body Part can be struck from In Reach, On Body, or Blind Spot — except an arm holding a Grabbed soldier.
  • The steps between In Reach, On Body and Blind Spot can all be made on foot.
  • It ends when no leg is Broken — which Regeneration will eventually do.

Nape strike action · Strength + Blade Set

  • Requires: Blind Spot; not holding its Attention; working ODM Gear unless it is grounded; not Grabbed; no retreat under way.
  • Needs its Nape Depth in this one roll. Reach it and the Titan dies. Fall short and each success becomes 1 Opening.
  • It sets the hooked-by-strike flag whatever happens — so a striker who falls short draws the Titan’s next behavior. That is the cost of trying.
  • Successes never carry over to a later strike except as Openings.

Openings are tokens on the Titan, each recording who made it. Any other soldier may spend them, 1 Bonus Die each, on a Nape strike. You can never spend your own. This is the engine of the whole game: you cut, you fall short, and you hand your comrade the kill.

Regeneration

Fill 1 segment at the end of each round. When it fills: erase every Opening; set every Body Part’s count to 0; move the most damaged part one state toward Intact; if that moved a part, everyone On Body rolls on the steam table; empty the clock. If that part was its last Broken leg, it stands.

The falling body

When a Focus Titan dies, or a living one becomes grounded, its body comes down. It destroys 1 Anchor where it lands. Everyone On Body or at Blind Spot who is not airborne makes a Leap Clear roll — Agility, Gear Dice from ODM Gear or from the horse while mounted, needing 1 success. It is not an action and not a Reaction; it spends nothing.

  • Success: you clear, and hold In Reach.
  • Failure: you are Pinned, with a Crush Critical Injury at a rolled Injury Location that cannot be lethal. An arm or leg result pins that limb; a torso or head result pins your whole body.
  • Airborne: you swing clear with no roll and keep your Position. Down: no roll, and you are Pinned.

A Pinned soldier cannot move or be moved, cannot Cover or React, and a body-pinned one has no action at all. Under a corpse they take a Burn Critical Injury at the start of each of their turns. They are freed by a Heave count reaching the body’s Heave rating, by the pinning Body Part being Broken, or by a living Titan standing up. The end of a Titan Engagement frees nobody — and the fight does not end while anyone is still Pinned.

11 · The Grab

The Grab is the centre of gravity of this one-shot. Learn it before you sit down.

When a Grab lands — on 1 or more Net Successes

  1. Hold. The target is Grabbed. They stop being airborne; a mounted target is dismounted with no fall; a carried target stops being carried; anyone they were carrying is set down. The holding arm is the first arm on the stat block that is not Broken, and while it holds them its Toughness is 1 and its count is 0.
  2. Crush. They gain a torso Critical Injury, Injury Type Crush, which cannot be lethal. It crosses off a Health box like any other — so a soldier whose last box it takes is Down in the Titan’s hand. The Grab takes no Net Success rider.
  3. Attention. The Grabbed soldier holds the Titan’s Attention for as long as it holds them.
  4. Witnesses. Every witness makes a comrade-grabbed Fear Roll.

While Grabbed

  • You hold On Body relative to the holding Titan.
  • Your move changes nothing. Your only action is Break Free.
  • You cannot Help, Cover, or React. You can still Push.
  • Going Down does not free you.

While it holds someone

  • Every card of the holding Titan resolves nothing. No Ladder, no behavior, the Next Behavior kept, the flags standing.
  • It cannot Grab anyone else.
  • Its Regeneration clock still fills.

This is the trap: the Titan goes quiet, and the quiet is the clock running out on your comrade.

The countdown

It counts the Grabbed soldier’s own turns that come up after the Grab lands, while they are still Grabbed. A turn spent in advance counts. A turn taken while Down counts. The action of the first counted turn is spent by the Grab, whatever else already spent it.

  • End of the first counted turn: they are lifted.
  • End of the second counted turn: the Titan devours them. They die. Every witness makes a comrade-dies Fear Roll.

Neither step rolls dice, and no Reaction answers either. They simply happen.

12 · Harm, Down, and death

Health

Health = (Strength + Agility) ÷ 2, rounded up. A row of boxes. Current Health = Health − boxes crossed off by untreated Critical Injuries − Health lost to damage.

Resolve = (Instinct + Empathy) ÷ 2 rounded up, +1 per Scar, −1 per point of Grief. It counts against every Stress Response and Fear Roll total.

Two kinds of harm

Damage marks boxes. Reaching 0 current Health makes you Down and gives one Critical Injury of the damage’s type. Damage taken at 0 gives another Critical Injury.

A Critical Injury crosses off a box while untreated and never adds to damage.

A Titan attack is always a Critical Injury and never damage — whatever your Health. Add +1 to its 2D6 for each Net Success beyond the first.

Steam, the falling body, corpse heat and a fall are not Titan attacks. They are damage, and they take no rider.

Gaining a Critical Injury

  1. Location. Use the one the rule names, or roll D6: 1 left arm · 2 right arm · 3 left leg · 4 right leg · 5 torso · 6 head. An arm or leg named without a side rolls D6: odd left, even right.
  2. Roll 2D6, +2 for each Critical Injury already at that location and side, +1 per Net Success beyond the first if it is a Titan attack.
  3. Cap if the rule says it cannot be lethal: use that table’s non-lethal cap row instead.
  4. Record the row, the side, the type, the time limit, the healing days. It crosses off a box. Apply its effects at once.

Full tables in Part Five.

Down

You are Down at 0 current Health, or while you hold an untreated Critical Injury whose row says so.

Down forbids every action including Help, Pushing, Covering, every Reaction, every attribute roll but the Death Roll — so no Stress Dice and no Stress Responses — and every Fear Roll.

Your turns still happen, with a move only, and they still count for time limits and Grab countdowns. Your move can only take you from In Reach to Distant, on foot. You can be treated, Rallied, lifted, and you make Death Rolls.

Treat Injury Wits + medical kit

Declare one use before rolling. Needs 1 success. In a fight it is an action, and the patient must hold your Position.

  • Treat — one named untreated Critical Injury becomes treated. It gives back the box it crossed off, and if it was lethal it is stabilized. Treating never removes its effects.
  • Revive — a Down soldier at 0 current Health with damage marks: restore 1 Health lost per success.
  • Treating yourself takes a −2 dice penalty, and you can never revive yourself.
  • Surviving a lethal Critical Injury — being stabilized alive — gives a Scar.

When a Titan Engagement ends — work down this list

  1. Turns spent in advance are cancelled. Bans on Reactions end. Pending Fear results are cancelled.
  2. Everyone who held a Position loses 1 Stress.
  3. Every lasting Stress Response gained in the fight ends.
  4. Every turn limit becomes an engagement limit.
  5. Aftermath rolls: each patient with an untreated lethal engagement injury gets at most one, from a treater the players choose who was at their Position or one step away. No Help, no Cover.
  6. Death Rolls for every lethal engagement injury still untreated. A death here causes no Fear Roll.
  7. Care window: everyone who held a Position may make one Treat Injury roll. Field Repair is rolled here too.
  8. Grief for every death during the fight. Then retirement at five Scars, then gear shared out, then promotion.

13 · Gas, wear, and falls

The Gas Roll

At the end of any round you used ODM Gear, roll 2 dice — 3 if you Pushed any roll that round whose gear item was your harness. Count the 1s and lower your Gas Rating by that many. Not an attribute roll: no Push, no Help, no Stress Dice, nothing.

Full Gas Rating is 3. At 0 you are dry: the harness counts as not had, you cannot make an ODM move, and you cannot make a Nape strike or a strike from On Body or Blind Spot. Dry does not drop you — an airborne soldier stays airborne.

Change Canister is an action: fit a carried spare and its Gas Rating becomes yours.

Wear and the Jam

Every rated item’s Gear Dice are its current rating. One point of wear lowers it by 1.

ODM Gear at 0 Jams: counts as not had, no ODM moves, no Nape strikes — and if you are airborne, you fall once the roll that Jammed it is finished. Field Repair (Wits + tool kit, needs 1) fixes it.

Any wear ruins a Blade Set. It is discarded at once and your handles are empty. Swap Blade Set spends your move, once per turn — so a ruined set costs you that turn’s Flight and the Momentum it would have given.

Dry is not Jammed. Dry, the harness is whole and one canister fixes it. Jammed, only a repair or a new harness will do, and it drops you out of the air.

14 · Called rolls and Leg rolls

The called roll — the GM’s one lever

Called for an act no rule covers, whose outcome is in doubt, and where failure would cost something. All three, or you do not call for one. Before the pool is built, say three things out loud:

  1. The entry — the Action Catalog entry closest to the act, or a bare attribute if none fits. A called roll on a bare attribute has no Gear Dice; a tool that helps counts toward the Circumstances instead.
  2. The Circumstances — one step, or Standard.
  3. The stakes — what failing costs, from the menu below and nothing else.

It needs 1 success. It can be Pushed, Covered, and Helped. Once the dice are rolled, nothing changes. If failure would change nothing, or the soldier has the time, tools and training to do it unpressed — it just happens. If it is impossible, say so before anyone rolls.

The passive roll. When something is there that a soldier would never know to look for, you roll their Spot or Size Up out of sight, with their dice and whatever the circumstances give.   1. The Squad rides at the Leg’s Formation Post.
  2. The Leg roll. One soldier, the Lead, rolls the Post’s entry, needing 1. Up to 3 comrades may Help, spending nothing, once each per Leg. Success: spend 1 ration, and −1 to the hazard for each success beyond the first. Failure: spend 2 rations, and +1 to the hazard.
  3. You always arrive. The Squad always reaches the Waypoint. Riding is never the question; what happened on the way is.
  4. Roll the hazard in the open and resolve the row. Do not soften it and do not swap it.
  5. A Titan Engagement a row begins takes its Anchor Rating from the kind of the Waypoint the Leg leads to.

15 · One round, played out

Round 1 of the Act I fight, as it actually goes at a table. Read this once before you run and the procedure will hold together in your hands.

The table: Mila (Flier, Agility 4), Jonas (Slayer, Strength 4), Kade (Tactician, Instinct 4), and Corporal Voss as a Squadmate on Mila’s Wing. Everyone at Stress 0, Resolve 3, ODM Gear 3, Blade Set 1. The field: Sparse — Anchors 1, so everyone’s Momentum cap is 1. The Titan: one standard Small — Tempo 2, Nape Depth 3, every Body Part Toughness 1.

Wings and deal

Voss goes on Mila’s Wing, so he takes no card and acts right after her. The GM deals: Mila 4, Jonas 11, Kade 17, and two cards to the Titan, 7 and 14.

Swap step: Jonas and Kade both hold Distant — the same Position — so they could swap. They look at the 7 and decide not to.

Card 4 — Mila

She takes her action first, because a Read is worth two extra dice while she is still Distant. Read: Instinct 3 + 2 Bonus Dice (reading from Distant) + 0 Stress = 5 base dice. She rolls 6 2 4 1 3 — one success. She spends it on next behavior.

The GM turns the card face up. “Grab. Kill tier. Targets whoever holds its Attention. Needs them In Reach or On Body. Uses an arm. Six Attack Dice.” Everybody at the table goes quiet, which is the entire point of the Read.

Then her move: an ODM move from Distant to In Reach, naming the Titan. Every ODM move is a Flight. She rolls Fly — Agility 4 + Gear 3 + Stress 0 = 4 base and 3 gear dice — and gets 6 3 2 5 + 6 1 4: two successes. The step happens regardless; the successes would be 2 Momentum, but the cap is 1 Anchor, so she takes 1 and the other is simply gone. She is In Reach and airborne.

Voss’s turn — right after Mila’s

Move: a Flight, Distant to In Reach. He rolls no successes. He comes in loud — he sets the loudest flag on the Titan. Action: a Body Part strike on its left leg — Strength 4 + Blade Set 1. He rolls 6 2 3 4 + 5: one success. The left leg’s Toughness is 1, so the count reaches 1 and the leg moves Intact → Wounded, count back to 0. He sets the just-hurt flag.

A Squadmate never Pushes. Voss had the chance to make it two and could not take it, and the players notice.

Card 7 — the Titan’s first card

Dead? No. Holding? No. Decoy? No. Attention — the GM reads the Ladder aloud:

“Anything hooked into its body? Nobody is On Body, nobody has the hooked flag. No. Nearest person In Reach — Mila and Voss. That’s the rung. Anyone holding loudest? Voss, but he’s already in the set. Just hurt it? Voss did. So it keeps only Voss.”

Choose: the Next Behavior is Grab. Voss holds In Reach, which the entry allows. Both arms are unbroken. It happens.

Roll and announce: “Grab. Kill tier. Against Voss.” Six Titan Dice, in the open: 5 6 2 3 5 1 — fives and sixes count, so Severity 3.

Reactions: Voss dodges. Agility 3 + ODM Gear 3 = 6 dice. He rolls two 6s. Two cancelled, one Net Success. It lands.

Effects. Voss is Grabbed. He stops being airborne. The holding arm is the left arm, and while it holds him its Toughness drops to 1. He takes the crush: a torso Critical Injury, Crush, cannot be lethal — 2D6 gives 7, which is Deep Bruising, crossing off one of his four Health boxes and giving −1 die on Fly, dodge and Break Free. He holds the Titan’s Attention.

Witnesses. Mila, Jonas and Kade each make a comrade-grabbed Fear Roll, together, from one snapshot. Mila rolls D6 4 + Stress 0 − Resolve 3 = 1: Heart in Your Throat, +1 Stress. Jonas rolls 2 → −1: Steeled. Kade rolls 6 → 3: Clenched Trigger — his next action is spent, and he makes a Gas Roll at once: two dice, 1 5, Gas 3 → 2. That one is the row’s, not the round’s, and it is not ODM use, so it neither replaces nor adds to the Gas Roll he will still owe at the end step.

Next. The card resolved a behavior. Grab becomes the previous behavior, every flag on this Titan clears — Voss’s loudest and just-hurt both go — and the GM rolls a new Next Behavior in secret.

Card 11 — Jonas

The countdown is running: Voss is lifted at the end of his own next turn, devoured at the end of the one after. Jonas goes for the arm.

Move: a Flight, Distant to In Reach. Two successes → 1 Momentum (cap 1). Action: a Body Part strike on the holding arm — legal from In Reach, because Voss has not been lifted yet. Before rolling he spends his 1 Momentum on Bite: +1 die. Strength 4 + 1 Bonus + Blade Set 1. He rolls 6 4 2 3 5 + 3 — one success. The arm’s Toughness is 1 while it holds, so it goes Intact → Wounded. One more success on it, from anyone, Breaks it and frees Voss.

Part Two

The Scenario

Three acts, one afternoon, and a gate that shuts at sunset whether or not anyone is through it.

What is actually true

The players will learn most of this. You should know all of it before the first word.

  • Relay Station 18 is a four-soldier flare post on the hedgerow line, two hours east of Karanes and about as far outside Wall Rose as anyone is stationed. Its job is to relay flare reports from the deeper waystations and to keep the route records: marked corridors, Titan sightings by day, supply schedules, and the flare log. It has missed its last two reports.
  • Around midday it was hit. Three Titans came through the hedgerow line together, which is not supposed to happen, and the post had no chance of holding. Nobody at Karanes knows this yet.
  • The four personnel tried to evacuate west toward Brandt’s Hollow, an abandoned hamlet two kilometres on, because it has stone walls and a mill house with a cellar. Two got there: Tomas Riehl, whose leg is crushed, and Anka Brenner, a trainee field medic who has kept him alive since.
  • One did not leave. Linne Faber was hit covering the retreat and dragged herself back into the station. She has a torn artery in her thigh and perhaps a few more hours. She has been alone in there since midday, listening.
  • One is dead on the road, and the PCs will find pieces of his harness in a tree.
  • The route records are still in the flare log box in the station’s back room, under the map table. They are worth an afternoon of everyone’s lives because they mark a corridor east that Command needs open for the next expedition, and redrawing it costs another scouting run and another set of names.
  • A Sprinting Abnormal is working the ground between the station and the hamlet. It is not guarding anything. It is not clever. It runs things down, it does not stop when it should, and it turns toward noise from further away than it has any right to. It was one of the three that hit the station and it is the one that has not moved on.
  • Corporal Diedrich Voss believes this is a courier run. He knows he is wrong by the time he is inside the station, and he says nothing about it, because the Squad are eleven days out of the Training Corps and he thinks that is what a corporal is for.

The shape of the evening. Act I proves the Squad can fight a Titan. Act II proves that fighting one was never the hard part. Act III makes them choose, under a clock, between the mission, a man they like, and two people they have never met — and then makes them carry whatever they chose back through open ground.

The Sunset Clock

Sunset — 8

Draw it where everyone can see it and fill a segment in front of them. It is not a mood-lighting device: it is the one thing in this scenario that makes doing the right thing expensive, and it only works if the players can count it.

Fill one segment, and only for these:

  1. The Squad reaches Relay Station 18.
  2. Each failed Leg roll.
  3. Each called roll made at the station after the first two.
  4. Each Titan Engagement that ends — and one more if it ran past round 4.
  5. Holding a care window anywhere.
  6. Choosing to search Brandt’s Hollow rather than leave with what they have.

Nothing else fills it. Not a slow conversation, not a bad plan, not you wanting more tension. If the players are dawdling, tell them the light is going; do not tick the clock for it.

What filling it means

When the eighth segment fills, the gate at Karanes closes. Say so aloud the moment it happens, wherever they are. It does not end the scenario — it changes the ending, and every soldier at the table already knows what it means to be outside a wall after dark.

Typical play fills six to eight. If it is at 4 when they leave the station, they are doing well and should be allowed to feel that. If it is at 7, the run home is the best scene of the night.

  6. Announce name, tier, targets. Roll Attack Dice in the open. 5s and 6s. That is the Severity.
  7. 0 successes → whiffs against everyone, no Reactions. Otherwise each target dodges. Lands on 1+ Net.
  8. Effects in order, target by target in card order. telegraph and wreck apply even on a whiff.
  9. Next: previous behavior set, decoys-in-a-row to 0, all flags clear, roll a new Next Behavior.

Standard Ladder

  1. Hooked into its body — On Body, or holds hooked-by-strike
  2. Nearest person In Reach
  3. Just hurt it — holds just-hurt
  4. Loudest or brightest — holds loudest
  5. Nearest — On Body > In Reach > Blind Spot > Distant

Then: add everyone holding loudest (unless rung 1 was the top) → if rung 1, keep only hooked-by-strike → narrow down the lower rungs → previous holder → lowest card → nobody.

Flags last until the end of its next card that resolves a behavior. A card that resolves nothing leaves them all standing.

Sprinting Abnormal: hooked → loudest → the one it was already on (not at Distant) → nearest.

  • Death Roll: 1
Reach
  • Eyes: On Body, Blind Spot
  • Arm / leg: In Reach, On Body
  • Nape: Blind Spot only, and not while holding its Attention
  • Holding arm, before the lift: In Reach, On Body, Blind Spot
  • Holding arm, after the lift: On Body, Blind Spot

Working ODM Gear needed for
  • Every Nape strike
  • Body Part strikes from On Body or Blind Spot
  • Every ODM move

End steps, in order
  1. Gas Rolls (2 dice, 3 if Pushed with the harness)
  2. Regeneration +1 on every living Focus Titan
  3. Background clocks +1 and resolve; then retreat clock +1
  4. No ODM move this round → lose all Momentum
  5. Round ends; everything unspent is lost

Part Six

Handouts

One page for the players, one page for you, one order slip to put on the table face down.

The player card

Print one per player. Nothing on it is secret.

Your turn: one move, one action, either order

Every roll

Attribute + Talent + Bonus Dice (cap 4) − penalties + Gear Dice (one item) + Stress Dice. Count the 6s. You need 1 unless told otherwise.

Push, once

+1 Stress, +1 Stress Die, re-roll everything that is not a 6 (Gear Dice showing 1 are locked). You cannot Push if a Stress Die showed 1. Any Gear Die on 1 at the end wears the item — and any wear ruins a Blade Set.

Cover a comrade

Same Position or one step away, not Down, not the Pusher. You take the Stress; they add no Stress Die. Costs you nothing else.

Help a comrade

+1 die each, up to 3 helpers. In a fight it spends your action and needs the same Position or one step away.

Your move — the Flight

Every ODM move is a Flight. Roll Fly; the step happens whatever you get. Each success is 1 Momentum (capped by Anchors). No successes and you come in loud.

Spend Momentum

Carry one more step · Bite +1 die on a strike or Break Attention · Brace +1 die on your next dodge · Quiet set no flag · Clean Line no Gas Roll. Lost at end of round if you did not fly.

Hurting it

Body Part strike: Strength + blades, successes add toward Toughness. Break a leg and it is grounded — that is the big one. Nape strike: from Blind Spot, not holding its Attention, needs the Nape Depth in one roll. Falling short makes Openings for everyone else.

Moving it

Draw Attention (action, no roll) makes you loudest. Break Attention (Perception) plants a decoy — flare, riderless horse, thrown cloak, or your own Feint.

If it grabs you

You act only to Break Free (needs 2, −2 dice once lifted). Lifted at the end of your next turn. Eaten at the end of the one after. Your friends can cut the arm, Break Attention, or kill it.

Gas

End of any round you used the harness: roll 2 dice (3 if you Pushed with it), lose 1 Gas per 1 shown. At 0 you are dry: no ODM moves, no Nape strikes. Change Canister is an action.

When it hurts

A Titan attack is always a Critical Injury, never damage. At 0 current Health you are Down: no actions, no Push, no Help, no Reactions, no Fear Rolls — but your turns still happen and you can still be saved.

Your Drive

Once a session, while its trigger is met, shrug off an entire Fear Roll result. Say it out loud now so you remember you have it.

Notes on using generated art at the table

Backdrops carry a scene better than character art does, because a player’s own picture of their soldier is always better than a generated one. If you only make six images, make the six backdrops and skip the portraits entirely.

Do not generate an image of a Titan. Every table’s Titan is worse than any picture, and the moment you show one you have capped it. The one exception is a silhouette at distance for the Act III title card.
